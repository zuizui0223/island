# Chapter 1 court-style evidence ledger and theory synthesis — 2026-09-14

## The original question

> Why are island flowers dull?

The project no longer supports answering this as a single adaptive story. The data force a more general question:

> **When a geographic filter changes community composition, when does the visible trait syndrome reflect coordinated within-lineage adaptation, and when does it emerge from hierarchical lineage sorting?**

Islands provide the natural experiment-like gradient; floral/reproductive traits provide the phenotype; source pools and taxonomic decomposition reveal the assembly depth.

## Charge 1 — a universal floral island syndrome exists

### Evidence for the prosecution

- The classical expectation links greater isolation to reproductive assurance and reduced floral specialization/attraction.
- Some Palearctic results match that direction.

### Evidence against

- Direct multivariate tests show biogeographic response-vector heterogeneity rather than one common vector.
- Tropical component responses do not reduce to a weaker copy of the Palearctic pattern.
- The 8-component northern-midlatitude versus tropical response vectors are descriptively separated by about 95–116 degrees.

### Verdict

**Rejected as a universal rule.** The evidence supports context-dependent component reassembly rather than one obligatory syndrome.

## Charge 2 — the strongest Palearctic pattern is an artefact of missing trait data

### Defense evidence

V5 attacks state-dependent trait resolution rather than assuming MAR.

- The broad Palearctic plant-response vector survives the prospectively fixed finite MNAR grid in both evidence scopes.
- Some reproductive-assurance details and regional contrasts remain sensitivity-bounded rather than fully identified.
- Arbitrary MNAR is not ruled out.

### Verdict

**Broad Palearctic pattern retained; reproductive details bounded.**

## Charge 3 — the Palearctic pattern is generated by species-list incompleteness

### Defense evidence

V6 jointly varies distance-dependent list completeness and trait-state-dependent species recording without increasing information weight.

- 99/100 baseline-supported Palearctic accessibility surfaces survive the complete frozen grid.
- All 80/80 surfaces in the biologically motivated direction `remote islands less complete + focal generalized/accessibility species under-recorded` survive.
- The sole FDR break occurs without distance-dependent under-survey and does not reverse sign.

### Adverse evidence

- Tropical negative accessibility is substantially more sensitive: 40/75 baseline-supported surfaces break.
- The analysis does not protect against arbitrary taxon-dependent omission.

### Verdict

**The specified species-detection bias is a poor explanation for the positive Palearctic pattern but a real limitation for the tropical accessibility component.** Regional vector heterogeneity remains more robust than the tropical single-axis result.

## Charge 4 — the observed syndrome mainly reflects within-lineage adaptation

### Evidence against

The strongest Palearctic primary response undergoes a large taxonomic attenuation:

- family adjustment removes about 19.6–33.4% of vector magnitude;
- genus adjustment removes about 78.8–85.9% relative to the observed response;
- after family adjustment, genus composition removes about 70.6–79.1% of the remaining vector;
- formal broad support changes `observed 4/4 -> family 4/4 -> genus 0/4`.

### Claim boundary

This does not prove no within-lineage adaptation and does not identify why particular genera are sorted. Taxon-dependent observation bias is not fully excluded.

### Verdict

**The strongest broad Palearctic syndrome is principally expressed at the family-to-genus assembly transition, not as a robust beyond-genus primary residual.**

## Charge 5 — one common nonlinear isolation threshold generates the pattern

### Defense/evidence

- Naive nonlinear selection was rejected after spatial-block simulation exposed false breakpoint selection.
- Calibrated V2 showed that a step would be identifiable in 11/12 design cells.
- Observed opening then yielded 12/12 `monotonic_or_unresolved`, with zero promoted steps or reversals.

### Verdict

**No universal/common assemblage breakpoint is supported.** This does not rule out lineage-specific or local-system thresholds.

## Charge 6 — a pollinator-side mechanism is identified globally

### Favorable circumstantial evidence

- Pollination-associated floral architecture is biologically compatible with the observed floral response.
- H3 permits an upstream interaction filter to be absorbed into genus assembly.
- Local `izu-core` systems supply a mechanistically explicit route from interaction state to reproductive outcome and phenotype.

### Adverse independent evidence

1. **N1 channel heterogeneity**: frozen global isolation × channel test `p = 0.65516`; not promoted. The design is underpowered for modest heterogeneity, so this is non-identification rather than proof of equality.
2. **GloBI source breadth**: effort-matched sampled channel breadth promoted in 0/4 context × stratum cells.
3. **H5c independent pollination-mode specificity**: only one design cell qualified prospectively. In direct-only native-nonendemic Palearctic plants, `distance × biotic` = `+0.06495`, 95% CI `[-0.09030, 0.22020]`, p=`0.41221`; no specificity support.
4. **H5d distributed thresholds**: 0/8 design cells could distinguish distributed threshold generators from heterogeneous smooth clines under the frozen gate. False distributed-threshold selection under smooth clines was 0.19–0.255.

### Verdict

**A global pollinator-specific causal mechanism is not identified and should not be promoted.** Pollinator mechanisms remain local/prospective hypotheses rather than an explanatory label pasted onto H3.

## What remains standing after cross-examination

The strongest surviving case is:

1. geographic isolation is associated with reproducible floral/reproductive assemblage change;
2. the response is not a globally uniform syndrome;
3. trait components can decouple and their multivariate direction differs by biogeographic context;
4. the strongest Palearctic response is concentrated at a specific taxonomic assembly depth, especially the family-to-genus transition;
5. this result survives strong trait-missingness and specified species-detection challenges;
6. simple mechanisms — area, a common breakpoint, coarse channel heterogeneity, sampled interaction breadth, and independent biotic-vs-wind specificity — do not individually explain the pattern.

## The conceptual upgrade

The primary conceptual object should no longer be the **island floral syndrome** as a fixed trait list. It should be a **hierarchically assembled phenotypic syndrome**.

A community-level trait syndrome can arise from at least three processes:

`within-lineage response + lineage sorting + interaction/network reorganization`.

A cross-sectional macroecological pattern cannot be interpreted as adaptation until those levels are separated.

The Chapter 1 contribution is therefore not simply that island flowers vary with isolation. It is that the visible syndrome can be decomposed by:

- **component** — which traits move;
- **direction** — how they move;
- **context** — where the vector changes;
- **taxonomic depth** — where the response disappears under composition control;
- **response geometry** — cline versus identifiable nonlinearity;
- **falsification boundary** — which observation/process explanations survive explicit stress tests.

## General question emerging from the island case

> **How do geographic filters translate lineage sorting and biological interactions into emergent phenotypic syndromes across scales?**

A sharper inferential version is:

> **When does a macroecological trait syndrome represent repeated adaptation of lineages, and when is it an emergent consequence of which lineages can assemble?**

This question generalizes naturally beyond islands without abandoning the island origin of the project. Similar logic applies to urban, alpine, fragmented, arid or otherwise filtered communities whenever a repeated trait syndrome is inferred from cross-sectional assemblages.

## Chapter 1 / Chapter 2 scale bridge

### Chapter 1 — macroecological decomposition

Many islands and many lineages are mixed together. Chapter 1 identifies response direction, component decoupling, hierarchical depth and the absence of a common global breakpoint. It cannot identify the pollinator-side causal chain.

### Chapter 2 — local mechanistic resolution

A deeply resolved `Campanula` interaction system can measure interaction regime, effective service, reproduction and phenotype. It can distinguish smooth and threshold-like response channels in a way the global design cannot.

The two chapters therefore test different levels of the same problem:

> **macro-scale syndrome = what survives after averaging many lineage histories; local system = the response geometry and mechanism hidden inside that average.**

A local threshold and a smooth global assemblage gradient are logically compatible, but Chapter 1 cannot identify a distributed-threshold generator against heterogeneous smooth alternatives. That distinction is now empirically demonstrated rather than hand-waved.

## Publication-facing core claim

Recommended central claim:

> **Geographic isolation does not impose one floral island syndrome. It reorganizes floral and reproductive composition through biogeographically contingent, hierarchically structured assemblage filtering; the strongest Palearctic response is concentrated at the family-to-genus transition, while several simple adaptive and interaction-based explanations fail explicit falsification tests.**

General conceptual sentence for the Discussion:

> **Trait syndromes observed across environmental gradients need not be repeated organismal adaptations; they can emerge from hierarchical changes in community membership, and identifying the assembly depth of a syndrome is therefore a prerequisite for mechanistic interpretation.**

## What not to claim

- pollinator loss caused the Palearctic response;
- the H5c null proves pollinators do not matter;
- genus attenuation proves dispersal alone;
- tropical accessibility is as observation-robust as the Palearctic branch;
- a smooth global response proves local systems lack thresholds;
- endemicity establishes within-lineage evolution;
- taxonomic composition control alone separates colonization, persistence, diversification and adaptation.

## Highest-value next empirical move

Do **not** add another global proxy mechanism after H5c/H5d. The information value now lies in independent local evidence that resolves the latent chain:

`interaction state -> effective pollination service -> reproductive outcome -> phenotype`.

The strongest Chapter 2 test is not merely whether a floral trait changes across islands. It is whether predeclared interaction/service transitions predict distinct response geometries across reproductive and floral channels within the same biological system.
