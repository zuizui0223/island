# Figure 1 specification — hierarchical syndrome inference

## Core message

Figure 1 must begin with the biological island question and show how the analysis *changes the level of explanation*.

The reader should understand the paper in one glance:

> **Why are island flowers expected to become dull/generalized/self-reliant?**
> 
> The same assemblage pattern can be generated by repeated within-lineage response, hierarchical lineage sorting, or both. Chapter 1 identifies the response direction and assembly depth before allowing a named mechanism.

The figure is therefore an inference map, not a methods flowchart and not a collage of all analyses.

---

## Layout: four panels, read left-to-right

### Panel A — Original island question and rival generators

Header:

**WHY DO FLORAL TRAITS CHANGE WITH ISOLATION?**

Visual: one near-source island and one remote island. Show three separate response channels, not one bundled “syndrome” icon:

- floral attraction/colour;
- structural accessibility/generalization;
- reproductive assurance.

Under the remote island place two rival generators plus a mixed path:

```text
A1 repeated within-lineage response
source lineage ───────> island lineage*
                       trait changes

A2 hierarchical lineage sorting
source pool: A B C D E
                 ↓ geographic filter
island pool: A   C   E
             different trait composition

A3 mixed generator
sorting + within-lineage response
```

The purpose is to make clear that an assemblage mean cannot by itself distinguish adaptation from membership change.

Do not show a pollinator icon as the cause in this panel. Pollinator mechanism has not yet been identified.

**THE RESPONSE BRANCHES, THEN COLLAPSES AT A TAXONOMIC DEPTH**

Use a vertical decision tree:

```text
    Palearctic: accessibility ↑ + assurance ↑

    observed       family-adjusted       genus-adjusted
       4/4   ────────>   4/4   ───────────>   0/4
                         │

```

Directly annotate:

- genus attenuation: **78.8–85.9% of observed vector**;
- conditional family→genus attenuation: **70.6–79.1%**;
- label the transition **assembly depth**.

Use a bracket or depth ruler on the side:

`assemblage → family → genus → beyond-genus`

The visual emphasis should be on the *location of attenuation*, not on “controlling away” the result.

### Panel C — Cross-examination / falsification wall

Header:

**WHAT DID NOT EXPLAIN THE PATTERN?**

Use compact evidence cards with a checkmark only for “tested”, not for “supported mechanism”. Suggested structure:

```text
Trait missingness (V5)        Palearctic core survives finite MNAR grid
Species-list bias (V6)        Palearctic 99/100; remote-under-survey 80/80 survives
Area mechanism (H4)           0/16 promoted
Common breakpoint             0/12 observed nonlinear shapes promoted
Channel heterogeneity (N1)    p = 0.655; underpowered for modest effects
GloBI source breadth          0/4 promoted
Biotic vs wind specificity    interaction +0.065, p = 0.412
Distributed thresholds        0/8 identifiable; false threshold calls 19–25.5%
```

Colour logic for the final artwork:

- **survives challenge** = shield icon;
- **alternative not promoted** = open circle / crossed mechanism icon;
- **non-identifiable** = question-mark boundary icon.

Avoid green/red “success/failure” framing. These are inferential outcomes, not software tests.

A short conclusion ribbon under the cards:

> **Strong plant-side pattern; upstream pollinator mechanism still unidentified.**

## Visual hierarchy

The dominant eye path should be:

`island question → rival generators → context branch → family→genus attenuation → falsification wall → local mechanism`

Do not make H1–H5 labels visually dominant. The biology should be readable even if the reader ignores the hypothesis codes.

Recommended largest text elements:

1. `WHY DO FLORAL TRAITS CHANGE WITH ISOLATION?`
2. `ADAPTATION OR HIERARCHICAL ASSEMBLY?`
3. `ASSEMBLY DEPTH: FAMILY → GENUS`
4. `MECHANISM REMAINS TO BE RESOLVED LOCALLY`

## Figure 1 legend draft

**Figure 1 | From an island floral syndrome to a hierarchy-of-assembly test.** An isolation-associated change in floral or reproductive composition can arise through repeated within-lineage response, differential assembly of source-available lineages, or both (A). The global analysis first rejects a single universal floral/reproductive response and identifies biogeographic branching, then asks at what taxonomic depth the strongest branch is expressed (B). The broad Palearctic response remains after family adjustment but fails the predeclared gate after source-matched genus adjustment (`4/4 -> 4/4 -> 0/4`), with most attenuation concentrated at the family-to-genus transition. Independent robustness and falsification analyses constrain alternative explanations: the Palearctic pattern survives finite trait-missingness and specified species-detection challenges, whereas continuous area, a common nonlinear breakpoint, coarse pollinator-channel heterogeneity, sampled interaction breadth, and an independent biotic-versus-wind contrast do not supply a promoted global mechanism; distributed lineage thresholds are not identifiable against heterogeneous smooth clines in the present design (C). Chapter 1 therefore identifies the direction, context, and assembly depth of the syndrome but not a pollinator-specific causal chain. A deeply resolved local system is required to distinguish interaction, effective-service, reproductive, and phenotypic response geometries (D). Solid arrows indicate levels directly supported by Chapter 1 data; dashed arrows indicate mechanistic links that remain prospective or are delegated to Chapter 2.

## Claim-safety rules

- Do not draw `distance -> pollinator loss -> flower simplification` as a solid causal chain.
- Do not draw large-bee/butterfly/bird architecture labels as observed visitors.
- Do not label genus attenuation as `evolution absent`.
- Do not label H5c p=0.412 as evidence that pollinators do not matter.
- Do not draw one global threshold after the 0/12 geometry result.
- Do not use endemicity as a time axis.
- Do not make the tropical accessibility branch look as robustness-secure as the Palearctic branch.
- Do make family→genus attenuation visually central: it is the key explanatory result, not a nuisance adjustment.
