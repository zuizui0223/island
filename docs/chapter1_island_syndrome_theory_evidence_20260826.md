# Island floral/reproductive syndrome theory and evidence

Date: 2026-08-26
Scope: primary empirical studies, original quantitative syntheses, and the claim boundary for Chapter 1.

## Bottom line

There is no well-supported universal floral island syndrome. The best-supported general pattern is a **colonization/assembly filter favouring some capacity for uniparental reproduction**, but its effect is moderate, taxonomically and geographically variable, and does not imply that flowers must become small, plain, or generalized. Island pollination networks are often smaller and functionally poorer, yet alternative pollinators, interaction release, and context-specific specialization can maintain or redirect floral traits. Direct evidence linking the entire sequence

```text
distance -> guild-specific dispersal failure -> realized service loss
         -> selfing and/or changed attraction traits
```

is rare. The Izu system currently provides the closest community-level empirical bridge, but even there the design is regional and observational rather than a replicated historical manipulation.

The proposed theory should therefore be sharpened from a one-way “pollinator loss causes simplification” model into the **Dispersal-filtered pollination-service branching hypothesis**, with two parallel plant responses and an explicit branching condition:

```text
source-channel availability x island distance/connectivity
x channel-specific dispersal and establishment ability
    -> realized island pollination service
       ├─ reproductive-assurance pathway (SC/autofertility/selfing)
       └─ attraction/access pathway
            ├─ relaxed attraction when the focal channel is effectively unavailable
            ├─ reinforced attraction when scarce remaining pollinators still respond to display
            └─ maintained/redirected traits under effective replacement
```

This branching condition matters. Experimental pollinator reduction in *Lobelia siphilitica* strengthened selection for taller inflorescences and more vibrant petals, not reduced attraction. Reduced attraction is therefore predicted only when the marginal return of attraction collapses, not whenever pollinators become scarce.

## 1. What “island floral syndrome” currently means

The literature contains several partly independent theories that are too often collapsed into one label.

1. **Baker/colonization assurance**: a lone or very small founder group benefits from self-compatibility or autofertility because mate and pollinator availability are uncertain.
2. **Disharmonic assembly**: island floras and pollinator faunas are non-random subsets of regional source pools because dispersal, establishment, environment, and mutualist availability filter colonists.
3. **Pollinator depauperation and interaction reorganization**: oceanic-island networks can contain fewer species and links, lower interaction diversity, and greater overlap or opportunism.
4. **Post-colonization floral evolution**: changed pollinator composition may alter size, access architecture, colour, scent, reward, and breeding system within lineages.
5. **Functional replacement/interaction release**: resident or newly arriving birds, lizards, flies, moths, bees, or introduced species can take on interactions not predicted from their mainland roles.

These mechanisms make different predictions. Baker's law concerns establishment and reproductive mode; it does not by itself predict colour, shape, visitor identity, or a distance gradient within islands. Raw island trait composition can be produced by source-pool composition and colonization filtering without any in-situ evolution.

## 2. Evidence matrix

### 2.1 Baker's law and reproductive assurance

| Source | Scope and method | Main result | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Baker 1955](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1558-5646.1955.tb01544.x) | Original theoretical note on establishment after long-distance dispersal. | A self-compatible founder can establish sexually without a compatible mate. | Mechanistic logic, not a global empirical effect and not a floral-attraction theory. |
| [Grossenbacher et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.14534) | >1,500 species in Asteraceae, Brassicaceae, and Solanaceae; island-mainland comparison with taxonomic controls. | 66% of island species versus 41% of mainland species were self-compatible; island occurrence of SI species was less than half that of SC species. The effect persisted after excluding island endemics. However, SC frequency did not differ between near and far islands; oceanic islands had about 20% more SC taxa than continental islands. | Strong association for three families and a colonization-filter interpretation; no direct pollinator mechanism and no continuous distance response. |
| [Razanajatovo et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.12854) | 1,752 species from 161 angiosperm families; continuous self-compatibility/autofertility metrics; native, naturalized, colonist, endemic and mainland classes. | High selfing ability made island-colonist status only slightly more likely. It did not predict island endemicity, and did not differ between oceanic and continental colonists or native and naturalized colonists. | Broad but moderate support for establishment advantage; explicitly rejects treating Baker's law as a universal or large-magnitude island syndrome. |
| [Lord 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/aobpla/plv095) | Trait compilation for 321 native species across 11 Southern Ocean island groups. | 34.3% had traits consistent with wind pollination. Of 94 potentially self-fertilizing species with data, 92.6% were at least partly SC, but wind pollination and gender dimorphism were not elevated relative to southern temperate reference floras. Biotic-pollination traits were more frequent on milder islands. | Regional assemblage evidence with severe missing reproductive data; does not establish pollinator loss or universal selfing. |

**Verdict:** reproductive assurance is a real and broadly supported colonization filter, but it is neither necessary nor sufficient for floral simplification. It should be modeled as one response pathway, not the upstream cause of all attraction changes.

### 2.2 Pollinator depauperation, network generalization, and functional loss

| Source | Scope and method | Main result | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Traveset et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/geb.12362) | Global comparison of 52 quantitative pollination networks: 11 mainland, 23 continental-island, and 18 oceanic-island networks. | Oceanic networks were least species- and link-rich, had the lowest interaction diversity, and the highest plant niche overlap. Geographic origin affected only 5 of 12 network metrics; connectance, nestedness, modularity, and network specialization were not generally different from mainland networks. | Strong evidence for smaller/poorer networks, not a universal “more generalized network” result and not direct pollination service. Cross-study sampling remains heterogeneous. |
| [Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14527) | 40 spatiotemporal coastal networks at three Honshu sites and five Izu oceanic islands; flower-tube and pollinator-proboscis matching; community pollination success. | Pollinator richness, functional diversity, and community trait matching decreased with mainland distance. Low abundance of long-tongued pollinators, rather than species richness alone, reduced plant functional specialization and trait matching; lower matching predicted lower community pollination success. | The strongest regional evidence for `distance -> functional-guild loss -> matching/function`; still observational, one region/habitat, and not evidence of historical plant evolution. |
| [Brosi & Briggs 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1307438110) | Experimental removal of a single bumblebee species and tracking of fidelity, pollen transport/deposition, and seed production in a mainland alpine community. | One-species loss changed remaining bees' floral fidelity and reduced plant reproduction despite other potential pollinators. | Direct proof that apparent replacement can be functionally incomplete; not an island or long-term evolutionary study. |

**Verdict:** species richness is an inadequate proxy. The causal variable should be **effective service**, ideally `visitation x per-visit effectiveness`, with pollinator functional diversity and trait matching as intermediate measurements.

### 2.3 Floral simplification, maintenance, and the relaxed-attraction claim

| Source | Scope and method | Main result | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Inoue & Amano 1986](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-1984.1986.tb00018.x) | Mainland Honshu and Izu *Campanula punctata* populations; visitor observations, flower size, common-garden pollination experiments. | Honshu was dominated by *Bombus diversus*; more distant islands were dominated by halictid bees, flowers were smaller, and breeding systems shifted from mostly SI on Honshu to SC/potentially autogamous on Hachijo. | Compelling single-lineage concordance and a model natural history system; distance, pollinator composition, founder history, and environment are not experimentally separated. |
| [Abe 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcl117) | Traits for 269 native Ogasawara species; field visitor surveys on 12 islands, with visitor data for 103 species. | The flora was rich in small/subdued/accessibly shaped flowers; small endemic bees visited 66.7% of observed species on satellite islands. Yet bird-, moth-, and carpenter-bee-associated specialized niches persisted. | Community concordance with both a dominant accessible pattern and exceptions; most visitors were not evaluated by per-visit effectiveness, so phenotype cannot identify causal pollinators. |
| [Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018) | 556 species and 136 phylogenetically independent island-mainland sister contrasts across seven Pacific island systems. | No average evolution toward smaller flowers. Galápagos and Revillagigedo taxa did become smaller, while family effects ranged from smaller to larger or unchanged. | Direct rejection of universal flower-size simplification; it also implies that assemblage filtering may explain some raw community patterns better than in-situ evolution. |
| [Ciarle et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcaf005) | 129 independent colonization events in ten Southwest Pacific archipelagos, paired with closest mainland relatives; controls for breeding system, source pool, family, island type, and differentiation. | Animal-pollinated flowers followed an island rule (small flowers became larger and large flowers smaller), whereas wind-pollinated flowers became larger. Animal-pollinated display did not simply decline. | Strong evidence of context-dependent in-situ size evolution, not a pollinator-identity or attraction mechanism. |
| [Brown & Caruso 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.10706) | Experimental reduction, ambient pollination, and supplemental hand pollination in bee-pollinated *Lobelia siphilitica*. | Reduced pollination caused pollen limitation and strengthened selection for taller inflorescences and more vibrant petals. | Direct counterexample to automatic relaxed attraction: moderate scarcity can reinforce attraction if remaining pollinators are responsive. Not an island study. |
| [Schueller 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10682-006-9125-9) | Island-mainland *Nicotiana glauca*: corolla length, hummingbird bill length, phenotypic selection and per-visit effectiveness. | Island flowers and bills were longer, but longer corollas were not consistently favoured through pollen transfer or seed output. | Demonstrates that floral-pollinator trait matching alone does not prove pollinator-mediated selection. |

**Verdict:** the relaxed-attraction pathway is plausible but conditional. A better prediction uses the **marginal fitness return of attraction**:

```text
low service + attraction-responsive residual pollinators -> stronger attraction may be favoured
near-zero focal service + no effective replacement      -> relaxed attraction/generalized access
effective alternative service                           -> maintained or redirected attraction traits
```

### 2.4 Guild mobility and functional replacement

| Source | Scope and method | Main result | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Poulsen & Rasmussen 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13592-020-00778-x) | Bee faunas of seven islands/island groups compared with mainland bee trait distributions. | More isolated islands were enriched for wood-nesting bees; moderate-sized bees were also disproportionately represented, consistent with rafting/passive transport and longer flight range. | Supports trait-based, not guild-label-based, dispersal vulnerability. It does not rank Bombus, birds, butterflies, and moths on one universal mobility scale. |
| [Traveset et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms7376) | Four years of bird-flower observations on 12 Galápagos islands. | All sampled land-bird species used floral resources, producing a highly generalized network through interaction release. | Strong evidence for alternative visitation and niche expansion; birds were described as potential pollinators, not universally effective replacements. |
| [Rodríguez-Rodríguez et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-013-2606-y) | Canary Island *Isoplexis canariensis*; bird and lizard visitation plus single-visit fruit/viable-seed outcomes. | Birds and lizards occupied different effectiveness positions; birds were more effective because of higher visitation, and adult and juvenile lizards differed in seed quality. | Shows that replacement must be measured as quantity and quality, and that even co-visiting vertebrates are not functionally equivalent. One plant system cannot establish a global replacement rule. |

**Verdict:** “birds/butterflies/moths are more mobile and therefore replace bees” is not currently established as a general mechanism. Mobility varies strongly within every guild and depends on flight morphology, migratory behaviour, nesting/rafting, life cycle, host dependence, wind assistance, and stepping-stone geography. The confirmatory predictor must be an independently compiled **channel-specific mobility/colonization trait index**, not a categorical guild ranking. Current evidence is sufficient to motivate such an index, not to assign its values from intuition.

### 2.5 Source-pool assembly versus in-situ evolution

| Source | Scope and method | Main result | Claim ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| [König et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecog.05174) | 178 oceanic island floras and 735 mainland regions. Probabilistic source pools were predicted from mainland-only geographic and environmental turnover, then observed family representation was compared with source-pool null expectations. | Predominantly biotically pollinated families were under-represented relative to abiotically pollinated families. Family traits explained little total variation (Bayesian R² about 0.022). Island area and elevation, but not nearest-mainland distance, predicted overall compositional disharmony clearly. | Definitive evidence that source-pool standardization is necessary and feasible; family-level pollination modes are too coarse to identify floral channels or explain much island-level variance. |
| [Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1086/709018) | Sister-lineage contrasts, as above. | A raw island excess of small flowers, if present elsewhere, need not be caused by in-situ shrinking because the broad sister-lineage test found no universal reduction. | Direct warning against interpreting community composition as plant evolution. |

**Verdict:** realm is a useful coarse stratifier but not a source-pool adjustment. Nearest-continent distance simultaneously represents isolation, connectivity, and access to candidate sources. Chapter 1 needs an island-specific mainland expectation before it can say which part of a floral pattern is excess filtering beyond source supply.

## 3. What the current repository has already demonstrated

The current result in [`chapter1_pr138_robustness_checkpoint.md`](chapter1_pr138_robustness_checkpoint.md) supports a plant-side pattern:

- mainland distance is associated with biogeographically contingent floral/syndrome composition;
- the Palearctic shows a strong shift away from `large_bee_like` and toward `generalized_accessible` composition;
- the attraction/access shift remains after conditioning on the measured strict selfing core;
- the Nearctic pilot does not reproduce the Palearctic vector;
- Neotropical and Oceanian results do not demonstrate maintained bird/butterfly syndromes;
- there is no current moth/hawkmoth axis;
- no pollinator variable enters the fitted Chapter 1 design.

Therefore the repository already contradicts a selfing-only explanation at the level of statistical decomposition, but it has **not** identified the upstream cause. The current direct claim remains:

> Isolation distance is associated with a biogeographically contingent reorganization of floral and reproductive trait composition, especially a Palearctic shift from large-bee-compatible architecture toward generalized accessibility, which is not reducible to the measured strict selfing core.

It cannot yet say:

- Bombus disappearance caused the Palearctic pattern;
- bird, butterfly, or moth mobility prevented a comparable syndrome elsewhere;
- distance is a pure pollinator-dispersal treatment;
- realm controls have separated source-pool supply from isolation;
- the assemblage pattern represents within-lineage evolution.

## 4. Dispersal-filtered pollination-service branching hypothesis

For island `i`, channel `c`, and floral response `t`, define:

- `S_ic`: source-channel exposure, estimated independently from mainland/source data;
- `V_ic`: channel vulnerability to the island route, based on mobility and establishment traits;
- `G_i`: geography/connectivity, including distance and stepping-stone structure;
- `Q_ic`: realized effective service (`visitation x per-visit effectiveness`);
- `R_ic`: effective replacement by alternative channels;
- `E_ic`: elasticity of effective service to plant attraction traits.

The causal proposal is:

```text
Q_ic = f(S_ic, V_ic, G_i, climate_i, area_i, observation_effort_i)

reproductive_assurance_i = g(Q_ic, mate_availability_i, lineage_i)

attraction_or_access_i = h(Q_ic, R_ic, E_ic, selfing_core_i, lineage_i)
```

The last function must allow nonlinearity. It is not defensible to predeclare that all lower service causes less attraction.

### Falsifiable predictions

| Prediction | Result that supports it | Result that weakens it |
|---|---|---|
| Source exposure is necessary | Distance-related channel-specific floral change occurs where the source pool could use the channel, but not where it was structurally absent. | The same syndrome appears in exposed and structurally absent regions. |
| Vulnerability is mobility-dependent | Within source-exposed systems, low-mobility/low-establishment channels decline more strongly with route isolation than independently scored high-mobility channels. | Guild-specific decline is unrelated to predeclared mobility/establishment traits. |
| Functional loss, not richness alone, matters | Trait matching and effective service track functional diversity/abundance after richness is controlled. | Species richness fully explains service and functional composition adds nothing. |
| Selfing and attraction are parallel | Distance/service effects on attraction persist after a strict selfing core is modeled, while selfing also responds independently. | Attraction shifts disappear after selfing is represented or occur only in selfing lineages. |
| Relaxed attraction has a boundary | Attraction weakens only when effective service and its elasticity approach zero. | Attraction weakens equally under moderate service shortage where visitors still respond. |
| Replacement attenuates or redirects | High measured alternative-channel service weakens the generalized/selfing shift or preserves an alternative multivariate syndrome. | Putative replacement has no effect on pollen deposition, seed output, or the plant response vector. |
| Assembly differs from evolution | Source-pool residuals persist after status/genus/phylogenetic matching, and sister-lineage changes point in the same direction. | Raw effects vanish against the source pool or are absent in island-mainland sister contrasts. |

## 5. Stronger proof strategy

### Tier 1 — global, outcome-blind causal bridge

1. **Construct island-specific probabilistic source pools** from mainland occurrence, climate, geography, and stepping-stone connectivity without looking at island trait outcomes. Realm can be retained as a sensitivity, not the source-pool definition.
2. **Define source exposure by channel** from mainland plant dependence/visitor evidence. A channel that is structurally absent from the source cannot be coded as island loss.
3. **Predeclare a pollinator vulnerability index** from independent traits: flight morphology, body size, migration, nesting/rafting, sociality/colony establishment, host dependence, diapause, and known over-water dispersal. Do not assign `bird > butterfly > moth > bee` globally.
4. **Use source-pool residual plant outcomes**: observed island syndrome score minus the expected score under the island-specific candidate pool. Repeat by all native, native non-endemic, and endemic strata and with genus/phylogeny-preserving nulls.
5. **Fit direct interaction tests**, not separate significance claims:

   ```text
   effective_service ~ source_exposure * isolation * vulnerability
                       + connectivity + area + climate + effort

   attraction_residual ~ predicted_effective_service
                         + replacement
                         + nonlinear_service:attraction_elasticity
                         + selfing_core + geography

   selfing_core ~ predicted_effective_service + geography
   ```

6. **Calibrate syndrome scores on a direct-data subset** with visitation, pollen deposition, and bagging. A measurement-error or latent-variable model can transfer that calibration globally, but floral syndrome must remain a noisy plant-side indicator rather than observed pollinator identity.

This tier would markedly strengthen Chapter 1, but it still establishes a causal-compatible global pattern rather than historical proof.

### Tier 2 — replicated archipelago natural experiment

Select 3–5 source-to-island systems spanning the candidate regimes:

- Palearctic large/long-tongued-bee channel;
- tropical Lepidoptera or opportunistic vertebrate channel;
- southern bird/generalist-vertebrate channel;
- at least one structurally absent source regime.

Within each, pair mainland, near-island, and far-island sites matched on climate, area/habitat, and source flora. Measure:

- pollinator abundance and visitation by functional guild;
- pollen deposition and pollen quality per visit;
- fruit/seed set under open, bagged, and supplemental-cross treatments;
- plant dependence and autonomous selfing;
- quantitative flower size, tube depth, symmetry, spectra/colour contrast, scent, nectar and display;
- species status, phylogeny, and island-mainland sister or same-genus contrasts.

The key endpoint is `effective service = visitation x per-visit effectiveness`, not occurrence. Historical introductions/extirpations, successful pollinator reintroductions, or well-documented recent channel changes offer stronger before-after/control-impact or difference-in-differences leverage where available.

### Tier 3 — experimental test of the attraction branch

In tractable focal species, manipulate both service and replacement:

```text
focal channel retained / reduced / excluded
x alternative channel retained / excluded
x controlled pollen supplementation
```

Measure phenotypic selection on colour contrast, display, tube/access traits, herkogamy, autonomous selfing, and fitness. This separates:

- direct selection on attraction;
- correlated selfing-syndrome evolution;
- reproductive assurance;
- functional replacement.

It is the most direct way to test whether attraction is relaxed, reinforced, or redirected under pollinator decline.

## 6. Recommended Chapter 1 positioning

The current data are strong enough for **when/where plant-side pattern identification** and for rejecting a universal, selfing-only island syndrome. They are not strong enough to make pollinator loss the Chapter 1 identified cause.

The corresponding global analysis checkpoint is
[`chapter1_global_branching_checkpoint_20260826.md`](chapter1_global_branching_checkpoint_20260826.md).

Recommended main claim:

> Island floral filtering is biogeographically contingent rather than universal. Isolation is associated with different multivariate plant-trait responses across regions, and the Palearctic attraction/access response is statistically separable from strict reproductive assurance. These patterns are compatible with dispersal-filtered reorganization of regionally available pollination channels, but direct channel loss, attraction-selection relaxation, and functional replacement remain hypotheses requiring independent service and source-pool data.

Recommended mechanistic handoff:

> Chapter 2 should test whether channel-specific effective service declines as a function of source exposure, route isolation, and pollinator mobility, and whether selfing and floral attraction respond as parallel, replacement-modified pathways.

The centre of the theory is therefore not “islands become selfing and plain.” It is:

> **Island isolation filters pollination channels according to their source availability and route-specific dispersal/establishment traits; plant reproduction and attraction respond separately to the effective service that remains, and alternative guilds can attenuate or redirect—but do not automatically replace—the lost function.**
