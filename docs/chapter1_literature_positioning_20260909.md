# Chapter 1 literature positioning — 2026-09-09

This note fixes the closest literature comparisons for `docs/chapter1_manuscript_full_v7_submission_order_20260909.md`. It is a manuscript-writing aid, not a new analysis contract.

## Closest empirical and conceptual neighbours

### Grossenbacher et al. 2017 — Baker/colonization filter

D. L. Grossenbacher et al. **Self-compatibility is over-represented on islands.** *New Phytologist* 215:469–478. DOI: `10.1111/nph.14534`.

Contribution already established by prior work:

- island floras can over-represent self-compatible species;
- the biologically relevant interpretation can be a colonization/establishment filter rather than repeated post-colonization evolution;
- breeding system can therefore leave a geographic assembly signal.

What Chapter 1 adds:

- reproductive assurance is only one component of the response;
- it is analysed jointly with floral-access architecture rather than as a single trait;
- the effect is evaluated along a continuous source-separation gradient rather than only island/mainland status;
- regional response-vector differences are tested directly;
- source/family/genus depth is decomposed after the pattern is established.

Therefore the paper should not be sold as a new global test of Baker's law.

### Zell et al. 2025 — trait-dependent island colonization

A. N. Zell, C. H. Miranda, E. L. Grady, D. L. Grossenbacher & B. Igić. **Island colonization in flowering plants is determined by the interplay of breeding system, lifespan, floral symmetry, and arrival opportunity.** *New Phytologist* 245:420–432. DOI: `10.1111/nph.20234`.

Contribution already established by prior work:

- self-compatibility, lifespan and generalized/radial floral traits can predict island occurrence;
- arrival opportunity is a major component of island colonization;
- taxonomically conserved unmeasured traits contribute strongly.

What Chapter 1 adds:

- shifts from `trait -> probability of island occurrence` to `source separation -> multivariate assemblage response`;
- asks whether the island response itself branches biogeographically;
- separates reproductive assurance from floral architecture and demonstrates their decoupling;
- decomposes the supported assemblage signal from observed -> after family -> after source-matched genus;
- treats genus-level assembly as a biological result rather than only a control.

This is probably the closest modern neighbour and should be cited explicitly in the Introduction and Discussion.

### Schrader et al. 2024 — explicit source-pool trait filtering

J. Schrader, I. J. Wright, H. Kreft, K. Dixon, R. V. Gallagher, S. C. Andrew, P. Weigelt & M. Westoby. **Trait filtering in island floras: A conceptual framework.** *Journal of Biogeography* 51:1596–1606. DOI: `10.1111/jbi.14703`.

Contribution already established by prior work:

- island trait distributions should be compared explicitly with source-pool distributions;
- dispersal and environmental filters can alter island trait composition;
- filtering can be particularly evident on smaller islands;
- a source-pool framework can be more informative than generic multivariate functional-diversity summaries.

What Chapter 1 adds:

- scales the source-filter question from 70 islands in one archipelago to a global island universe;
- focuses on floral and reproductive architecture rather than vegetative/seed traits;
- tests biogeographic branching and direct response-vector heterogeneity;
- identifies the taxonomic depth of the broad response, including the `4/4 -> 4/4 -> 0/4` family-to-genus result;
- couples source filtering to an explicitly separate, still-unidentified pollination-channel side of the geographic barrier.

Schrader et al. should be used as a conceptual ally rather than framed as something the current paper replaces.

### Delavaux et al. 2024 — mutualism filter on island species deficits

C. S. Delavaux et al. **Mutualisms weaken the latitudinal diversity gradient among oceanic islands.** *Nature* 627:335–339. DOI: `10.1038/s41586-024-07110-y`.

Contribution already established by prior work:

- mutualist-dependent plant species can be disproportionately filtered from oceanic island floras;
- area, isolation and biotic dependence jointly contribute to island species deficits;
- the strength of the inferred mutualism filter varies geographically.

What Chapter 1 adds:

- response is floral/reproductive trait architecture rather than species-richness deficit;
- primary analysis does not assign a pollinator mechanism from plant traits;
- reproductive and pollination-associated floral components are estimated separately;
- source/genus assembly is explicitly decomposed before a pollination-channel mechanism can be promoted;
- H5 requires direct visitation/effectiveness/service evidence rather than a plant mutualism category alone.

The manuscript should acknowledge that biotic/mutualism filtering of island floras is not a novel concept; the novelty is the identification ladder and multivariate floral/reproductive decomposition.

### Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020 — no universal flower-size island rule

M. C. Hetherington-Rauth & M. T. J. Johnson. **Floral Trait Evolution of Angiosperms on Pacific Islands.** *The American Naturalist* 196:87–100. DOI: `10.1086/709018`.

Contribution already established by prior work:

- island flowers are not universally smaller than mainland relatives;
- local/archipelago-specific responses can occur without a universal direction.

What Chapter 1 adds:

- moves from one floral-size axis and sister contrasts to assemblage-level multivariate responses;
- asks directly where the response vector changes direction;
- separates floral architecture from reproductive assurance;
- explains part of the regional pattern through source-matched lineage assembly.

This paper is useful in the Introduction as direct motivation for H1 as a rival rather than an assumed law.

### Rosas-Guerrero et al. 2014 — pollination syndromes as probabilistic multivariate hypotheses

V. Rosas-Guerrero et al. **A quantitative review of pollination syndromes: do floral traits predict effective pollinators?** *Ecology Letters* 17:388–400. DOI: `10.1111/ele.12224`.

Use in Chapter 1:

- supports using floral-trait combinations as biologically meaningful multivariate architecture;
- does not license equating a floral template with realized pollinator identity;
- fits the PR142 design in which guild-labelled templates are secondary concordance and H5 requires independent channel data.

## Ready-to-insert novelty paragraph

> Previous global work has shown that self-compatibility can be over-represented on islands and that breeding system, floral symmetry and arrival opportunity jointly predict island colonization (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Zell et al. 2025). Source-pool studies further show that island trait distributions can be shaped by selective assembly rather than by island status per se (Schrader et al. 2024), while global analyses indicate that mutualist dependence can contribute to island species deficits in geographically contingent ways (Delavaux et al. 2024). At the same time, floral evolution does not follow one universal island direction, as illustrated by the absence of a general flower-size reduction across Pacific island–mainland sister contrasts (Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson 2020). We build on these results by asking a different hierarchical question: whether increasing source separation produces a common multivariate floral/reproductive response, where that response branches, and at what source and taxonomic depth each branch is generated. Rather than treating lineage composition as a nuisance control or floral syndrome as a pollinator classifier, we decompose the response into reproductive assurance, pollination-associated floral architecture and source-matched lineage assembly before allowing any pollination-channel mechanism to be considered.

## Ready-to-insert Discussion positioning paragraph

> Our results extend trait-based island biogeography from asking which traits predict island colonization to asking how the trait architecture of entire island assemblages changes along a source-accessibility gradient. The genus-depth result is especially important in this respect. A strong floral/reproductive distance gradient can arise without a robust beyond-genus residual if isolation changes which source-available genera are represented. This is compatible with the colonization-filter interpretation of Baker's law and with recent source-pool approaches to island trait filtering, but it shifts the biological object from a single colonization trait to a multivariate assemblage response. The subsequent decoupling of reproductive assurance and floral architecture then shows why a single island-syndrome axis is insufficient even after a source filter is established.

## Claim discipline for citations

- Do not cite Grossenbacher or Zell as evidence that the current Palearctic response is caused by Baker's law.
- Do not cite Delavaux as evidence for a specific pollinator-channel loss in the current dataset.
- Do not cite pollination-syndrome literature as direct evidence that the `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like` or `bird_like` labels identify actual visitors.
- Use Schrader et al. to motivate source-pool trait filtering and area as a possible modifier, not to claim that the current H4 mechanism is identified.
- Use Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson to motivate rejection of universal floral directions, not as a direct replication of the current assemblage design.
