# Chapter 1 manuscript core story v2

## Working title

**The island floral syndrome is not one syndrome: biogeographic decoupling of reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture**

## Central claim

The principal contribution is not the final percentage of trait coverage and not the exhaustion of database search. The principal contribution is a fixed, progressive hypothesis ladder that asks, in order, whether a floral island syndrome is universal, where it branches, how much of each branch is generated by source and lineage assembly, whether island area modifies that filtering, and which mechanistic claims remain unidentified without independent pollination evidence.

The database campaign matters because it prospectively stress-tested this ladder. Large improvements in usable trait evidence changed some secondary conclusions and one important taxonomic-depth conclusion, but the latest high-coverage snapshot left the current H1-H5 claim ceiling stable. Diminishing returns therefore justify stopping indiscriminate database acquisition; they are not the main ecological result.

---

## PR142 method as the paper's inferential architecture

The analysis follows the frozen `chapter1_progressive_analysis_v1` contract. Species-level trait evidence may improve between waves, but the island universe, ontology, model order, support gates, multiple-testing families, source/lineage safeguards, and causal claim ceiling do not change after outcome inspection.

### H1 — Universal-syndrome rival

Question: does isolation produce one coherent floral/reproductive response across contexts?

A universal syndrome is not inferred from significance in one region and non-significance in another. The required evidence is multivariate within-context support plus direct between-context vector comparison.

### H2 — Biogeographic branching

Question: if the universal model fails, where do isolation-associated response vectors differ?

The primary response is deliberately pollinator-name-free and separates:

- `accessibility_generalization`, representing increasingly open/shallow/radially accessible floral architecture;
- `reproductive_assurance`, representing strict reproductive-assurance traits.

Results are stratified by floristic status and checked in both broad analysis regimes and formal biogeographic realms. Native non-endemic persistence is used to show that a pattern is not confined to endemic taxa, not as a causal endemicity contrast.

### H3 — Source and lineage assembly

Question: when a regional assemblage response is supported, at what taxonomic depth does it remain?

The analysis explicitly separates:

1. observed assemblage response;
2. response after family composition;
3. response after source-matched genus composition;
4. lineage entry and within-genus species loading where supported.

Lineage composition is treated as part of the biological assembly process rather than a nuisance covariate to be removed and forgotten.

### H4 — Continuous area/capacity moderation

Question: is isolation-associated filtering stronger on smaller islands?

Area remains continuous. No post-hoc small/large threshold is introduced. Directional distance-by-area effects are not promoted to founder filtering, habitat capacity, or pollinator persistence unless the frozen equal-island, capped-information, direct-only, common-support and heteroskedastic-null safeguards pass.

### Secondary pollination-associated floral-architecture concordance

Only after the primary H2 plant response is estimated are fixed `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like`, and `bird_like` templates evaluated. These are sampled floral-architecture concordances, not pollinator observations or classifiers. The same templates are rerun on direct-only evidence and through source-adjusted analyses.

Because the templates share many traits, V4 estimates a source-trained common factor and then examines template-specific residuals. This is crucial: simultaneous movement of several guild-labelled scores cannot be read as simultaneous changes in several pollinator guilds.

### H5 — Independent channel-gated mechanism

Question: after H2-H4 safeguards, does an independently measured pollination-channel deficit explain residual floral/reproductive filtering?

Chapter 1 does not promote H5 because it lacks outcome-blind source-channel exposure linked to effort-aware occurrence/abundance or functional interaction evidence, retained-versus-disrupted-versus-structurally-absent contrasts, and effective-service information. Floral phenotype cannot substitute for those data.

### V1-V5 robustness logic

The progressive pipeline additionally keeps the major explanation gaps explicit:

- **V5 MNAR:** does the broad result survive a predeclared finite trait-resolution missingness grid?
- **V1 climate overlap:** can regional contrasts be transported over common measured-climate support?
- **V4 architecture decomposition:** are guild-labelled template responses mostly a shared floral-architecture dimension, and what residuals remain?
- **V2 taxonomic depth:** does the response persist beyond family and genus composition?
- **V3 area-support falsification:** does the apparent area effect survive measurement and heteroskedastic null safeguards?

These are not optional post-hoc robustness checks; together they define the PR142 claim ceiling.

## Main ecological findings

### Result 1 — The floral island syndrome is not universal

The latest snapshot does not recover one coherent floral/reproductive response across biogeographic contexts. This is stronger than saying that one trait is significant in one region but not another: the analysis was designed around multivariate response vectors and direct regional contrasts.

The ecological implication is that island isolation is not a single deterministic floral filter. Comparable geographic isolation can be associated with different combinations of reproductive and floral architecture responses depending on biogeographic context.

### Result 2 — The classic syndrome separates into at least two partially independent components

The traditional island-floral-syndrome narrative often bundles reproductive assurance and floral simplification together. PR142 separates them analytically.

`selfing_core` contains only self-incompatibility, mating-system and autonomous-selfing information. Floral attraction/access architecture is analysed separately. The resulting pattern shows that these components can couple or decouple.

This is the central ecological result:

> **Reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture are not one obligatory island response. Their coupling is biogeographically contingent.**

### Result 3 — Palearctic islands show a broad response, but its floral component is not reducible to selfing alone

The Palearctic contains the clearest broad primary response, combining increased accessibility/generalization and reproductive assurance with isolation across the main evidence scopes and floristic strata.

However, the attraction/access shift persists in conditional decomposition after `selfing_core` is included. Thus the observed floral-architecture shift cannot be reduced to the measured selfing syndrome alone.

This does not prove relaxed pollinator-mediated attraction selection. It does show that a purely reproductive-assurance explanation is insufficient for the observed plant-side architecture shift.

### Result 4 — Tropical islands break the expected selfing-to-simplification sequence

Tropical analyses provide the strongest counterexample to a serial model in which reproductive assurance necessarily leads to floral simplification. Reproductive assurance can increase while specialized or attractive floral architecture is maintained or strengthened.

This means that the same island-associated reproductive problem can coexist with a very different floral response branch.

A useful interpretation is therefore parallel rather than serial:

```text
isolation / source accessibility
        |\
        | \
        |  -> reproductive assurance
        |
        -> floral architecture

The signs and coupling of the two branches depend on context.
```

### Result 5 — Much of the Palearctic broad response is an assemblage-lineage result

The strongest taxonomic-depth result is `observed 4/4 -> after family 4/4 -> after genus 0/4` across all-analysis/direct-only and all-native/native-nonendemic combinations.

Thus the broad Palearctic response is compatible with source-matched genus-level assembly beyond family composition, rather than a robust beyond-genus residual.

Ecologically, this changes the interpretation from repeated within-lineage evolution toward differential representation of source-available lineages. Island floral filtering can be real at the assemblage level even when the broad pattern is largely produced by which genera arrive, establish or persist.

### Result 6 — Pollination-associated templates reveal regional architecture, not pollinator identity

Palearctic assemblages tend to move away from butterfly-like and large-bee-like floral architecture with isolation, whereas tropical assemblages show increases in all three sampled templates. But one source-trained factor explains about 87% of variation among these templates.

The simultaneous tropical increase is therefore biologically interesting precisely because it falsifies a hard guild-classification reading. The result is better understood as maintenance or increase of a shared specialized/attractive floral-architecture dimension.

Some tropical template-specific residual structure remains after source/genus adjustment, including a positive large-bee-like residual in several robust comparisons. This is a real plant-side residual, but it does not identify large-bee occurrence, effectiveness or replacement.

### Result 7 — Area and climate define boundaries rather than complete mechanisms

Area repeatedly modifies the precision or magnitude of some responses, but all frozen V3 classifications remain measurement-sensitive and fail the heteroskedastic-null promotion gate. Area therefore remains a modifier, not an identified founder/capacity mechanism.

Similarly, regional response heterogeneity is supported, but measured-climate-independent categorical biogeography is not consistently transportable under V1 common-support tests. The ecological pattern is region-associated; a causal effect of realm identity is not established.

### Result 8 — H5 is now an identification problem, not a missing-trait problem

The remaining mechanistic question is not solved by adding more flower-colour or reproductive-trait records. Chapter 1 can show where selfing and floral-architecture components couple or decouple, and how much of those patterns are associated with source/genus assembly. It cannot determine whether historical pollinator loss, altered effective service, competition for scarce service, or functional replacement generated the residual pattern.

That distinction creates the Chapter 2 question rather than weakening Chapter 1.

## Role of progressive waves and diminishing returns

The progressive wave design is evidence that the ecological conclusions are not artifacts of one incomplete snapshot.

Wave52 contained 184,917 analysis-usable species-by-axis cells. The final integrated snapshot contains 222,688, an increase of 37,771 usable cells, including 11,315 reproductive-assurance cells. The large analytical gain came mostly from materializing previously labelled evidence with complete composition/provenance, not from discovering a second database of comparable scale.

At the same time, new source acquisition entered strong diminishing returns. One Orchidaceae source produced +192 strict reproductive cells; later reviewed source packets typically added one to three. A residual audit scanned 1,187 staging files and more than 2.25 million rows and found zero immediately strict-ready reproductive records under the frozen evidence rules.

The stopping argument is therefore inferential rather than numerical:

1. a large increase in usable evidence no longer changes the main H1-H5 claim ceiling;
2. remaining acquisition has sharply declining realized yield;
3. unresolved mechanism claims require a different data type, not merely more of the same trait database.

The final snapshot is thus a defensible computational freeze for Chapter 1, not a claim of complete botanical knowledge.

## Chapter 1 -> Chapter 2 -> Izu handoff

### Chapter 1: WHEN and WHERE

The global database establishes:

- no universal floral island syndrome;
- biogeographically contingent plant response branches;
- partial decoupling of reproductive assurance and floral architecture;
- a strong Palearctic pattern compatible with genus-level assembly;
- tropical coexistence of reproductive assurance and specialized architecture;
- explicit climate/area/source/lineage claim boundaries;
- non-identification of realized pollination mechanism.

### Chapter 2 (`izu-core`): HOW and proximal WHY

The mechanism programme asks why a common broad pollinator-interaction perturbation need not generate one plant response. Its conditional response geometry separates partner loss/arrival balance, plant starting functional state, realized pollinator community, functional matching, effective service, local interaction filtering and autonomous assurance.

This offers a mechanistic class capable of generating exactly the kind of decoupling observed in Chapter 1: an ensemble-level island tendency can coexist with opposing lineage-level branches.

The Chapter 2 model is not used to retroactively validate the Chapter 1 regional vectors. Instead it explains why the absence of one universal vector is mechanistically plausible and identifies which measurements are required to distinguish candidate pathways.

### Return to islands and zoom to Izu

The prospective Izu field design then measures the channels that Chapter 1 lacks in the same linked populations:

- observation effort and visit rate;
- visitor identity/contact;
- single-visit pollen deposition;
- rate-weighted effective pollen service;
- open reproduction;
- bagged autonomous reproduction;
- supplemental-outcross reproduction;
- fruit/seed outcome.

This makes it possible to discriminate service limitation, autonomous-assurance buffering, network/service allocation and non-assurance buffering without using floral syndrome as a proxy for dependency.

Izu is therefore not a confirmation case chosen because it resembles the global pattern. It is the depth system where the missing interaction -> effective service -> dependency/assurance -> reproduction chain can be measured directly.

## One-sentence ecological message

> **Island floral syndromes are emergent assemblage-level outcomes of partially separable reproductive-assurance and pollination-associated floral responses; their coupling depends on biogeographic context and source-lineage assembly, while the interaction mechanisms that generate the residual branches require independent pollination evidence.**

## Manuscript emphasis

The paper should therefore devote most Results/Discussion space to:

1. H1/H2 regional response branching;
2. selfing-core versus floral-architecture decoupling;
3. H3 taxonomic-depth and genus assembly;
4. pollination-associated shared architecture and its interpretation;
5. V1/V3/H5 identification boundaries.

Trait-acquisition saturation should appear as the reason the computational campaign can now be frozen, not as the ecological novelty itself.
