# Chapter 1 manuscript core story v3

## Working title

**The island floral syndrome is not one syndrome: source-distance filtering and biogeographic decoupling of reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture**

## Central claim

The central ecological result is not a database-coverage percentage. Island isolation is associated with different combinations of reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture in different biogeographic contexts, and much of the strongest Palearctic response is compatible with source-matched genus-level assembly. The progressive trait campaign matters because large gains in usable evidence no longer changed that claim ceiling.

Two pieces of ecological interpretation must remain explicit.

1. **Distance is a source-accessibility gradient, not merely kilometres.** The formal PR142 exposure is `log1p_distance_to_continent_km`, interpreted as a composite isolation/connectivity/source-accessibility gradient. Source-pool information enters separately through the H3 source-matched analyses. Conceptually, the near-mainland limit represents weak geographic filtering and increasing access to the regional source pool, whereas increasing distance represents progressively stronger opportunity for source-to-island filtering. This is a limiting interpretation, not a claim that an island at distance zero is literally equivalent to mainland vegetation.

2. **Pollination channels need not have the same isolation susceptibility.** A functional channel can be retained, disrupted after being available in the source region, or structurally absent from the source region. Disruption should depend not only on distance but on the channel's dispersal and establishment ability. This provides a mechanistic hypothesis for why comparable geographic isolation can produce different floral outcomes. Chapter 1 does not estimate guild-specific dispersal ability; it identifies the plant-side pattern that such a mechanism would need to explain.

## PR142 inferential architecture

### H1 — Universal-syndrome rival

Does increasing source separation move island floras along one common floral/reproductive response vector?

A universal syndrome requires multivariate within-context support and direct between-context similarity; significance in one context and non-significance in another is not sufficient.

### H2 — Biogeographic branching

If H1 fails, does the response to source separation branch among biogeographic contexts?

The primary plant response is pollinator-name-free:

- `accessibility_generalization`;
- `reproductive_assurance`.

The ecological meaning of the distance slope is therefore not simply `km -> trait`. It is better read as:

```text
regional source pool
      |
      |  increasing geographic separation / decreasing accessibility
      v
near-source islands ------------------------------ remote islands
weak filtering                                      stronger filtering opportunity
      |                                                     |
      v                                                     v
assemblage closer to source expectation         assemblage increasingly filtered
```

The primary H2 model does not replace each island by an explicit mainland counterfactual. Rather, H3 supplies the source-matched expectation needed to determine whether the observed distance response is largely generated by source and lineage assembly.

### H3 — Source and lineage assembly

Supported H2 patterns are decomposed into:

1. observed assemblage response;
2. after family composition;
3. after source-matched genus composition;
4. genus entry and within-genus species loading where evaluable.

This creates the closest formal version of the source-limit idea. If `T_source` is the expected source-pool trait composition and `T_island(d)` the observed island composition at source separation `d`, then the ecological quantity of interest is conceptually

`DeltaT(d) = T_island(d) - T_source`.

The analysis asks whether `DeltaT` grows or changes direction with isolation and at what taxonomic depth that deviation is generated. `d -> 0` is a conceptual high-accessibility boundary, not an observed requirement that a zero-distance island equal the source flora.

### H4 — Continuous area/capacity moderation

Does the source-to-island filtering gradient strengthen on smaller islands? Area remains continuous and modifies the distance response; it is not discretized after seeing results.

### Secondary pollination-associated architecture

The fixed `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like`, and `bird_like` templates are evaluated only after the primary plant response. They are floral-architecture concordances, not realized visitor assignments.

### H5 — Channel-gated mechanism

The mechanistic question is whether a regionally available pollination channel is retained or disrupted as source separation increases, and whether that independently measured disruption explains residual plant filtering after source, lineage, area, climate, observation and spatial safeguards.

The critical state distinction is:

```text
source channel available
       |
       +---- retained on island
       |
       +---- disrupted / deficient on island

source channel unavailable
       |
       +---- structurally absent (not a loss)
```

The ecological extension is a **differential channel-loss hypothesis**:

```text
channel disruption
  = f(source availability,
      geographic separation,
      dispersal ability,
      establishment ability,
      realized community context)
```

Different pollinator functional groups can therefore experience the same island distance differently. A channel with low ocean-crossing or establishment probability can decline rapidly with separation, whereas a more mobile or readily establishing alternative can remain available. If equivalent function is retained, the floral response need not follow the same simplification/generalization trajectory.

This is deliberately a candidate mechanism, not a Chapter 1 result. The global plant database contains no direct estimate of guild-specific migration, colonization, or effective replacement.

## Main ecological findings

### 1. Distance produces regional trajectories, not one global syndrome

The key response is a **distance gradient**. The Palearctic and tropical floras do not move along the same reproductive/floral trajectory as source separation increases.

Palearctic isolation is associated with increased accessibility/generalization and reproductive assurance. Tropical analyses can show increased reproductive assurance while specialized/attractive architecture is maintained or strengthened. Thus the same direction of geographic separation does not imply the same direction of floral change.

### 2. The classic syndrome decomposes into two partly independent components

Reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture can couple or decouple. Palearctic floral architecture shifts persist after conditioning on `selfing_core`, so the floral response is not reducible to measured selfing alone. Tropical reproductive assurance can increase without floral simplification.

The ecological message is therefore not `distance -> selfing -> simple flower` but:

```text
source separation
      |\
      | \
      |  -> reproductive-assurance component
      |
      -> floral-architecture component

The signs and coupling of the two branches are context dependent.
```

### 3. Much of the strongest distance response is lineage assembly

The final Palearctic taxonomic-depth classification is `observed 4/4 -> after family 4/4 -> after genus 0/4` across both evidence scopes and both primary floristic strata. The broad distance response is therefore compatible with source-matched genus-level assembly rather than a robust beyond-genus primary residual.

This makes source distance biologically interpretable: increasing separation changes which source-available lineages are represented, not necessarily the phenotype of each lineage in parallel.

### 4. Pollination-associated architecture links the pattern to candidate interaction regimes

Palearctic assemblages move away from butterfly-like and large-bee-like floral architecture with isolation, whereas tropical assemblages increase along all three sampled guild-labelled templates. Because roughly 87% of template variance lies on one shared floral-architecture factor, the tropical result is best read as maintenance or strengthening of a common specialized/attractive architecture rather than evidence for three simultaneously increasing pollinator guilds.

### 5. Mobility provides a plausible explanation for why the same distance can have different biological meaning

A kilometre of ocean is not an equivalent barrier for every pollination channel. The plant-side pattern is consistent with a framework in which pollinator functional groups differ in oceanic-barrier susceptibility because dispersal and establishment abilities differ. Under this interpretation:

- where an important source-region channel is disruption-prone, increasing isolation can reduce functional continuity and favour different floral/reproductive assemblages;
- where alternative channels retain equivalent function despite isolation, specialized or conspicuous floral architecture need not be lost;
- structural absence of a channel in the source region is not evidence of island-driven loss.

The present analysis does **not** test which guild is more mobile. This paragraph is the ecological hypothesis generated by the Chapter 1 pattern and passed to H5/Chapter 2.

## Distance as a source-pool continuum

The manuscript should avoid describing the design as a simple island-versus-mainland comparison. A more informative interpretation is a continuous source-pool gradient:

```text
source mainland / regional source expectation
                 |
             d -> 0
                 |
       very near-source islands
                 |
       moderately isolated islands
                 |
          remote islands
                 |
        source filtering accumulates
```

The source pool functions as the ecological reference state, and distance represents the strength of geographic separation from that reference. In a conceptual limiting sense, an island placed arbitrarily close to its source should experience minimal oceanic source filtering; as distance increases, the probability that arrival, establishment and interaction channels are filtered can change.

However, the formal PR142 implementation keeps two quantities distinct:

- `distance_to_continent` is the geographic exposure used in the primary gradient;
- source-pool assignment and source-matched expectations are introduced in the H3 analyses.

Therefore the paper should say **`distance is interpreted as a source-accessibility gradient`**, not **`distance is literally the distance to the exact source pool for every island`**. The `d -> 0` source-pool limit is a conceptual counterfactual that clarifies the ecology; it is not an additional fitted data point.

This distinction also explains why H3 is necessary. A raw distance slope can reflect both true source-to-island filtering and pre-existing regional lineage composition. Source-matched family/genus decomposition asks how much of the apparent distance response remains once the regional source expectation is made explicit.

## Chapter 1 -> Chapter 2 handoff

Chapter 1 establishes WHEN and WHERE source separation is associated with assemblage filtering and where reproductive and floral components decouple. It also identifies the taxonomic depth of the strongest broad response.

Chapter 2 asks HOW the source-to-island interaction channel can produce those branches. The `izu-core` framework explicitly introduces partner loss/arrival balance, starting functional state, realized pollinator community, matching, effective service, local interaction filtering and autonomous assurance.

The mobility hypothesis sits exactly at this handoff:

```text
Chapter 1
source distance -> different plant-side response branches
        |
        v
unidentified ecological link
        |
        v
Chapter 2
partner arrival/loss and realized community
        -> matching
        -> effective service
        -> dependency / assurance
        -> response branch
```

The final Izu field design then measures the missing links directly. It does not infer migration or dependency from flower form.

## One-sentence ecological message

> **Increasing separation from a regional source pool does not impose one floral island syndrome: it filters lineage representation and partially independent reproductive and floral responses in context-dependent ways, potentially because pollination channels differ in their ability to cross and establish across oceanic barriers.**

## Claim boundary

The final clause is a mechanistic hypothesis, not a Chapter 1 causal result. Chapter 1 supports the distance-dependent, source-conditioned plant pattern; guild-specific dispersal ability, realized channel disruption, effective replacement and historical pollinator loss require independent evidence.
