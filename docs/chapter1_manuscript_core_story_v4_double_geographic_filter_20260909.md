# Chapter 1 manuscript core story v4

## Working title

**The island floral syndrome is not one syndrome: a double geographic filter on plant assembly and pollination channels**

## Core idea

Geographic distance is not a single ecological pressure. The same oceanic barrier acts on at least two coupled processes:

1. **plant source-to-island filtering** — which source-pool plant lineages can disperse, arrive, establish and persist on an island;
2. **pollination-channel filtering** — which pollinator functional groups can cross the same barrier, establish, persist and continue to provide effective service.

The observed island floral/reproductive assemblage is therefore generated by a **double geographic filter** rather than by distance acting directly on floral phenotype.

```text
regional source pool
      |
      | increasing oceanic separation
      v
+------------------------------+
| FILTER A: PLANTS             |
| propagule dispersal ability  |
| arrival probability          |
| establishment / persistence  |
+------------------------------+
      |
      | surviving plant lineages
      v
island plant assemblage
      ^
      |
+------------------------------+
| FILTER B: POLLINATION        |
| source-channel availability  |
| flight / dispersal ability   |
| ocean crossing               |
| establishment / persistence  |
| realized effective service   |
+------------------------------+
      ^
      |
regional pollinator pool
```

The two filters are coupled because plants that arrive under one pollination regime may face a different realized interaction environment after colonization.

## Distance as a source-pool continuum

The formal PR142 exposure is `log1p_distance_to_continent_km`. It is not treated as a mechanistically pure causal variable. It is interpreted as a composite gradient of geographic separation, connectivity and source accessibility.

Conceptually:

```text
regional source expectation
        |
      d -> 0
        |
very near-source islands
        |
moderately isolated islands
        |
remote islands
        |
stronger opportunity for geographic filtering
```

The ecological limiting argument is:

- as `d -> 0`, an island approaches a high-accessibility boundary where geographic filtering relative to the regional source pool should be weak;
- as `d` increases, source-to-island filtering can increasingly alter which plant lineages and ecological interaction channels remain represented.

This does **not** mean that a zero-distance island is literally equivalent to mainland vegetation. The `d -> 0` state is a conceptual source-accessibility limit, not an observed mainland datapoint.

For plant traits, a useful conceptual quantity is

`DeltaT(d) = T_island(d) - T_source`,

where `T_source` is the regional source-pool expectation and `T_island(d)` is the observed island assemblage at separation `d`.

PR142 does not fit this equation literally in the primary H2 model. Instead, it approximates the logic in two stages:

1. H2 estimates the distance-associated island response;
2. H3 introduces outcome-blind source-pool expectations and taxonomic decomposition to ask how much of the distance response is generated by source and lineage assembly.

---

## Filter A: plant source-pool and dispersal/establishment filtering

For plant lineage `z`, let

`E_plant(z, d)`

represent the probability that a lineage available in the regional source pool reaches, establishes and persists on the island.

Conceptually:

`E_plant = f(source availability, distance, propagule dispersal ability, establishment ability, persistence conditions)`.

Different plant lineages therefore experience the same geographic distance differently. Long-distance dispersers, lineages with repeated propagule supply, or lineages tolerant of small-island conditions may retain high establishment probability at distances that strongly filter other lineages.

This is the ecological interpretation of the strong H3 result. In the latest PR142 snapshot, the broad Palearctic response follows:

`observed 4/4 -> after family 4/4 -> after source-matched genus 0/4`.

Thus the strongest broad distance-response signal is compatible with genus-level source/assembly filtering rather than a robust beyond-genus primary residual.

The implication is not that distance directly transformed flowers. Instead, increasing source separation changed which source-available floral and reproductive lineages were represented.

---

## Filter B: pollinator movement, flight and establishment filtering

For pollination channel `g`, let

`A_poll(g)`

indicate whether the channel is available in the regional source system, and let

`E_poll(g, d)`

represent its probability of reaching, establishing and remaining functionally available on the island.

Conceptually:

`E_poll = f(source availability, distance, flight/dispersal ability, establishment ability, habitat suitability, realized community context)`.

This creates three biologically distinct channel states:

```text
source channel available
      |
      +---- retained on island
      |
      +---- disrupted / deficient on island

source channel unavailable
      |
      +---- structurally absent
```

Structural absence is not channel loss.

The same ocean distance therefore need not have the same biological effect for all pollinators. Flight ability is one component of dispersal capacity, but ocean crossing and successful colonization also depend on behaviour, propagule number, stepping-stone opportunities, habitat requirements and establishment probability.

The paper should therefore discuss **channel isolation susceptibility**, not simply “mobile versus immobile pollinators”.

A useful hypothesis is:

`channel disruption susceptibility = 1 - E_poll(g, d)`.

Channels with low ocean-barrier susceptibility may remain functionally available at distances that strongly disrupt other channels. If functional alternatives remain available, the floral response need not follow a classic simplification/generalization trajectory.

## Why this produces different island syndromes

The observed assemblage is generated jointly by the two filters.

```text
DISTANCE / OCEANIC BARRIER
          |
          +-----------------------------+
          |                             |
          v                             v
plant source filtering          pollinator-channel filtering
          |                             |
which lineages arrive           which services remain
          |                             |
          +--------------+--------------+
                         v
              realized plant-pollinator context
                         |
                         v
              reproductive assurance
                    + floral architecture
                         |
                         v
               observed island syndrome
```

This explains why the same geographic separation can generate different outcomes.

### Case A — both plant and pollinator filters align toward the classic syndrome

If isolation disproportionately retains plant lineages capable of reproductive assurance and simultaneously disrupts a source-available specialist pollination channel, distant islands can become enriched in accessible/generalized flowers and reproductive assurance.

### Case B — plant filtering occurs but effective alternative pollination remains

If some plant lineages are filtered by distance but an alternative functional pollination channel remains highly available, reproductive assurance can increase without floral simplification.

### Case C — source floral architecture differs and pollination channels are structurally different

If the regional source pool and its pollination architecture differ from the Palearctic source system, the same distance may generate a different floral trajectory. Structural absence of one channel cannot be interpreted as island-driven loss of that channel.

## Connection to the PR142 results

### 1. H1 — universal syndrome fails

A double-filter model predicts no reason for one universal response vector. Plant lineages and pollination channels differ among source regions and in their susceptibility to oceanic barriers.

### 2. H2 — biogeographic branching becomes biologically interpretable

Palearctic and tropical assemblages can occupy different trajectories because the underlying source pools and interaction channels are filtered differently as separation increases.

The current result is consistent with:

- Palearctic: accessibility/generalization increases together with reproductive assurance;
- Tropical: reproductive assurance can increase while specialized/attractive floral architecture is maintained or strengthened.

This does not identify the pollinator mechanism, but it is exactly the pattern expected when the two filters and their component susceptibilities differ among regions.

### 3. H3 — plant-side geographic filtering is directly supported

The genus-depth result provides empirical support for the plant-source side of the double filter. Much of the broad Palearctic distance response is represented by source-matched genus composition.

### 4. Secondary guild-concordance suggests, but does not identify, the pollinator side

Palearctic assemblages move away from butterfly-like and large-bee-like floral architecture with isolation, while tropical assemblages increase along all three sampled architecture templates.

Because approximately 87% of template variation lies on one shared floral-architecture factor, these are plant-side compatibility patterns, not direct evidence that one pollinator guild was lost or another replaced it.

### 5. H5 is the missing direct test of Filter B

PR142 does not contain guild-specific movement, colonization or effective-service data. Therefore the pollinator side of the double filter remains a mechanistic hypothesis.

H5 requires independent evidence of:

- source-region channel availability;
- retained vs disrupted vs structurally absent state;
- effort-aware occurrence/abundance or functional interaction;
- per-visit effectiveness / effective service where possible;
- residual explanatory value beyond source, lineage, area, climate, observation and spatial safeguards.

## Relationship to Baker filtering

The double-filter framework also separates a plant-only colonization explanation from an interaction-mediated explanation.

### Plant colonization / Baker-type filter

`distance x reproductive assurance -> establishment`

can operate even without pollinator-channel disruption.

### Interaction-mediated channel filter

`pollinator disruption x plant functional dependence -> reproductive / floral filtering`

requires an independently observed change in the pollination channel.

Therefore reproductive assurance and floral architecture should not be forced into one serial causal chain. The current PR142 result supports this separation because Palearctic floral-architecture shifts are not reducible to measured selfing core, while tropical reproductive assurance can coexist with specialized floral architecture.

## Chapter 1 -> Chapter 2

Chapter 1 resolves the plant-source side much farther than the pollinator-channel side.

```text
Chapter 1
source distance
   |
   +-> source / genus assembly      [substantially identified]
   |
   +-> reproductive + floral branch [identified as plant-side pattern]
   |
   +-> channel movement/service      [not identified]

                    v

Chapter 2 (`izu-core`)
partner arrival / loss balance
   -> realized pollinator community
   -> functional matching
   -> effective service
   -> dependency / autonomous assurance
   -> response branch
```

This is the mechanistic meaning of the Chapter 1 identification ceiling.

The global database is capable of showing that source separation produces context-dependent assemblage trajectories. It cannot by itself identify whether those trajectories arose because a particular pollinator failed to cross, failed to establish, was replaced by another functional channel, or remained present but ineffective.

---

## Izu as the direct test of the second filter

The Izu field design is valuable because it can observe several links that the global database collapses together:

- visitor occurrence and effort;
- visitor identity/contact;
- single-visit pollen deposition;
- rate-weighted effective service;
- reproductive dependency;
- autonomous assurance;
- open versus supplemented reproduction.

This allows a direct test of whether geographic reorganization of pollinator communities actually propagates through service and dependency to reproduction, rather than inferring the process from floral syndrome labels.

---

## One-sentence ecological claim

> **Oceanic distance acts twice: it filters which plant lineages can arrive and persist from the regional source pool, and it filters which pollination channels can cross, establish and continue to provide service; the interaction of these two geographic filters can generate different floral and reproductive island syndromes from the same increase in isolation.**

## Claim boundary

The current Chapter 1 data directly support the plant-side distance response, its biogeographic branching, and substantial genus-level source/assembly representation. They do not directly estimate plant dispersal kernels, guild-specific flight or ocean-crossing ability, pollinator colonization probability, historical channel loss, or effective functional replacement. Those terms are the mechanistic interpretation and prospective predictions of the double-filter framework, not fitted Chapter 1 causal estimates.
