# A recurrent global floral island syndrome aligns with pollen limitation through two trait pathways

## Full working manuscript v13 — unified global-pollination-constraint draft — 2026-09-17

### Abstract

Island plants are often expected to become more self-reliant and less dependent on specialized pollination as geographic isolation increases, but it remains unclear whether this expectation represents a recurrent global syndrome, a set of region-specific patterns, or taxonomic sorting among lineages. We combined a fixed global universe of 8,265 islands and 106,295 accepted angiosperm species with experimental pollen-limitation data to test a unified hypothesis: isolation is associated with a recurrent floral/reproductive island-syndrome direction because successful pollination becomes increasingly limiting, with plant assemblages responding through partially separable reproductive-assurance and floral-accessibility pathways.

Across contemporary observed island floras, six atomic floral and reproductive responses showed a supported multivariate association with isolation in all four broad biogeographic contexts. The average classic-syndrome direction was positive in northern mid-latitude, northern high-latitude, tropical and southern extratropical islands in both the primary and Direct-only evidence scopes. Response vectors nevertheless differed among regions; the North–Tropical joint difference remained supported (`p=0.000763` primary; `p=0.00450` Direct-only). Thus the global result is a recurrent direction with regional modification, not equality of response vectors.

Independent experimental evidence supported a common pollination constraint. Across 2,969 GloPL experiments from 1,248 sites and 919 publications, pollen limitation increased with distance from major continental landmasses (standardized slope `0.0794 ± 0.0377`; two-sided `p=0.0354`, one-sided positive `p=0.0177`). Plant responses separated into two globally positive components: reproductive assurance and generally accessible floral architecture. A post-hoc functional triangulation using the frozen exact-species GloPL overlaps then asked whether those trait states were associated with lower current pollen limitation. Autonomous selfing showed the strongest association (`β=-0.4467`, `p=2.60×10^-8`) and remained negative within publications (`β=-0.3060`, `p=0.0295`) and within publication-by-site groups (one-sided `p=0.0417`). Actinomorphy and generalized floral form also showed negative global associations, although their within-study support was weaker.

Taxonomic realization differed by flora layer. The broad all-observed response remained detectable after source-free genus residualization, whereas a separately defended source-backed native Palearctic response lost support after source-matched genus adjustment (`4/4 -> 4/4 -> 0/4`), with true genus structure exceeding matched arbitrary within-family partitions (`p=0.02899`). On the same 375 status-supported islands, the broad all-observed North–Tropical result persisted while the native-only contrast did not. We therefore infer a recurrent global assemblage syndrome aligned with increasing pollination limitation and expressed through two partially independent plant-response pathways, while taxonomic assembly determines how that syndrome is realized in different flora layers. The evidence triangulates a pollination-constraint hypothesis but does not establish historical mediation from pollen limitation to trait evolution.

**Keywords:** island biogeography; pollen limitation; reproductive assurance; selfing; floral accessibility; pollination syndrome; community assembly; taxonomic sorting; GloPL

## Introduction

Islands provide one of the clearest natural settings in which dispersal, reproduction and species interactions are simultaneously challenged. Geographic separation reduces connectivity with continental source regions, limits repeated immigration, and can make mates and mutualists unreliable. These pressures motivated a long-standing expectation that island plants should increasingly possess traits that permit establishment and reproduction without dependable partners. Baker's law formalized one part of this expectation: uniparental reproduction can be advantageous when colonists experience mate limitation or uncertain pollination (Baker 1955; Pannell & Barrett 1998). Global comparisons subsequently showed that self-compatible plants are over-represented on islands and that breeding system interacts with other traits during island colonization (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Zell et al. 2025).

The same logic extends beyond breeding system. Flowers are packages of traits shaped by the economics of attracting, admitting and using pollen vectors. Floral symmetry, tube depth, form, colour and display can increase compatibility with particular functional groups, but specialized architectures also create dependence on the availability and effectiveness of suitable visitors. Pollination-syndrome theory therefore predicts correlated combinations of floral traits rather than one diagnostic character (Fenster et al. 2004; Rosas-Guerrero et al. 2014). If successful pollination becomes unreliable on increasingly isolated islands, two responses are plausible. Plants may gain from **reproductive assurance**, through self-compatibility, autonomous selfing or predominantly selfing mating systems. Independently, the benefit of maintaining restricted or specialized floral access may decline, favouring assemblage composition with more open, generalized or radially accessible flowers. The first route reduces dependence on pollen transfer; the second reduces dependence on a narrow interaction architecture.

These routes are related but need not form one obligatory sequence. The selfing syndrome includes recurrent reductions in floral investment associated with transitions toward selfing (Sicard & Lenhard 2011), but reproductive assurance and floral generalization are not synonymous. Self-compatible species need not self autonomously, autonomous selfing need not dominate realized mating, and floral architecture may respond to interaction environments even without a change in breeding system. A macroecological island syndrome could therefore emerge from two partly independent pathways rather than from a single chain in which isolation first causes selfing and selfing subsequently causes floral simplification.

A second problem concerns the meaning of a community-level syndrome. Cross-sectional island data combine evolutionary change within lineages with ecological and biogeographic sorting among lineages. An isolated island with a high proportion of self-compatible or generalized flowers may contain lineages that evolved those states after colonization, or it may preferentially contain lineages that already possessed those states in the source region. Trait filtering in island floras is therefore simultaneously a problem of adaptation and community assembly (Schrader et al. 2024). Taxonomic structure is not merely a nuisance covariate: where a response disappears after family or genus adjustment can reveal the lineage depth at which assemblage differences are represented, although such attenuation does not identify the causal process.

A third problem is that geographic context can modify a recurrent global response without eliminating it. Source pools, climates, island histories and interaction communities differ among tropical, temperate and high-latitude regions. A strict universal-response hypothesis requiring identical multivariate slopes is therefore stronger than the biological proposition that isolation repeatedly shifts assemblages in a common direction. The important distinction is between **recurrence** and **equality**. A global island syndrome can recur while its magnitude and the allocation of response among component traits vary geographically.

Direct evidence for the proposed ecological pressure is also essential. Floral traits alone cannot demonstrate that pollination is limiting. We therefore use GloPL, a global database of pollen-supplementation experiments (Bennett et al. 2018), as an independent functional evidence layer. Pollen-supplementation experiments quantify the reproductive deficit attributable to insufficient natural pollen receipt. They do not identify whether that deficit arises from visitor abundance, visitation frequency, pollen quality, mate availability or other components of pollination service, but they provide a direct experimental measure of pollen limitation that is independent of the island floral-trait response.

Here we synthesize the now-frozen Chapter 1 evidence around one mechanistic hypothesis. **H1** asks whether a recurrent classic island-syndrome direction is detectable across four broad biogeographic contexts without requiring equal response vectors. **H2** asks whether experimental pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation globally. **H3** separates the plant response into reproductive-assurance and accessibility/generalization pathways and tests whether both recur across contexts. **H4** provides an explicitly post-hoc functional triangulation: using trait states frozen before the new analysis, do plants with reproductive-assurance or generally accessible states show lower current experimental pollen limitation after accounting for geography and measurement structure? **H5** asks how the recurrent assemblage response is taxonomically realized, distinguishing broad contemporary observed floras from the narrower source-backed native system in which source-matched assembly inference is defensible.

This architecture changes the interpretation of regional heterogeneity. Regional differences remain real and directly tested, but they become modifiers of a recurrent global response rather than evidence that one region possesses an island syndrome and another does not. It also changes the role of pollinator data. Named floral templates and GloBI interaction records are retained as supplementary compatibility evidence, but neither is required to establish a named pollinator mechanism. The central test is instead whether three independent evidence layers converge: isolation is associated with a recurrent plant syndrome, isolation is associated with experimental pollen limitation, and trait states expected to reduce dependence on successful pollination are associated with lower current pollen limitation.

## Materials and Methods

### Geographic universe and plant database

The plant analysis used a fixed geographic universe of 8,265 islands and a fixed denominator of 106,295 accepted angiosperm species. The final trait snapshot contained 222,688 resolved species-by-raw-axis cells among 318,885 possible cells (69.83%) across flower colour, floral structural complexity and reproductive assurance. Missing trait evidence was never converted to absence. Species-direct high/medium evidence was distinguished from validated lower-confidence evidence; the broader `all_analysis_eligible` scope was primary for the global contemporary-flora analysis and the high/medium `direct_only` scope was retained as an evidence sensitivity.

The primary contemporary-flora layer used all observed island occurrences rather than imposing native status as a prerequisite. This choice maximized a consistent global observation frame but changes the estimand: the result describes contemporary observed island-flora composition and cannot automatically be interpreted as native colonization or native assembly. Source-backed native and native-nonendemic strata, together with WCVP regional-native compatibility, were used separately to establish the floristic-status boundary.

### Geographic exposure and covariates

The primary plant exposure was log distance to the nearest continental source boundary. Models included log island area and four climate principal components and used spatial-block cluster-robust covariance. Distance is interpreted as a composite gradient of geographic separation, connectivity and source supply rather than as a pure causal treatment. No distance threshold or post-hoc near-versus-far island cutoff was introduced.

For GloPL, all valid georeferenced experimental sites were placed on a continuous distance axis to the same six seeded major continental landmasses used by the frozen Chapter 1 global-distance extension. Distances were log1p-transformed and standardized for analysis. Mainland sites were retained at zero distance in the primary model; an offshore-only shape analysis was post-hoc.

### H1: recurrent global island-syndrome direction

The all-observed primary response consisted of six atomic probability outcomes:

1. `generalized_form`;
2. `actinomorphic_symmetry`;
3. `shallow_open_tube`;
4. `self_compatibility`;
5. `selfing_mating_system`;
6. `autonomous_selfing`.

Each outcome was coded so that a positive isolation coefficient represents the classic island-syndrome direction: more generally accessible floral architecture or more reproductive assurance. Island-level trait counts were fitted with the frozen beta-binomial logit framework using distance, island area and climate PC1–PC4, with spatial-block cluster-robust covariance. A matched grouped-binomial analysis was retained as model-form sensitivity.

The v13 synthesis distinguishes recurrence from equality. Within each of four predeclared contexts—northern mid-latitude, northern high-latitude, tropical and southern extratropical—we use the already locked six-dimensional omnibus test to establish that the response vector differs from zero. We summarize orientation descriptively as the arithmetic mean of the six identically coded distance slopes. No new p-value is attached to that mean because the required full six-slope covariance is not used for this post-hoc scalar summary. A positive mean in all four regions, together with supported regional joint vectors, constitutes evidence for a recurrent direction. The previously frozen North–Tropical direct vector test remains the test of regional modification; equality of vectors is not required for H1.

### H2: global experimental pollen limitation

GloPL data were taken from the version-pinned public repository associated with Bennett et al. (2018), commit `abbe193770981b2c61713e75a0f01e004bdded8e`; the canonical `Output/GloPL.csv` file had SHA-256 `264ca8c2237f126b3c209fcba039e19b291324ddf6e7a96992dc8235a55984de`. The effect size is the log response ratio of pollen-supplemented to natural reproduction, so positive values indicate stronger pollen limitation.

The frozen full-global analysis contained 2,969 experimental rows, 1,248 sites and 919 publications. Duplicate measurements were summarized at publication-by-coordinate-by-measurement cells. Each publication received total analysis weight one, preventing heavily sampled publications from dominating. Models included context intercepts and fixed effects for GloPL effect-size type, supplementation definition, constant addition and supplementation level, with publication-cluster robust covariance. The primary hypothesis was a positive global coefficient of standardized log distance. Supplemental-only and no-zero-constant subsets were frozen sensitivities.

### H3: reproductive-assurance and floral-accessibility pathways

The six atomic responses were grouped into two biological families without changing their individual fits. The reproductive-assurance family comprised self-compatibility, selfing mating system and autonomous selfing. The floral-accessibility family comprised generalized form, actinomorphic symmetry and shallow/open tube. For v13, family means are descriptive summaries of already fitted identically oriented atomic coefficients, not newly fitted composite traits and not new inferential tests.

A separate frozen plant-side analysis had already evaluated whether the floral-accessibility response was reducible to measured reproductive assurance. `selfing_core` combined strict reproductive evidence while excluding floral-attraction traits. An attraction/accessibility shift contrasted generally accessible architecture against large-bee-like restricted architecture and was fitted with distance, selfing core and covariates. These models were interpreted as conditional decomposition rather than causal mediation. Their role in v13 is to establish that an obligatory `isolation -> selfing -> floral simplification` sequence is not required by the plant data.

### H4: post-hoc functional triangulation

The functional bridge was designed after the frozen Route A and Route B distance-by-trait moderation results had been inspected. It is therefore explicitly **post-hoc functional triangulation**, not a confirmatory mediation analysis. The design, code, parent artifacts and claim ceiling were frozen before the reproduction workflow was run.

The analysis reused the exact-species matched effect rows generated by the frozen reproductive-assurance and floral-architecture GloPL workflows. No synonym rescue, genus fallback, trait recoding or support-threshold relaxation was permitted. The evaluable pre-existing contrasts were self-compatibility, autonomous selfing, generalized floral form and actinomorphic symmetry; selfing mating system and shallow/open tube remained non-evaluable under their parent support gates.

For each trait separately, duplicate effect rows were aggregated at the publication-by-site-by-species-by-measurement cell. Publication total weight was again one. The primary model was

`PL ~ trait_state + z_distance + context_intercepts + measurement_fixed_effects`,

with publication-cluster robust covariance. A negative trait-state coefficient means that the reproductive-assurance or generally accessible state is associated with lower current experimental pollen limitation after adjustment for distance and the measured study structure.

Two measurement sensitivities repeated the model using only supplemental effect definitions and after excluding effects requiring a zero constant. Two additional checks exploited within-study contrasts. Publications, and then publication-by-site groups, were retained only when both trait states occurred. Outcomes and covariates were weighted-demeaned within the relevant fixed-effect group before fitting the remaining contrast with publication clustering. These checks reduce between-publication and between-site confounding but remain observational comparisons of trait states within an experimental database.

The earlier Route A/B moderation tests remain separate. They asked whether trait state changed the *slope* of pollen limitation with isolation and were not supported under their frozen family rules. v13 does not reclassify those failures.

### H5: taxonomic realization and floristic-status boundary

Taxonomic realization was evaluated in two evidence layers with different inferential meanings.

**H3A/broad contemporary flora** used source-free leave-one-species-out family and genus expectations on the same all-observed species support at observed, family-residual and genus-residual stages. This asks whether the broad contemporary-flora response is erased by raw taxonomic composition without asserting a historical source pool.

**H3B/defended native assembly** retained the previously frozen source-matched Palearctic native analysis. Observed, family-adjusted and source-matched genus-adjusted stages used common support. A matched-complexity randomization reassigned species to pseudo-genera within families while preserving the exact number and size distribution of genus groups. This tests whether true genus membership carries more attenuation information than an arbitrary equally fine grouping.

The floristic-status boundary was evaluated on the same 375 islands with source-supported status data by comparing all-observed and native-only fits. A separate WCVP sensitivity upgraded unresolved records only when the focal island mapped unambiguously to an extant non-doubtful regional native range. WCVP compatibility is not exact island-native proof.

### Supplementary pollination evidence

Named `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like` and `bird_like` scores are multivariate floral-architecture concordances, not realized pollinator identities. A source-trained common factor accounts for more than 86% of their sampled variance. GloBI effective functional-channel breadth is likewise retained only as supplementary evidence because it depends on documented interaction records and source definitions. Neither evidence family can identify global pollinator loss, abundance decline or effective service.

### Evidence roles and claim ceiling

The v13 paper combines evidence generated under different temporal roles. The original plant and GloPL global-distance models retain their frozen inferential roles. The v13 recurrence summaries re-express locked coefficients, and H4 is explicitly post-hoc. No analysis is interpreted as proving historical mediation from pollen limitation to trait evolution. The strongest allowed conclusion is triangulation among a recurrent geographic plant pattern, an independent experimental pollen-limitation gradient, and functional associations of pre-existing trait states with current pollen limitation.

## Results

### H1: a recurrent island-syndrome direction occurs in all four broad contexts

The six-atomic isolation-response vector was supported within every broad biogeographic context in both evidence scopes. In the primary all-analysis scope, omnibus p-values were `1.06×10^-6` for northern mid-latitude islands, `1.10×10^-6` for northern high-latitude islands, `1.56×10^-5` for tropical islands and `2.09×10^-13` for southern extratropical islands. Direct-only evidence retained all four responses (`p=0.00231`, `1.79×10^-9`, `2.83×10^-5` and `1.14×10^-20`, respectively).

The direction of those vectors was recurrent rather than regionally exclusive. Under the common classic-positive coding, the descriptive mean of the six atomic slopes was positive in all four regions: `0.0195`, `0.1256`, `0.1324` and `0.0832` from northern mid-latitude through southern extratropical contexts. Direct-only means were similarly positive (`0.0228`, `0.1299`, `0.1113`, `0.0779`). In the primary scope, five of six atomic slopes were positive in northern mid-latitudes, all six in northern high latitudes and the tropics, and five of six in southern extratropical islands. Direct-only evidence showed six, six, five and five positive slopes, respectively.

Recurrence did not imply equal response vectors. The pre-existing formal North–Tropical six-atomic difference remained strongly supported across 3,626 primary analysis islands (`p=0.000763`) and 3,569 Direct-only islands (`p=0.00450`). The correct global description is therefore **a recurrent classic island-syndrome direction with biogeographic modification of strength and component allocation**, rather than either a single identical global vector or a syndrome confined to one region.

### H2: experimental pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation

The independent GloPL analysis supported a global increase in experimental pollen limitation with distance from major continental landmasses. Across 2,969 experimental rows, 1,248 sites and 919 publications, the standardized distance coefficient was `0.07937` (SE `0.03773`; two-sided `p=0.03543`, one-sided positive `p=0.01772`). The no-zero-constant sensitivity retained a positive supported coefficient (`0.07789`, one-sided `p=0.02095`); the supplemental-only estimate was smaller but remained positive (`0.03349`).

A post-hoc shape audit showed that the signal was not explained solely by a mainland-versus-offshore level shift. The estimated mainland-to-average-offshore step was positive but imprecise (`0.13745`, `p=0.1084`), whereas the continuous gradient among sampled offshore sites was positive (`0.19983`, `p=0.01110`), as was the offshore-only fit (`0.23545`, `p=0.005865`; 260 sites, 158 publications). These shape analyses are descriptive and do not alter the confirmatory status of the parent global-distance result.

The old prediction that pollen-limitation increase should be stronger in northern mid-latitudes than in the tropics was not supported. The northern point estimate was `0.06170`, while the implied tropical estimate was `0.20470`. Thus GloPL is more consistent with a shared global pollination constraint than with the earlier North-only service-loss story.

### H3: reproductive assurance and floral accessibility form two recurrent response pathways

Both plant-response families pointed in the classic direction in every broad context. For reproductive assurance, descriptive mean slopes in the primary scope were `0.0190`, `0.0730`, `0.1838` and `0.1490` across northern mid-latitude, northern high-latitude, tropical and southern extratropical contexts. Direct-only values were `0.0298`, `0.1003`, `0.1309` and `0.0967`. Thus reproductive-assurance composition increased with isolation across all four contexts even though individual components varied in magnitude and precision.

The accessibility/generalization family also had positive mean slopes in all four contexts: `0.0200`, `0.1783`, `0.0810` and `0.0175` in the primary scope, and `0.0158`, `0.1596`, `0.0916` and `0.0590` in Direct-only evidence. The family does not imply universal movement of every structural trait. For example, shallow/open tube depth was negative in the southern extratropical primary fit and near zero or negative in some Direct-only contexts. The recurrent signal instead reflects the multivariate tendency toward greater access and generalization, particularly generalized form and actinomorphy.

The two routes were not reducible to one obligatory sequence. The previously frozen conditional analyses retained a distance-associated attraction/accessibility component after accounting for `selfing_core`, and the distance-by-selfing interaction did not support a model in which accessibility shift occurs only where reproductive assurance is high. Thus the plant data are more consistent with partially separable reproductive-assurance and floral-accessibility responses than with compulsory `isolation -> selfing -> floral simplification`.

### H4: current pollen limitation is lower in key island-syndrome trait states

The reproduced post-hoc functional analysis yielded four evaluable frozen trait contrasts, three of which had negative directional primary associations at `p<=0.05`. Their robustness differed substantially.

Autonomous selfing provided the strongest functional bridge. Species coded as autonomous or delayed selfers had substantially lower current experimental pollen limitation after adjustment for distance, context and measurement definition (`β=-0.44672`, SE `0.08025`, two-sided `p=2.60×10^-8`). The association remained negative in both global measurement sensitivities: supplemental-only `β=-0.30372` (one-sided negative `p=2.69×10^-6`) and no-zero-constant `β=-0.45480` (`p=9.18×10^-9`). It also persisted within publications containing both trait states (`β=-0.30603`, two-sided `p=0.0295`; 30 mixed publications) and within publication-by-site groups (`β=-0.18636`, one-sided negative `p=0.0417`; 36 mixed groups). This result directly links a reproductive-assurance phenotype to reduced current pollen limitation without relying solely on between-study geography.

Self-compatibility pointed in the same direction but was imprecise (`β=-0.11773`, two-sided `p=0.154`; directional `p=0.0768`). Its measurement sensitivities remained negative but unsupported, and within-publication designs were rank deficient. Selfing mating system remained non-evaluable because the frozen parent overlap contained insufficient protected-state offshore support.

Floral architecture provided weaker but concordant functional evidence. Actinomorphic species had lower pollen limitation in the primary model (`β=-0.38119`, `p=1.18×10^-5`) and both measurement sensitivities retained negative directional associations. Within-publication (`β=-0.12190`) and within-publication-by-site (`β=-0.09712`) estimates remained negative but were imprecise. Generalized floral form was also negative in the primary model (`β=-0.18410`, `p=0.0449`) and in the no-zero-constant sensitivity, but the supplemental-only sensitivity was not supported and only seven mixed publications and nine mixed publication-site groups were available. Shallow/open tube depth remained non-evaluable under its frozen parent support gate.

These results do not overturn the negative moderation results. The predeclared Route A and Route B analyses had asked whether reproductive-assurance or accessibility states specifically weakened the *increase of pollen limitation with isolation*; neither family met its promotion rule. H4 instead shows that several frozen trait states are functionally associated with the level of current pollen limitation, with the clearest and most internally controlled evidence for autonomous selfing.

### H5: the recurrent syndrome has different taxonomic realization across flora layers

The broad all-observed pattern was not erased by source-free taxonomic residualization. Restricting the analysis to species with common family/genus support first reproduced the broad North–Tropical response (`p=8.39×10^-5` primary; `p=1.21×10^-4` Direct-only). After source-free genus residualization, equal-island tests remained supported (`p=0.01150` primary; `p=0.00459` Direct-only). Generalized form and self-compatibility retained detectable post-genus components. Thus the broad contemporary-flora response cannot be summarized as a raw genus-composition artifact.

The separately defended native Palearctic system behaved differently. Under source-matched decomposition the response was supported at the observed stage and after family adjustment but failed the predeclared vector gate after genus adjustment in every focal evidence-scope-by-stratum profile (`4/4 -> 4/4 -> 0/4`). A matched-complexity null showed that real genus boundaries produced stronger conditional attenuation than arbitrary within-family partitions preserving identical genus-group complexity (`p=0.02899`). The exact incremental family-to-genus attenuation remained spatially imprecise in the earlier bootstrap analysis, so genus structure is real but not a precisely located causal breakpoint.

The distinction between these flora layers is not merely sample size. On exactly the same 375 islands with source-status support, the all-observed North–Tropical contrast remained strong (`p=0.0005607` primary; `p=0.0003959` Direct-only), whereas the source-backed native restriction did not (`p=0.2491`; `p=0.3018`). Expanding native compatibility using WCVP produced 2,372 islands but still did not restore the broad contrast (`p=0.07954`; Direct `p=0.17472`). The global contemporary syndrome therefore cannot be promoted wholesale to a native island-assembly syndrome.

### Supplementary interaction evidence does not change the mechanism claim

GloBI documented interaction-channel breadth covered 3,252 islands and showed context heterogeneity in three of four frozen source definitions, but the result was source-definition sensitive. Given known recording-effort dependence and the user's decision to treat this evidence conservatively, GloBI remains supplementary. It is not required for the v13 mechanistic synthesis and does not identify effective service or pollinator loss.

Named large-bee-like, butterfly-like and bird-like plant templates were likewise not used as pollinator observations. More than 86% of their sampled variance was shared architecture, so simultaneous movement of several named templates is interpreted as a structural floral shift rather than evidence of simultaneous changes in the named visitor guilds.

## Discussion

### A global island syndrome can recur without being identical everywhere

The central change in v13 is conceptual but follows directly from the accumulated evidence. Earlier versions posed a strict universal vector against regional branching and concluded that a universal syndrome failed. That statement was statistically correct for equality of multivariate responses, but it was biologically stronger than necessary. The current six-atomic results show that every broad context has a supported isolation response and that the average direction of the identically coded traits is positive in every context and both evidence scopes. What varies geographically is the strength and allocation of that response among components.

This distinction resolves an apparent contradiction. Tropical islands, for example, do not lack an island syndrome. In the current all-observed analysis they show some of the strongest increases in selfing mating system and self-compatibility and positive increases in generalized form and actinomorphy. The tropical response differs from northern mid-latitudes, but it differs around a shared direction rather than reversing the entire syndrome. Regional heterogeneity is therefore biologically important as a modifier, not as the defining evidence for syndrome presence or absence.

The result also avoids treating a syndrome as a rigid checklist. Shallow floral access is not uniformly positive, and some reproductive components are weak or even slightly negative in individual contexts and scopes. A macroecological syndrome is better understood as recurrent multivariate orientation than as a requirement that every constituent trait change significantly in every region.

### Pollen limitation supplies an independent global ecological pressure

The GloPL result gives the plant pattern an independent ecological counterpart. Geographic separation is associated with stronger experimental pollen limitation across the full global sampling frame, and the positive relationship continues among sampled offshore sites. Pollen limitation is therefore not inferred from floral morphology; it is measured by reproductive response to pollen supplementation.

This supports the broad idea that successful pollination becomes more limiting with isolation, but the mechanism should be described carefully. Pollen limitation can arise because pollinator abundance is low, visitation is infrequent, visitor identity is mismatched, pollen transfer is inefficient, compatible mates are scarce, or these factors covary. The current analysis does not identify which component declines. “Pollination-service constraint” is therefore more defensible than “global pollinator decline.”

The lack of the old North-greater-than-Tropical service pattern is informative. If anything, the tropical GloPL point estimate is larger. This matches the revised plant interpretation: the tropics also exhibit a strong island-syndrome direction. The shared global pressure does not require one particular pollinator guild to be missing in all regions.

### Reproductive assurance is the strongest functional bridge

The post-hoc functional triangulation is most compelling for autonomous selfing. Autonomous selfing is precisely the form of reproductive assurance expected to reduce the reproductive consequences of failed pollen delivery, and species possessing autonomous or delayed selfing show substantially lower experimental pollen limitation. The association persists after global geography and measurement adjustment, through two measurement sensitivities, and, importantly, within publications and within publication-by-site groups containing both trait states.

This does not prove that historical pollen limitation selected autonomous selfing on islands. Current trait state and current pollen limitation can be related for several reasons, including evolutionary response, pre-existing trait differences, ecological sorting and unmeasured lineage covariates. Indeed, successful reproductive assurance can erase the very pollen limitation that historically favoured it, so present-day cross-sectional associations need not reproduce the historical selection gradient. The appropriate inference is functional: autonomous selfing is associated with reduced realized pollen limitation in the independent experimental database, making it a biologically credible route through which plants can cope with a global isolation-associated pollination constraint.

Self-compatibility alone is a weaker bridge, which is biologically reasonable. Self-compatibility permits self-fertilization but does not guarantee autonomous pollen transfer; a self-compatible species can remain strongly dependent on visitors. The imprecise self-compatibility result and the strong autonomous-selfing result therefore reinforce the distinction between compatibility and actual reproductive assurance.

### Floral accessibility is a second, more cautiously supported route

The floral-accessibility pathway is independent in logic from reproductive assurance. Open or generalized forms and actinomorphy can make flowers accessible to a wider set of visitors or reduce dependence on tightly matched interaction morphology. Globally, actinomorphy and generalized form are associated with lower current pollen limitation after adjusting for distance and measurement structure. The actinomorphy result is retained in both global sensitivities, whereas generalized form is less stable.

These architecture results are not as strong as the autonomous-selfing bridge. Within-publication and within-site contrasts are sparse for generalized form and imprecise for actinomorphy. Moreover, lower pollen limitation in an accessible architecture does not by itself show that isolation selected that architecture. Floral form covaries with lineage, life history, habitat and many other properties. We therefore interpret the architecture result as functional concordance with the second route, not as causal proof.

The plant-side conditional analysis nevertheless matters: accessibility/generalization persists as a distance-associated component after accounting for strict reproductive assurance, and the distance-by-selfing interaction does not require accessibility change to occur only in highly selfing assemblages. Together, the plant and GloPL evidence support a **dual-pathway model** more strongly than a compulsory one-dimensional selfing syndrome.

### Why failed slope moderation does not contradict the functional bridge

The frozen Route A/B moderation tests asked a different question from v13 H4. They predicted that reproductive assurance or accessible architecture would specifically flatten the positive relationship between isolation and pollen limitation. That interaction was not supported. The new result asks whether, at comparable geography and measurement structure, the protected trait state is associated with a lower level of pollen limitation.

Both statements can be true. A trait can reduce pollen limitation across the sampled gradient without changing how the residual limitation scales with geographic distance. Conversely, an evolutionary response to past pollen limitation can itself reduce current limitation, weakening an interaction expected under a static exposure-response model. For transparency, the moderation failures remain in the paper as negative results; they are not “rescued” by the post-hoc analysis.

### Taxonomic assembly changes the realization, not the existence, of the syndrome

The taxonomic analyses show why one global syndrome need not have one global assembly mechanism. In broad contemporary floras, source-free genus residualization does not erase the context response. In the defended native Palearctic system, source-matched genus adjustment removes the multivariate support, and real genera attenuate the response more than equally complex pseudo-genera. These are different findings because they concern different flora layers and different source assumptions.

The all-observed layer includes native, introduced and unresolved occurrences and is therefore the appropriate layer for describing contemporary global composition, not historical native assembly. The defended Palearctic layer makes much stronger source and status assumptions and supports a narrower assembly inference. The status comparison on the same islands confirms that these estimands cannot be collapsed: the broad contemporary signal is strong where the native-only North–Tropical contrast is not.

Thus taxonomic assembly belongs downstream of the global syndrome statement. It asks **how the response is represented**, not whether the syndrome exists. In some systems the response can be strongly carried by which genera assemble; in the broad contemporary layer, substantial response remains beyond raw genus composition. Neither result by itself distinguishes ecological sorting from within-lineage evolutionary change.

### Pollination syndromes are useful as trait geometry, not visitor labels

The revised framing also clarifies the role of classical pollination syndromes. The study does not require flowers to be assigned to a realized “bee,” “butterfly” or “bird” pollinator. Such labels are especially risky at global scale because colour, form, symmetry and tube depth overlap among functional groups and because the named template scores share most of their variance.

What remains useful is the underlying trait geometry. Restricted, deep or bilaterally specialized architectures can represent greater dependence on particular visitor access, whereas open, radial and generalized architectures represent broader accessibility. This is the level at which pollination-syndrome theory supports v13: multivariate floral architecture is biologically meaningful, but phenotype is not a direct census of the visitor community (Fenster et al. 2004; Rosas-Guerrero et al. 2014).

GloBI is similarly demoted to a supplementary role. Its interaction records are valuable for generating candidate hypotheses, but documented breadth depends on study effort and source definition. The current source-sensitive context heterogeneity does not carry the paper's mechanism claim.

### Limits of causal inference

The main limitation is temporal. The data triangulate three contemporaneous relationships: isolation with island-flora composition, isolation with experimental pollen limitation, and trait state with current pollen limitation. They do not directly observe the historical sequence from pollination constraint through selection to trait evolution. The word “aligns” in the title is deliberate.

A second limitation is floristic status. The strongest global syndrome is an all-observed contemporary-flora result and weakens under current native-status restrictions. Introduced and unresolved records may themselves contain ecologically meaningful signals of establishment under human transport and modern environmental filtering, but they cannot be interpreted as natural native colonization without additional evidence.

A third limitation is uneven trait and experimental coverage. Trait coverage reached 69.83% across the three raw axes, and some key GloPL trait contrasts remain support-limited. Selfing mating system and shallow/open tube depth could not be evaluated in the functional bridge. Architecture within-study contrasts were sparse. These are identification boundaries, not biological nulls.

Finally, global distance is a composite exposure. It covaries with source separation, connectivity, colonization opportunity and potentially many environmental and historical factors. Models adjust for island area and climate in the plant analysis and for broad context and measurement structure in the GloPL analysis, but residual confounding remains possible. A causal test of evolutionary sequence requires repeated lineage-level or local-system measurements of pollinator service, reproductive success and trait variation.

### A unified interpretation of the floral island syndrome

Despite those limitations, the evidence now supports a simpler and more general synthesis than the earlier region-first story. Island isolation is associated with a recurrent global direction toward greater reproductive assurance and greater floral accessibility. Independent experiments show that pollen limitation increases with isolation. The functional trait analysis shows that the clearest reproductive-assurance state—autonomous selfing—is associated with substantially lower current pollen limitation, with weaker but concordant evidence for generally accessible floral architecture. These links make pollination constraint a plausible common ecological pressure without requiring identical trait coefficients, identical pollinator communities or identical taxonomic assembly in every region.

The syndrome is therefore best viewed as an **emergent solution space**. A common ecological challenge can be met by more than one plant strategy. Reproductive assurance reduces dependence on pollen delivery. Generalized floral accessibility may reduce dependence on narrowly matched visitor access. Different regions and source pools can distribute response across those strategies differently, and taxonomic assembly can determine which strategies are represented. The recurrence lies in direction; contingency lies in implementation.

### Conclusion

Across thousands of contemporary island floras, increasing geographic separation is associated with a recurrent floral/reproductive island-syndrome direction rather than a syndrome restricted to one biogeographic region. Independent pollen-supplementation experiments show that pollen limitation also increases with geographic isolation. The plant response contains at least two partially separable components—reproductive assurance and floral accessibility/generalization—and post-hoc functional triangulation provides the strongest bridge for autonomous selfing, which is associated with markedly lower current experimental pollen limitation even within studies.

The global syndrome is not equivalent to one global assembly process. Broad contemporary-flora context dependence remains after source-free genus residualization, whereas a defended native Palearctic response is strongly structured by source-matched genus composition. Regional differences and lineage assembly therefore modify how a common response is realized rather than determining whether the response exists.

These results support a unified pollination-constraint hypothesis while preserving a strict causal boundary. They show convergence among geography, experimental reproductive limitation and functional trait states, but they do not identify historical pollen limitation as the cause of the observed trait evolution. The next decisive step is longitudinal or lineage-resolved evidence linking pollination service, reproductive success and trait change through time.

## Claim ceiling for v13

The manuscript may state that geographic isolation is associated with a recurrent global classic island-syndrome direction across the four broad contemporary-flora contexts, while the exact response vectors differ geographically. It may state that experimental pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation globally. It may state that reproductive assurance and floral accessibility/generalization are partially separable plant-response pathways. It may state that, in an explicitly post-hoc exact-species triangulation, autonomous selfing is robustly associated with lower current experimental pollen limitation and that actinomorphy and generalized floral form provide additional global functional concordance of differing robustness. It may state that broad contemporary and defended native flora layers have different taxonomic realization.

The manuscript must not state that historical pollen limitation is proven to have selected the observed traits, that pollen limitation statistically mediates the global syndrome, that pollinator abundance or visitation globally declines with isolation, that a named pollinator group caused the pattern, that GloBI identifies the causal mechanism, that all-observed flora are equivalent to native assembly, that taxonomic attenuation identifies dispersal or pollination as the cause, that regional response vectors are equal, or that the current global data distinguish species sorting from within-lineage evolution.
