## Why pollination-syndrome matching must remain weak

Traditional syndromes are multivariate functional hypotheses, not deterministic labels.

Ollerton et al. (2009) found that the closest idealized syndrome predicted the major pollinator for only about 30% of tested species across six communities, rising to about 35% when the first or second closest syndrome was accepted. Rosas-Guerrero et al. (2014), using effective rather than merely frequent pollinators across 417 species, found substantially more support for pollination syndromes, especially for pollinator-dependent species and tropical plants. Dellinger (2020) reconciled these views by emphasizing scale, empirical pollinator observations, and richer multivariate trait sets; colour alone is less reliable than traits such as reward and corolla dimensions.

Therefore this chapter uses syndrome literature only to evaluate **partial concordance and mismatch of an already frozen multivariate direction**.

## 1. Bombus / large- or long-tongued-bee interpretation

### Direct Izu evidence

Inoue & Amano (1986) reported a transition from *Bombus diversus* on adjacent Honshu, to *B. ardens* plus halictids on Oshima, to halictid-dominated pollination on Niijima, Kozushima and Hachijo. Island *Campanula punctata* flowers were smaller than mainland flowers, and breeding-system differences included increasing self-compatibility and potential autogamy in the more insular populations.

Inoue (1988) strengthened this pattern with bagging experiments: most Honshu and Oshima plants were self-incompatible, whereas almost all plants on the other sampled Izu islands were self-compatible. This became the explicit **bumblebee-absence hypothesis**: reduced pollination efficiency after bumblebee loss could favour reproductive-system change.

Hiraiwa & Ushimaru (2024) provide a more general functional interpretation. Across island coastal networks, low pollinator functional diversity — specifically low abundance of long-tongued pollinators — reduced plant–pollinator trait matching, functional floral-niche partitioning and community-level plant functional specialization, with negative consequences for pollination success. Pollinator species diversity itself was not the key predictor.

### Verdict

> **The current global northern-midlatitude result is not a demonstrated Bombus-loss syndrome.**

There is **partial concordance only at the broad functional level**: isolation is associated with floral-architecture reorganization, and independent Izu work shows that loss of long-tongued pollinator function can reorganize trait matching and functional specialization. However, the global trait directions do not reproduce the central Izu breeding-system prediction (`SC` increase) or a simple generalization axis. The simultaneous increase of `spurred` / `funnel_trumpet` states and decrease of other specialized forms is better described as **architectural turnover / component-specific reorganization** than as uniform loss of specialization.

This is exactly the point that should be handed to Chapter 2: pollinator identity or functional diversity may alter different floral components through different response branches rather than through one syndrome trajectory.

## 2. Bird-associated interpretation

Traditional ornithophilous descriptions often combine red/orange colour, tubular flowers, abundant nectar, weak scent, and floral architectures that improve bird contact while sometimes excluding bees. Experimental work in *Penstemon* shows that transitions to hummingbird pollination can involve narrower corolla tubes, reduced lower lips, exserted sexual organs and other coordinated “pro-bird” / “anti-bee” modifications rather than a single colour change (Castellanos et al. 2004).

The frozen Chapter 1 data do **not** provide the necessary tropical/southern counter-vector for a bird interpretation:

- formal joint regional heterogeneity is not confirmatory;
- no FDR-supported confirmatory tropical/southern atomic vector opposes the northern vector;
- nectar amount, scent, organ exsertion and several other high-information bird-syndrome traits are not present in the frozen primary comparison.

Therefore:

> **Bird-mediated functional replacement is not supported or refuted by Chapter 1. It remains a Chapter 2 / future-system hypothesis, not a Chapter 1 explanation.**

## 3. Butterfly / Lepidoptera-associated interpretation

Traditional psychophily is itself heterogeneous. Common descriptions include conspicuous colours, landing surfaces, and nectar hidden in long/narrow tubes or spurs. For example, butterfly-pollinated systems may combine red/pink/mauve colours with flat landing surfaces and long tubes/spurs, while individual clades can show distinct solutions. Empirical studies also show that butterfly pollination may involve trumpet-shaped flowers, narrow tubes, or pollen transfer by wings rather than the proboscis.

Some northern-midlatitude atomic responses (`spurred` +, `funnel_trumpet` +) overlap superficially with traits that can occur in butterfly-associated systems. But `red_pink` moves downward, and the response occurs in the northern vector rather than as a supported tropical/southern counter-vector. These mixed directions demonstrate why floral categories cannot be converted directly into a Lepidoptera label.

Therefore:

> **The frozen data do not support a butterfly counter-syndrome or functional-replacement claim.**

The appropriate Discussion use is to note that some individual architectures are compatible with multiple long-tongued functional groups, reinforcing the need for direct functional evidence in Chapter 2.

## 4. Synthesis for the manuscript Discussion

### Supported statement

A defensible interpretation is:

> **Isolation-associated floral change in the global dataset is concentrated in particular architectural and colour components rather than in a coherent classical reproductive/floral syndrome. The concentration of strong signals in northern mid-latitude islands is broadly compatible with independent evidence that island pollinator functional structure — especially the availability of long-tongued pollinators — can alter floral trait matching and specialization, but the present trait vector neither reproduces a simple bumblebee-absence syndrome nor identifies an alternative bird or Lepidoptera syndrome.**

### Stronger statements that are not allowed

Do not write:

- “Bombus loss caused the northern island syndrome.”
- “Northern islands evolved generalized flowers because bumblebees disappeared.”
- “Tropical islands retain specialized flowers because birds or butterflies replace bumblebees.”
- “The decline in `red_pink` identifies the pollinator guild.”
- “`spurred` or `funnel_trumpet` flowers prove butterfly pollination.”

## 5. Chapter 2 handoff

The post-freeze concordance audit strengthens the Chapter 2 question:

> **Why does a change in pollination environment produce increases in some floral architectures, decreases in others, and little robust change in broad reproductive-assurance/generalization summaries?**

Chapter 2 should discriminate among:

- pollinator taxonomic identity;
- functional tongue/body-size structure;
- trait matching;
- effective pollen transfer;
- reproductive dependency / assurance;
- interaction-network state;
- functional replacement; and
- non-pollination biogeographic/history effects.

The Chapter 1 evidence alone cannot choose among them.
