# PR138 Nearctic wave-1: source inventory for vascular-plant native/introduced status

Date checked: 2026-08-26

## Outcome

The 15 coordinate-to-island identifications were verified, but source quality is highly uneven. Two islands have a current or original island-specific checklist that explicitly records origin status: **San Nicolas Island** (best source) and **Isla de Cedros** (introduced taxa are marked, but only as “presumably introduced”). **Saint-Pierre Island** has a promising two-source route: an original archipelago flora with island localities plus VASCAN's explicit Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon origin assertions. For the five Alaskan islands, AKEPIC can support only **positive introduced records whose coordinates fall inside the island polygon**; it cannot support native classifications. No complete, machine-readable, island-specific origin checklist was verified for the remaining islands.

This is a source inventory, not a completed status crosswalk. A species occurrence is never converted to `native` merely because it appears in an island flora, herbarium database, or survey.

## Admission rule

1. `introduced` may be assigned when an island checklist explicitly says non-native/introduced, or when an exact island occurrence is joined to an authoritative regional source that explicitly says the taxon is non-native to the whole containing region.
2. `native` requires an explicit native assertion applicable to the island, or an explicitly documented exhaustive checklist convention whose unmarked class is native and whose published category totals can be reproduced.
3. A regional `native` assertion is not automatically an island-native assertion: the regional native taxon could still be introduced to a particular island.
4. Negative searches, absence from AKEPIC, and `Presence = 0` survey rows are sampling information, not biological absence and not evidence of native status.
5. Ambiguous labels such as “possibly introduced”, “presumably introduced”, or “both native and non-native strains” remain separately flagged; they are not silently collapsed into high-confidence binary status.

## Shared machine-readable sources: what they can and cannot do

### AKEPIC (Popof, Nunivak, Baranof, St. Matthew, Umnak)

ACCS states that its non-native list is botanist-maintained and that nativity decisions are based on early floras, herbaria, and current literature. Therefore a **positive** AKEPIC record inside an island polygon can support an introduced assignment. Use the [FeatureServer](https://services7.arcgis.com/DlE4MSUhoXlyq64t/arcgis/rest/services/AKEPIC_Data/FeatureServer) with geometry, positive occurrence/presence filters, pagination, and coordinate QA; do not use island-name substring matching alone.

The separate USFWS dataset [*Alaska island invasive plant survey data for AKEPIC, July 2024*](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/alaska-island-invasive-plant-survey-data-for-alaska-exotic-plants-information-clearin-2024) is a first-party open CSV ([direct download](https://iris.fws.gov/APPS/ServCat/DownloadFile/270484?Reference=179555)). The CSV was directly inspected. Its surveyed islands are Adak, Aghiyuk, Aiktak, Caton, Chernabura/Chernaburan, Chirikof, Chowiet, Great Sitkin, Long, Outer Iliasik, Poperechnoi, Rootok, Sedanka, Simeonof, Ugamak, Uganik, Ukolnoi, Unalaska, Unalga, Village, and Wosnesenski. **None of the five PR138 wave-1 Alaskan islands is included.** Consequently neither its positive nor negative rows can be applied to those five islands.

### VASCAN (Campobello and Saint-Pierre)

VASCAN's [API documentation](https://data.canadensys.net/vascan/api) shows that taxonomic assertions include `locality`, `establishmentMeans`, and `occurrenceStatus`. Use `NB` for New Brunswick and `SM` for Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon. The API can reconcile current names and synonyms in batches of 200. These are regional assertions: `introduced` can support the introduced subset when exact-island occurrence is independently established, whereas `native` is not automatically island-native.

### Greenland DwC-A (Disko)

The [source archive](https://danbif.au.dk/ipt/archive.do?r=flora-of-greenland-checklist) is preferable to scraping GBIF pages. Join `taxon.txt` to `distribution.txt` by `id`; retain `Non-native`, `Native`, and `Both non-native and native strains` as three distinct source values. The archive is Greenland-wide and historically based on the 1978 flora, so it should be versioned and cited as Jacobsen et al. (2020), DOI [10.15468/r9efxw](https://doi.org/10.15468/r9efxw).

## Acquisition order

1. **San Nicolas:** parse CCH2 `clid=25` first. It is current, island-specific, synonym-rich, and status-explicit. Validate source category totals before assigning unannotated taxa as native.
2. **Cedros:** retain the Appendix 2 parser but change the claim ceiling: `*` = `presumably_introduced`; unstarred is unresolved unless independently supported. Add historical-to-current synonym resolution.
3. **Saint-Pierre:** acquire/cache the Le Hors PDF, OCR its island-locality and origin phrases, and reconcile names through VASCAN `SM`.
4. **Alaska and Disko introduced subsets:** spatially intersect AKEPIC positive records with each exact Alaska polygon; join exact Disko occurrences to Greenland `Non-native`. Never fill the residual as native.
5. **Longboat, Lasqueti, Horn, Campobello, Peck Beach, Block Island:** extract exact-island/reserve occurrence lists where available, but admit only explicit origin assertions. Keep all other status rows unresolved while seeking a modern island checklist or the inaccessible original chapter/thesis.

## Claim ceiling

This search does **not** justify a complete native/introduced partition for all 15 islands. It identifies two high-value island checklists, one promising historical-plus-regional route, several defensible introduced-only routes, and explicit evidence gaps. Those gaps must remain missing data; they cannot be repaired by coding occurrence as native, AKEPIC non-detection as absence, or a regional native label as island-native.
