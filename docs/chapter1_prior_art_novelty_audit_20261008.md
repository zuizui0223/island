## Executive decision

The defensible novelty is **not** that isolation distance has never been used for plant reproductive traits, that self-compatibility is enriched on islands, or that autofertile/generalist flowers show lower pollen limitation.

The paper's distinctive **combination of estimands** is:

1. within an assembled, contemporary island flora, test **seven separate reproductive, colour, and architecture associations** with corrected *island-to-continental-coast* distance in four regions, including broad flora, regional-native-compatible, and Direct-only scopes;
2. test whether measured reproductive assurance statistically absorbs isolation-associated floral accessibility, without claiming mediation;
3. independently relate the *continental-source distance of experimental sites* to GloPL pollen-limitation responses, including an offshore-only sensitivity;
4. associate exact-matched species-level reproductive-assurance/accessibility scores with present-day pollen limitation **as post-hoc functional compatibility**, not evidence that pollen limitation historically caused island trait composition.

The novelty is **cross-domain geographic integration of assemblage patterns and experimental constraint**, rather than a new individual ecological relationship.

## Actual result and novelty ceilings

### H1: repeatability versus heterogeneity

- 8,264 island units is the *candidate universe*; the broad H1 fitted union is 4,379 islands. The plant database contains 106,295 accepted angiosperms, but not all appear in each model.
- Self-compatibility's association with isolation is positive and nominally significant in all four broad All regions and all four WCVP regional-native-compatible All regions.
- Seven traits × four regions are tested with **pointwise, unadjusted** finite-cluster t inference after retrospective specification. No family-wise proof that reproductive function is statistically more recurrent than floral morphology is provided merely by counting significant tests.
- A substantial majority of broad flora occurrences have unresolved origin. WCVP provides **regional native compatibility**, not exact island nativeness; the result concerns *contemporary assemblage sorting* and cannot identify in-situ evolutionary changes.

**Defensible**: isolation-associated reproductive-trait recurrence is more geographically consistent *in these observed traitwise results* than colour/structural associations.  
**Not defensible**: first demonstration of island reproductive filtering, a universally significant multivariate syndrome, or repeated within-lineage adaptation.

### H2: conditional independence is not mediation

- Selfing-adjusted accessibility is FDR-supported in northern high latitude and tropical All. Tropical Direct-only is nominal but not FDR-supported. Complete-three-component Direct sensitivity retains positive isolation coefficients in those two regions.
- A residual isolation coefficient is compatible with unmeasured selfing, source-pool/lineage assembly, region-specific climate, or pollinator-related accessibility; it does not identify direct selection by pollinators.

**Defensible**: reproductive assurance alone does not *statistically absorb* every accessibility–isolation association.  
**Not defensible**: proof of a second independent causal pathway.

### H3: strongest candidate for an individually distinctive test

- Experimental GloPL site-to-coastline distance associates positively with pollen limitation: primary corrected beta +0.09191 (finite-publication P = 0.01594).
- In the **post-hoc offshore-only** sensitivity, beta +0.22031 (P = 0.02459); the binary mainland/offshore indicator is unsupported.
- This differs from species-specific range-edge position and from prior land-use/pollinator-specialization questions.
- Crucially, H3 and H1 assess **parallel geographic gradients**, not the exact same islands and sampled species; phylogenetic composition, site choice, intervention type, geography and measurement definitions remain alternative explanations. Supplemental-only H3 is unsupported (P = 0.31082).

**Defensible**: a positive experimental pollen-limitation association with a source-coast isolation gradient in this versioned dataset, with meaningful sensitivity limits.  
**Not defensible**: isolation demonstrably *causes* pollen shortfall or that pollen limitation monotonically increases on every remote island.

### H4: existing biology, new bridge

- Exact-match Direct H2 scores: `selfing_core` beta -0.29830 (455 matched species, finite-publication P = 0.00417); `generalized_accessible` beta -0.29566 (143 species, P = 0.02334).
- Negative effects are directionally consistent with earlier GloPL analyses of autofertility and floral generalization (Burns 2019; Bennett 2020). H3 and H4 use overlapping source experimental data; they are **not two independent experimental replications**.
- It is a post-hoc cross-data triangulation; earlier prespecified interaction/moderation tests did not establish buffering of the **isolation-to-pollen-limitation slope** by these traits.

**Defensible**: functional compatibility connects island-associated trait scores with current experimental pollen limitation.  
**Not defensible**: demonstration that those traits mediated island selection or reduced the slope of pollen limitation with distance.

## The manuscript's defensible English claim

> Previous research established self-compatibility and mutualistic dependence as filters of island colonization and linked reproductive and floral traits to pollen limitation. We ask a different, cross-domain question: along a common geographic axis of continental isolation, do established island floras show recurrent reproductive function without a uniform floral phenotype, and does independently observed pollen-supplementation evidence reveal a parallel reproductive constraint? Traitwise regional comparisons, conditional floral-access analyses and an exact-species functional bridge support this constraint–response alignment, but they do not identify historical mediation or a universal pollinator mechanism.

**Editorial differentiation**: not “the first island gradient” or “the first functional pollen-limit result”, but “a comparable geographical exposure applied to assembled plant reproductive phenotypes and independently measured pollen limitation, with explicit separation of recurrence, phenotype and functional compatibility.”

## Submission implications

1. Put Grossenbacher (2017), Razanajatovo (2019), Zell (2025) and Delavaux (2024) together in the first background argument, so the manuscript does not pretend colonization or mutualism filters are new.
2. Cite Burns (2019) and Bennett (2020) near H4, explicitly stating that lower pollen limitation for autofertile/generalist plants is prior art.
3. State that island–continental distance is not Zell's species-to-island arrival-opportunity metric or Dawson-Glass's range-edge metric.
4. Avoid “independent H3–H4 validation”: the *plant flora and GloPL* are separate evidence sources, but H3/H4 reuse GloPL. Do not claim paired island-site experimental follow-up.
5. Do not promote any >783 km far-tail flower architecture to a pollination mechanism; that post-hoc signal was superseded by PRs #311/#313/#314 and is outside the frozen inference.
