## Panel A — global contemporary flora

Primary scope: all observed flora.

1. H1 rejects one universal six-atomic response across contexts.
2. H2 identifies biogeographic branching, with the predeclared North-Tropical contrast supported across thousands of islands.
3. H3A decomposes that same broad H2 response without a native source pool. The exact beta-binomial H2 contrast survives restriction to the family/genus-LOO common species support, and the North-Tropical residual remains supported after both family and genus residualization. Therefore the broad H2 contrast is not erased by raw genus composition.
4. H4 area moderation remains a modifier whose biological capacity interpretation is measurement-sensitive.
5. H5 independent pollination evidence is suggestive in some contexts but does not identify one source-definition-robust global pollinator mechanism.

Panel A is observational. It does not identify native colonisation filtering, historical source ancestry, introduction effects or in-situ evolution.

## Panel B — defended native assembly

The separately defended native result is narrower.

The strongest frozen result is the Palearctic native response. Under source-matched taxonomic decomposition it survives family adjustment and disappears after genus adjustment. Matched-complexity pseudo-genus randomization shows that real genus structure exceeds an arbitrary equally fine grouping, while the exact family-to-genus increment remains spatially imprecise.

Panel B therefore supports strong genus structuring of a defended native response, not a global native North-Tropical mechanism.

## Relationship between panels

The panels are nested by defensibility, not consecutive steps in one causal ladder.

The new H3A result resolves the previously missing cell:

| flora layer | broad/context response | taxonomic-depth result |
|---|---|---|
| all observed | supported | persists after source-free genus residualization |
| defended native Palearctic | supported locally | strongly attenuated after source-matched genus adjustment |

Thus H3B cannot be used as an explanation of the broad all-observed H2 response. The two layers have different taxonomic behavior. Their comparison is itself a result.
