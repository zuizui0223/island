# Chapter 1 v13 — unified island-syndrome hypothesis

Date: 2026-09-17

Canonical paper lock: `config/chapter1_v13_unified_island_syndrome_result_lock.json`

## Central hypothesis

Geographic isolation is associated with a recurrent global floral and reproductive island-syndrome direction because successful pollination becomes more limiting as connection to major continental landmasses declines. The plant response has two partially separable routes:

1. a **reproductive-assurance route**, in which the value of self-compatibility and autonomous or realized selfing increases when successful outcross pollination is unreliable; and
2. an **accessibility/generalization route**, in which dependence on restricted or specialized floral access becomes less advantageous, shifting assemblage composition toward more generally accessible floral architecture.

These routes can coexist without forming one obligatory sequence. In particular, a floral-accessibility response need not be a downstream consequence of selfing alone.

The arrows from isolation to experimental pollen limitation and from isolation to plant composition are directly supported associations. The link from trait state to current experimental pollen limitation is post-hoc functional triangulation, strongest for autonomous selfing. The historical sequence `past pollen limitation -> selection -> trait evolution` is not directly identified.

## H1 — Global recurrent island syndrome

**Question.** Do island floras across broad biogeographic contexts repeatedly move in the classic island-syndrome direction as geographic separation increases?

**Prediction.** The six-atomic multivariate response is non-zero within each broad context and the descriptive average direction is positive under the same classic coding: greater generalized/accessibile floral architecture and greater reproductive assurance.

**Result.** Supported as a recurrent direction, not as equality of vectors. The six-atomic joint response is supported in all four broad contexts in both all-analysis and Direct-only evidence. The descriptive mean classic slope is positive in all four contexts in both scopes. The formal North–Tropical vector difference remains supported, so regional context modifies strength and component allocation rather than determining whether a syndrome exists at all.

**Claim.** A recurrent global floral/reproductive island-syndrome direction is present in contemporary observed island floras; response vectors are not identical among regions.

## H2 — Global pollination constraint

**Question.** Is geographic isolation independently associated with stronger experimental pollen limitation?

**Prediction.** GloPL effect size increases with distance from the seeded major continental landmasses.

**Result.** Supported. Across 2,969 experiments, 1,248 sites and 919 publications, the standardized global distance coefficient is `+0.07937` (SE `0.03773`; two-sided `p=0.0354`, one-sided positive `p=0.0177`). The sign is positive in both frozen measurement sensitivities. A post-hoc shape audit also finds a positive gradient within sampled offshore sites.

**Claim.** Experimental pollen limitation is a supported global correlate of geographic isolation. This is not a causal island-treatment effect and does not identify pollinator abundance or visitation decline.

## H3 — Dual plant-response pathways

### H3a Reproductive assurance

**Prediction.** Greater isolation is associated with more self-compatible, selfing-capable or autonomous-selfing composition.

**Result.** The reproductive-assurance family mean is positive in all four contexts in both evidence scopes. Individual atomic responses differ in precision and strength, so the family is interpreted as a recurrent direction rather than a requirement that every trait be positive in every context.

### H3b Accessibility/generalization

**Prediction.** Greater isolation is associated with more generally accessible floral architecture: more generalized/open forms, more actinomorphy, and, where supported, shallower/open access.

**Result.** The accessibility/generalization family mean is positive in all four contexts in both evidence scopes. The shallow/open-tube component is not universally positive, so the supported global statement concerns a multivariate accessibility/generalization direction rather than universal simplification of every floral structure.

### H3c Partial independence

The frozen plant-side analysis rejects an obligatory `isolation -> selfing -> floral simplification` sequence. Reproductive assurance and accessibility/generalization are therefore treated as partially separable response pathways.

## H4 — Functional compatibility with pollen limitation

**Status.** Post-hoc functional triangulation. This hypothesis was formalized after the frozen Route A/B distance-by-trait moderation results were inspected and is never labelled confirmatory.

**Question.** Are the already frozen reproductive-assurance and generally accessible floral states associated with lower current experimental pollen limitation after adjusting for distance, context, measurement definition and publication weighting?

**Result.** The strongest bridge is autonomous selfing: `beta=-0.4467`, two-sided `p=2.60e-08`, with the negative association retained in supplemental-only and no-zero-constant sensitivities and within publications (`beta=-0.3060`, `p=0.0295`). Within publication×site groups the estimate remains negative (`beta=-0.1864`; one-sided negative `p=0.0417`). Actinomorphy also has a strong negative global association with both global measurement sensitivities; generalized floral form is negative in the primary model but less robust; self-compatibility is negative but imprecise.

**Interpretation.** Frozen trait states expected to reduce dependence on successful pollinator service are associated with lower current pollen limitation, most clearly for autonomous selfing. This closes a functional-compatibility link but not the historical causal link from pollen limitation to trait evolution.

**Negative result retained.** The predeclared Route A/B tests asking whether those states buffer the *distance slope* of pollen limitation remain unsupported. v13 does not reclassify them.

## H5 — Taxonomic realization

**Question.** At what taxonomic layer is the recurrent assemblage response represented, and does that answer remain the same across flora definitions?

**Broad contemporary flora.** On common H3A support, the all-observed North–Tropical response remains after source-free genus residualization (`p=0.01150` all-analysis; `p=0.00459` Direct-only). Broad contemporary context dependence is therefore not erased by raw genus composition.

**Defended native assembly.** In the source-backed native Palearctic system, the response follows `4/4 -> 4/4 -> 0/4` from observed to family-adjusted to source-matched genus-adjusted stages. Real genus structure exceeds matched arbitrary within-family grouping complexity (`p=0.02899`).

**Status boundary.** On the same 375 status-supported islands, the all-observed North–Tropical result is strong (`p=0.0005607`; Direct `0.0003959`) while the source-backed native restriction is not (`p=0.2491`; Direct `0.3018`). WCVP regional-native-compatible expansion likewise does not restore the broad contrast.

**Claim.** Taxonomic structure determines how the syndrome is realized in different flora layers. H3A and H3B are not one causal sequence, and genus attenuation does not identify dispersal, pollination or within-lineage evolution as the cause.

## Role of regional variation

Regional variation is retained as a modifier. The formal North–Tropical six-atomic vector difference remains supported, but this no longer means that one region has an island syndrome and another lacks it. Instead, regions differ in the magnitude and allocation of a recurrent global response among floral and reproductive components.

## Role of pollination syndromes

Predeclared `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like` and `bird_like` scores remain compatibility layers only. Because more than 86% of their sampled variance is shared architecture, named-template changes cannot be read as realized pollinator identity. The stronger v13 use of pollination-syndrome theory is therefore at the trait-combination level: restricted/specialized floral architectures versus generally accessible architectures, alongside a separately defined selfing syndrome.

## Role of GloBI

GloBI is supplementary only. Its documented functional-channel breadth is strongly affected by interaction-recording effort and source definitions. The current `3/4` source-mode context heterogeneity is therefore retained as sampling-sensitive context evidence, not as a mechanism needed by the v13 argument.

## Claim ceiling

v13 may state that:

- isolation is associated with a recurrent global island-syndrome direction;
- experimental pollen limitation increases with isolation globally;
- reproductive assurance and floral accessibility/generalization form partially separable plant-response pathways;
- frozen functional trait states are associated with current experimental pollen limitation, with the strongest post-hoc bridge for autonomous selfing;
- taxonomic realization differs between broad contemporary and defended native flora layers.

v13 may **not** state that:

- past pollen limitation is proven to have caused the observed trait evolution;
- pollen limitation statistically mediates the global island syndrome;
- global pollinator abundance or visitation is proven to decline with island isolation;
- any named pollinator group caused the global pattern;
- GloBI identifies the causal pollinator mechanism;
- all-observed flora represent native island assembly;
- genus attenuation identifies the causal process;
- regional response vectors are equal;
- the current cross-sectional data distinguish species sorting from within-lineage evolution globally.
