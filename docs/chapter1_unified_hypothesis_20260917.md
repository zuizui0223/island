## Central hypothesis

Geographic isolation is associated globally with stronger experimental pollen limitation and with a recurrent **functional floral/reproductive island-syndrome core**, but recurrence at that functional level does not require identical trait vectors or a single universal pollinator mechanism.

The plant response is organized into three partially separable phenotype modules:

1. a **reproductive-assurance module**, in which self-compatibility, autonomous selfing and realized selfing can reduce dependence on successful outcross pollen delivery;
2. an **accessibility/generalization module**, in which more open, generalized and radially accessible floral architectures reduce dependence on narrowly matched visitor access; and
3. a **pollinator-facing display module**, in which raw flower-colour composition and the coupling between colour and floral architecture can be reorganized with isolation in a context-dependent way.

The first two modules form the frozen recurrent H1 functional core. The third is a reproduced H3 extension from the final raw-colour evidence and is **not** retroactively inserted as a seventh confirmatory H1 atomic trait.

The central biological claim is therefore **recurrent but non-uniform response**: geographic isolation is associated with a common functional shift in reproductive assurance and floral accessibility, while the detailed organization of pollinator-facing floral display can differ among ecological contexts.

The associations from isolation to pollen limitation and from isolation to the frozen six-trait plant response are directly supported by frozen parent analyses. The raw-colour extension independently shows that pollinator-facing display contains isolation-associated structure not reducible to measured reproductive assurance. The link between frozen trait state and current pollen limitation remains **post-hoc functional triangulation**, strongest for autonomous selfing. The historical sequence `past pollen limitation -> selection -> trait evolution` is not directly identified.

## H1 — Global recurrent functional island syndrome

**Question.** Does the classic floral/reproductive island-syndrome direction recur across geographically distinct island-flora strata?

**Prediction.** The frozen six-atomic multivariate response is non-zero in each of four predeclared geographic replication strata and the descriptive average direction is positive under the same coding: greater floral accessibility/generalization and greater reproductive assurance.

**Result.** Supported. The six-atomic joint response is supported in all four strata in both the primary and Direct-only evidence scopes. The descriptive mean classic slope is also positive in all four strata in both scopes.

**Claim.** A recurrent global **functional core** is present in contemporary observed island floras. The four geographic strata serve as replication of recurrence. Recurrence does not require every atomic trait, colour state or colour–architecture combination to move identically.

## H2 — Global pollination constraint

**Question.** Is geographic isolation independently associated with stronger experimental pollen limitation?

**Prediction.** GloPL effect size increases with distance from major continental landmasses.

**Result.** Supported. Across 2,969 experiments, 1,248 sites and 919 publications, the standardized global distance coefficient is `+0.07937` (SE `0.03773`; two-sided `p=0.0354`, one-sided positive `p=0.0177`). The sign is positive in both frozen measurement sensitivities, and a post-hoc shape audit is also positive among sampled offshore sites.

**Claim.** Experimental pollen limitation is a supported global correlate of geographic isolation. This does not identify a causal island treatment or a global decline in pollinator abundance or visitation.

## H3 — Partially separable plant-response modules

### H3a Reproductive assurance

**Prediction.** Greater isolation is associated with greater representation of self-compatible, selfing-capable or autonomous-selfing states.

**Result.** The reproductive-assurance family mean is positive in all four geographic replication strata in both evidence scopes. Individual atomic traits differ in magnitude and precision.

**Interpretation.** This is the reproductive-insurance component of the island syndrome: a plant can reduce dependence on successful outcross pollen delivery without requiring a particular floral-display change.

### H3b Accessibility/generalization

**Prediction.** Greater isolation is associated with more generally accessible floral architecture.

**Result.** The accessibility/generalization family mean is positive in all four geographic replication strata in both evidence scopes. Shallow/open tube depth is not universally positive, so the supported statement concerns multivariate accessibility/generalization rather than universal simplification of every floral character.

**Interpretation.** This is a pollinator-access component that can reduce dependence on narrowly matched visitor access and is not treated as a necessary downstream consequence of selfing.

### H3c Pollinator-facing display reorganization

**Question.** Does the visual/display component of flowers also change with isolation after measured reproductive assurance is conditioned out, and do raw colour–architecture associations themselves change?

**Prediction.** If floral display is an additional pollinator-facing response dimension rather than a by-product of selfing alone, at least some raw colour-composition or colour-conditioned architecture responses should persist after `selfing_core` adjustment. Their detailed directions need not be globally identical.

**Result.** Supported as an extended H3 evidence layer.

- **Northern mid-latitude:** `red_pink` declines in both evidence scopes after `selfing_core` adjustment (`q=0.0111` all; `q=0.0312` direct). No named colour-conditioned architecture survives FDR, so this is a robust colour-composition shift without a supported guild assignment.
- **Northern high latitude:** the five-colour vector itself is not supported, but `blue_purple × butterfly-associated form` and `blue_purple × intermediate/deep large-bee-associated tube` both decline strongly with isolation in all and Direct-only evidence. This is consistent with erosion of specialized colour–architecture coupling, not proof of loss of a realized pollinator guild.
- **Tropical:** Direct evidence supports `yellow_orange × deep butterfly-associated tube` increasing with isolation (`q=0.0355`), consistent with retention or strengthening of a long-tongued-insect-associated structural component rather than universal simplification.
- **Southern extratropical:** all-analysis shows mixed yellow/orange restructuring: bird-associated form declines while intermediate/deep tube representation increases. Direct-only evidence does not retain these after FDR, so a coherent named bird syndrome is not supported.

**Interpretation.** Isolation is associated with reorganization of **pollinator-facing floral display composition and, in some contexts, colour–architecture coupling**. The data reject a simple universal rule such as “remote islands become white” or “one pollination syndrome replaces another everywhere.”

### H3d Partial independence and hierarchy

The plant-side evidence does not require an obligatory `isolation -> selfing -> floral simplification` sequence. Reproductive assurance, accessibility/generalization and pollinator-facing display are therefore treated as partially separable phenotype modules.

The evidence hierarchy is explicit:

1. the recurrent six-trait functional core is frozen primary H1 evidence;
2. raw flower colour is a reproduced extended H3 response family;
3. colour-conditioned architecture tests whether trait coupling changes beyond marginal colour frequency;
4. named pollination syndromes remain compatibility interpretations, not realized pollinator identities.

## H4 — Functional compatibility with pollen limitation

**Status.** Post-hoc functional triangulation. This question was formalized after the frozen Route A/B distance-by-trait moderation results were inspected and is never labelled confirmatory.

**Question.** Are already frozen reproductive-assurance and generally accessible floral states associated with lower current experimental pollen limitation after adjustment for distance, broad context, measurement definition and publication weighting?

**Result.** The strongest bridge is autonomous selfing: `beta=-0.4467`, two-sided `p=2.60e-08`, with the negative association retained in supplemental-only and no-zero-constant sensitivities and within publications (`beta=-0.3060`, `p=0.0295`). Within publication-by-site groups the estimate remains negative (`beta=-0.1864`; one-sided negative `p=0.0417`). Actinomorphy also has a strong negative global association with both global measurement sensitivities; generalized floral form is negative in the primary model but less robust; self-compatibility is negative but imprecise.

**Interpretation.** Frozen trait states expected to reduce dependence on successful pollen transfer are associated with lower current pollen limitation, most clearly for autonomous selfing. This supplies functional compatibility, not historical causal mediation.

**Negative result retained.** The predeclared Route A/B tests asking whether those states buffer the *distance slope* of pollen limitation remain unsupported.

## Role of geographic replication strata

Northern mid-latitude, northern high-latitude, tropical and southern extratropical island floras retain two distinct roles.

For H1, they are replication strata asking whether the same classic-positive **functional core** recurs in geographically distinct data. A between-stratum difference is not a primary H1 submission claim.

For the extended H3 display layer, the observed component pattern is intentionally allowed to differ among contexts. This is not used to rank regions or infer realized pollinator identities. Instead it establishes that a recurrent functional island syndrome can coexist with non-identical pollinator-facing phenotype organization.

## Role of pollination syndromes

Predeclared `large_bee_like`, `butterfly_like` and `bird_like` scores remain compatibility layers only. Because much of their sampled variance is shared floral architecture, named-template changes cannot be read as realized pollinator identity.

The stronger pollination-syndrome use is now at the **raw trait-combination level**:

- colour is retained as reported raw states rather than collapsed to one conspicuous/plain score;
- floral form and tube depth are retained as raw architecture states;
- colour-conditioned coupling asks whether architecture changes among species retaining the same focal colour;
- named syndrome labels are used only to describe concordance between prespecified trait combinations and classical pollination-associated architectures.

Thus the paper can discuss erosion, retention or restructuring of **syndrome-like trait coupling** without claiming that colour uniquely identifies bees, butterflies or birds.

## Chapter 1 / Chapter 2 programme bridge

Chapter 1 now establishes two things simultaneously:

1. a recurrent global functional response to isolation and pollination constraint; and
2. non-uniform organization of pollinator-facing display among ecological contexts.

This creates the mechanistic question for Chapter 2 (`izu-core`): **why can a broad ecological constraint recur while detailed response trajectories and apparent determinant rankings differ among systems?**

The programme-level handoff is:

```text
Chapter 1: what recurs?
    isolation -> pollen limitation + recurrent functional core
                         |
                         +-> context-dependent display/coupling

Chapter 2: why need responses not be identical?
    receiving functional state + interaction regime + response geometry
                         |
                         v
              different response branches / determinant rankings
```

This is a conceptual bridge, not cross-repository causal validation. Chapter 1 does not use Chapter 2 theory to prove a mechanism, and Chapter 2 does not convert the Chapter 1 geographic associations into historical causal effects.

## Role of GloBI

GloBI is supplementary only. Documented functional-channel breadth is affected by interaction-recording effort and source definitions. It is retained as sensitivity evidence, not as a mechanism required by the v13 argument.

## Upgraded claim ceiling

v13 may state that:

- isolation is associated with a recurrent global **functional** island-syndrome core;
- experimental pollen limitation increases with isolation globally;
- reproductive assurance and floral accessibility/generalization form partially separable recurrent response modules;
- raw flower-colour composition contains additional isolation-associated structure that is not reducible to measured reproductive assurance;
- some colour–architecture couplings also change with isolation after conditioning on the focal colour itself and on `selfing_core`;
- the pollinator-facing display response is context dependent, so global functional recurrence does not imply identical detailed phenotype vectors;
- frozen functional trait states are associated with current experimental pollen limitation, with the strongest post-hoc bridge for autonomous selfing.

v13 may **not** state that:

- past pollen limitation is proven to have caused the observed trait evolution;
- pollen limitation statistically mediates the global island syndrome;
- global pollinator abundance or visitation is proven to decline with island isolation;
- any named pollinator group caused the global pattern;
- flower colour uniquely identifies a realized pollinator;
- reduced attraction investment, pigment concentration, animal-visual contrast or UV signalling were directly measured;
- GloBI identifies the causal pollinator mechanism;
- response vectors are identical across geographic strata;
- the current cross-sectional data distinguish species sorting from within-lineage evolution globally;
- the Chapter 2 transportability framework by itself proves the mechanism behind Chapter 1 geographic differences.
