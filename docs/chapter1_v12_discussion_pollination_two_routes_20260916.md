# Chapter 1 v12 — manuscript-ready Discussion replacement for pollination and two pathways

Status: candidate prose for the exploratory v12 branch. This does **not** overwrite the frozen v11 manuscript.

## Why one floral island syndrome fragments into two plant response components

The broad island pattern is inconsistent with a compulsory serial model in which isolation first increases selfing and floral simplification follows automatically. Reproductive assurance and pollinator-facing floral architecture are statistically separable components of the response. In the defended Palearctic analyses, the attraction/accessibility shift remains associated with isolation after conditioning on the measured `selfing_core` across four frozen source definitions (conditional distance estimates approximately 0.091–0.101; q=0.0079–0.034). The tropical branch provides a complementary counterexample: reproductive assurance can increase while accessibility/generalization does not move in the same simplifying direction. Thus reproductive assurance is one plausible response to unreliable pollen delivery, but it is not sufficient to account for all isolation-associated floral change.

This distinction changes how the classical selfing-syndrome argument should be used. A reproductive-assurance route can reduce dependence on outcross pollen delivery and thereby lower the fitness value of some attraction investments, but that is not the only route available to an island flora. Floral architecture can also respond to the identity, continuity and effectiveness of animal pollination opportunities. The present data therefore support two partially separable plant-side components—reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture—without establishing which upstream ecological process generates either component.

## Regional floral responses are consistent with pollination syndromes, but syndrome labels are not visitor observations

The predeclared syndrome templates provide a biological interpretation of the regional floral vectors. In northern mid-latitude native assemblages, large-bee-like architecture declines with isolation. In tropical assemblages, butterfly-like architecture increases with isolation, and warm colours are disproportionately coupled to tubular floral forms relative to northern mid-latitudes. These combinations are qualitatively consistent with different pollination-associated architectures being maintained or filtered in different biogeographic settings rather than with one universal movement toward floral simplification.

However, the named templates cannot be treated as realized visitor classifications. More than 86% of the covariance among large-bee-like, butterfly-like and bird-like scores is captured by a shared floral-architecture factor. A flower can therefore score highly on multiple named templates for the same structural reasons. The appropriate inference is that isolation is associated with different **pollination-associated floral architectures** among contexts, not that the analysis has identified which pollinator visited each species or island.

## Total pollinator-channel attrition does not explain the two plant pathways

We tested the upstream pollination hypothesis with independent exact-island occurrence data rather than floral phenotype. The canonical Search data distinguish strict, effort-qualified retention from disruption for five functional channels: Bombus, non-Bombus bees, Lepidoptera, flower-visiting birds and Diptera. Missing records under insufficient survey effort remain unresolved rather than being interpreted as biological absence. This distinction is essential because the global occurrence archive contains far more detections than effort-qualified non-detections.

No single functional channel provided adequate retained-versus-disrupted overlap in both northern-midlatitude and tropical contexts. Pooling the five channels solved that support problem. Under the frozen primary rule, the pooled comparison contained 303 northern-midlatitude islands (15 with at least one documented disruption) and 99 tropical islands (21 disrupted). Nevertheless, the predicted two-route response was absent. Functional-channel disruption was not associated with higher `selfing_core` in either context (North: beta=-0.0113, p=0.738; Tropical: beta=+0.0588, p=0.536), and generalized accessibility did not increase after conditioning on `selfing_core` (North: beta=-0.00496, p=0.834; Tropical: beta=-0.00863, p=0.897). The four primary tests had FDR q approximately 0.897.

This result rejects promotion of a simple global mechanism in which the loss of any source-available pollinator channel has the same consequence for reproductive assurance and floral accessibility. It does not show that pollinators are unimportant. Channel presence is a coarse state: effective service can decline through abundance, visitation frequency, pollen deposition or single-visit effectiveness while the functional channel remains detectable, and different channels can compensate for one another.

## Functional identity matters more than a simple count of lost channels, but the current signal is not robust enough for mechanism identification

Because the pooled test assumes interchangeability among visitor guilds, we next retained functional identity in an island-by-channel diagnostic. Every island received equal total weight across its evaluable channel rows, channel-by-context fixed effects absorbed static differences among visitor guilds and regions, and the same geographic and climatic controls were retained. For the floral route, disruption was matched to the corresponding predeclared architecture: Bombus to large-bee-like, Lepidoptera to butterfly-like, and flower-visiting birds to bird-like. This test therefore asks a more specific question than whether flowers simply become more generalized when any pollinator channel is lost.

The identity-aware analysis produced the strongest upstream hint in the tropical branch. In direct High/Medium data, channel disruption was associated with higher reproductive assurance (beta=+0.2198, p=0.0153), but the effect narrowly missed the four-test FDR family (q=0.0611) and was not reproduced in the broader evidence sensitivity (beta=+0.0740, p=0.542). The channel-matched floral response also pointed in the predicted tropical direction after conditioning on `selfing_core` (beta=-0.1382, p=0.122; all-analysis beta=-0.1207, p=0.162), but uncertainty remained too large for support. Northern effects were unsupported and the matched-floral coefficient pointed in the opposite direction.

The biological interpretation is therefore narrower than a pollinator-loss mechanism. Total functional-channel attrition is too coarse to explain the two plant response components, while preserving functional identity reveals a **suggestive tropical pattern** that does not survive the full inferential gate. This asymmetry is consistent with the broader Chapter 1 result: the ecological meaning of isolation depends on biogeographic context. It is not evidence that the same channel-disruption process operates globally.

## The remaining mechanistic target is pollination-service limitation, not channel presence alone

The upstream quantity most directly relevant to floral fitness is not whether a taxonomic channel can still be detected on an island, but the service that channel provides. A more resolved mechanism would require at least four linked quantities: pollinator abundance, visitation frequency, pollen delivery and effectiveness per visit. Functional identity and turnover would then determine which floral architectures are favoured, while compensation among channels would determine whether loss of one visitor class actually reduces total service. None of those quantities is recovered by the present global exact-island occurrence state.

This clarifies the role of H5. Chapter 1 does not need a positive pollinator mechanism to sustain its main result. It establishes that reproductive assurance and floral architecture are partially separable, that regional floral combinations are compatible with different pollination-associated architectures, that simple global interaction-breadth and total-channel-loss explanations fail, and that an identity-aware tropical signal remains suggestive but not identified. The causal arrow from pollination-service limitation to either plant pathway remains open rather than being inferred from floral traits.

## Relation to taxonomic assembly

The pollination result also needs to be read alongside the taxonomic-depth contrast. The broad contemporary-flora North–Tropical response is not erased by source-free genus residualization, whereas the narrower defended Palearctic native response loses support after source-matched genus adjustment. Pollination can therefore influence the visible island syndrome through more than one biological level: local trait change, species sorting and lineage assembly are all logically possible. In the defended Palearctic layer, much of the response is represented in real genus composition; in the broad all-observed layer, raw genus composition does not exhaust the context difference. Neither result licenses a within-lineage evolutionary interpretation by itself.

The combined lesson is that mechanism and representation depth must be separated. A pollination-related filter could alter which lineages assemble, while pollination-service limitation could also alter floral traits within retained lineages. The present data identify the representation structure more strongly than the causal route.

## Revised H5 conclusion for the manuscript

> Island isolation is associated with two partially separable plant response components: reproductive assurance and pollination-associated floral architecture. Regional floral combinations are consistent with different pollination syndromes, but the named templates do not identify realized visitors. Independent exact-island data reject a simple total pollinator-channel-attrition explanation; retaining functional identity reveals a suggestive tropical reproductive-assurance association and a directionally compatible matched-floral response, but neither survives the full inferential gate. The common upstream cause therefore remains unidentified, with effective pollination-service limitation—not coarse channel presence—the next mechanistic target.
