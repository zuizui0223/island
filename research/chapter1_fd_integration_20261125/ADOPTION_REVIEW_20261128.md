## Proposed abstract refinement (author review; not the rendered manuscript)

**Aim:** To test whether reproductive traits recur more consistently than floral architectures along continental-isolation gradients, and whether these patterns are functionally consistent with pollen limitation.

**Location:** Global islands and continental/offshore experimental sites.

**Time period:** Contemporary observed floras and published pollen-supplementation experiments.

**Major taxa studied:** Angiosperms.

**Methods:** A project-assembled database of 106,295 species and 8,264 island units supported seven traitwise analyses across 4,379 sampled islands, controlling for area, climate and spatial clustering. Reproductive-conditioned floral models and an exploratory paired symmetry–depth analysis tested whether additional floral responses could be reduced to measured reproductive assurance. Independent published pollen-addition experiments tested a complementary constraint gradient and exact-species functional associations.

**Results:** Self-compatibility increased with isolation in all four regions, including a regional-native-compatible sensitivity. Floral responses varied across regions and attributes. In 257 recorded tropical floras, radial prevalence increased more strongly among deep than shallow/open flowers (paired difference 0.02762, Holm p=0.000221); several atomic reproductive adjustments retained the contrast, but High-only did not reproduce it and literal-native support was insufficient. Pollen limitation increased with isolation across 2,969 experimental rows (beta=0.09191, p=0.01594). Exact-species reproductive-assurance and accessibility scores were negatively associated with current pollen limitation; both remained negative under strict atomic evidence sensitivity.

**Main conclusions:** Recurrent reproductive assurance and contingent floral architecture are distinguishable components of island trait composition. The data are consistent with a reproductive constraint but do not establish historical selection, a universal floral syndrome or pollinator replacement.
