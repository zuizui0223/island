**Why this paper matters to GEB readers (under 250 words):**

Island biogeography predicts recurrent reproductive filtering, but does not require a single floral phenotype or uniform functional-diversity loss. Using a 106,295-species trait database, we compared seven reproductive and floral traits across 4,379 recorded island floras in four regions. Self-compatibility increased with isolation in every region, including a regional-native-compatible sensitivity; other traits varied. Matched candidate-mainland comparisons distinguished compatibility from mating-system selfing and revealed architecture-specific composition, including tropical radial–deep combinations. Reproductive-adjusted floral associations, categorical diversity and rare-profile representation depended on the trait combination, taxonomic representation and comparison pool. Held-out genus information improved composition prediction but incompletely recovered geographic responses. Importantly, several stricter genus-null, native and evidence-grade checks failed, delimiting rather than obscuring the ecological interpretation. Independent published pollen-addition data showed an isolation-associated reproductive constraint, while post-hoc exact-species comparisons linked reproductive assurance and accessibility to lower current pollen limitation. We distinguish the recurrence of reproductive traits from contingent functional assembly, without interpreting comparative associations as historical selection or pollinator replacement.
