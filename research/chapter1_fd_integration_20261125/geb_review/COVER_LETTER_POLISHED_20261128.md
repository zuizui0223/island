**Why this paper matters to GEB readers (under 250 words):**

Islands offer geographically replicated comparisons of the constraints associated with reproductive assurance and floral composition. A single floral island syndrome, however, may obscure differences between reproductive traits and the floral attributes that mediate interactions with visitors. Drawing on a 106,295-species trait database, we analysed seven reproductive and floral traits across 4,379 sampled islands in four regions. Self-compatibility increased with isolation in every region, including a regional-native-compatible sensitivity, whereas other reproductive traits, colour and floral structure showed less uniform associations. Some accessibility associations persisted after reproductive adjustment. An exploratory paired-trait analysis of 257 tropical floras further showed that symmetry and tube depth need not change together: held-out congener profiles improved composition prediction but recovered little of the isolation-associated contrast. A separate compilation of 2,969 experimental pollen-limitation rows showed a positive association with isolation, including within offshore sites. Post-hoc exact-species comparisons linked reproductive-assurance and accessibility scores to lower current pollen limitation. These complementary analyses distinguish recurrent reproductive traits from contingent floral phenotypes and show why an apparent floral syndrome should not be interpreted as a single evolutionary response. They identify comparative associations rather than historical selection or causal mediation.
