Subject: Pre-submission guidance on reproducibility and third-party plant-trait data

Dear Global Ecology and Biogeography Editorial Office,

We are preparing a Research Article on recurrent reproductive assurance and geographically contingent floral composition across island plant assemblages. We would appreciate guidance on the data-sharing requirements before submitting.

Our frozen trait analysis uses 222,688 resolved species-by-trait-axis cells from heterogeneous botanical, literature and trait sources. A separately deposited rights-filtered derivative contains 46,274 cells (doi:10.5281/zenodo.22704973). The remaining 176,414 cells include source-rights review requirements and one unresolved semantic review; we do not claim that every remaining record is legally restricted. The public derivative contains only 1,382 of the 48,497 reproductive-assurance cells and is not a valid substitute for the complete analysis input.

We have prepared the manuscript, 14 aggregate result tables and an executable aggregate consistency check, including the original and exploratory analysis outputs. This permits checking reported numerical relationships, not refitting the original models from observations. Exact available input archives and source-level provenance are preserved in authorized private storage. Some historical native-range replay dependencies are not currently available. We are reviewing source-specific permissions and preparing a versioned analysis-code and reconstruction package; the permanent software/results archive DOI and anonymous reviewer-access mechanism have not yet been approved.

Could you advise whether GEB would consider a documented source-specific access arrangement for records that cannot lawfully be redistributed, and what evidence of licence restrictions, reconstruction instructions, code, aggregate results and editor/reviewer access would be required at initial submission? Would a pre-submission editorial assessment of such an arrangement be appropriate while the remaining rights-review records are resolved? We will not represent the public derivative as the full analysis input or offer unauthorized reviewer access.

Thank you for your guidance.
