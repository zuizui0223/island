## The novel question: does external-region GENUS actually beat a genus-BLIND source baseline?

The previous verified analysis asked whether a LOCAL region genus-conditioned predictor outperformed OTHER-region genus information. Different question: after **actually removing every plant species recorded in GIFT island floras of the focal physical 10° block and/or identical first potential mainland source entity FROM TRAINING**, does the OTHER-region genus-conditional model outperform its **externally region-exclusive GENUS-BLIND category prior**, both learned from precisely the geographically admissible source data?

All original donors are accepted species documented only in the required source region, with common across-region accepted names excluded from local and external sources; every focal island's complete accepted species names are also excluded. For each focal same botanical genus, donor name count is equal on both local/external sides, deterministic outcome-blind sample up to 20; all genus priors and the global category-blind source prior are **refitted after source group exclusion**. An original strict source gain is per-plant proper multiclass Brier or log-loss improvement of EXTERNAL same-genus over EXTERNAL genus-blind source, scored against identical accepted Direct original focal plants with matched-genera support. **The focal island's genus composition is GIVEN** to the model: this is conditional predictive transport of genus-associated floral trait states, NOT prospective colonization or a newly measured ancestral mainland phenotype.

The three mutually predeclared model source-training bans are (i) original co-block islands, (ii) original same first GIFT candidate mainland-source group, (iii) the union of both. Missing original source group identity or insufficient target/source species is recorded as *not identifiable*, never reverted to the old in-sample genus model. Original 10° physical-block and first GIFT source-group equally weighted, 499-sample source-group exploratory resampling uncertainty are calculated **separately** on the identical target cohort.

## What this DOES and DOES NOT establish for macroecology

**Supported botanical generality:** despite strong plant species turnover, observed categorically documented floral characteristics of different species belonging to the SAME genus remain useful for conditional predictive transfer to GIFT island floras in widely different geographic macroregions even after source-original stringent locality/source-group TRAINING exclusions. The seven-endpoint, all-three-source-ban result improves upon a Brier-only or simple whole-cluster bootstrap association. Local/regional botanical sorting can still modulate the relative fit of regional donor taxa while a genus-level shared floral repertoire persists.

**Not established:** Which genus will colonize an island in advance (the model is GIVEN actual island genus presence), independent ancestral mainland flower traits (source donors are actually different accepted plant species recorded on OTHER GIFT ISLANDS and assigned species-wide literature Direct scores), adaptive in-situ floral evolution or phenotype population shift, phylogenetically independent convergence, absence/replacement of pollinator guilds or effective seed/pollen fitness. Original separate strict actual mainland candidate source test found **0** directly scored colour×form botanically accepted names external to ALL original 4,505 source island floras, and just 9 when using the narrower 2,324 scoreable island subset; it does NOT independently identify colonization/ancestry. European WOL/GloBI/EuPPollNet visitor sources do not currently deliver globally independent study×plant phenotypic selection/efficacy to transform genus portability into a pollinator syndrome mechanism.

**Manuscript position:** retain original 4,505-island floristic functional category overlap, true region-specific floral architecture frequencies and exact genus-between/within source accounting as ecological MAIN. Add strictly geographically heldout **7/13 conditional genus-linked categorical trait transport** as the strongest currently verified multiregional predictive supplement, with the numerator and denominator waterfall and strong source-provenance/causal ceilings. Native-only three-region absence of common support and source-mode-dependent exceptions belong in supplementary sensitivity. The seven endpoints are correlated source phenotypes and this is an exploratory support rule, not a family-wise corrected probability of all future biogeographic cases.
