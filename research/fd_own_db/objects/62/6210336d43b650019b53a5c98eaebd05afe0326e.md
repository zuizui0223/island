## Main scientific question and stringent non-leakage design

Does a flower trait's original genus-associated category composition, observed in species exclusively documented in OTHER geographic regions, help predict SAME-genus flower trait categories of a focal island beyond a genus-blind source prior? Original source contrast code restricts BOTH training sources to **different species names** from the original GIFT focal island's ENTIRE accepted botanical flora. It excludes every accepted species whose occurrence crosses both target and external broad regions from BOTH source pools, then compares outcome-blind equally sampled source plant names per shared botanical genus, same external blind prior. Only actual direct High/Medium species-level floral categories from the original GIFT trait ledger are scored, no imaginary marginal cross-product flower combinations.

- The predictor knows the focal island's actual botanical **genus composition**, so it is a **conditional, not prospective**, distribution prediction.
- The original reference donors are accepted plants reported in other **GIFT island floras**; they are **NOT independently reconstructed ancestral mainland plant populations**. First candidate mainland GIFT source grouping is an uncertainty clustering alternative, not evidence that cross-region donor plants actually colonized the focal island.
- Require at least **30 original focal island floras**, **10 independent 10-degree physical spatial blocks** and **10 first original candidate mainland source groups**. Both genuine proper per-plant losses, **Brier and log loss**, must have **positive grouped exploratory 95% resampling lower endpoints in BOTH block and first-source cluster schemes**. The 13 correlated trait endpoints are not independent evolutionary tests, and intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory evidence.
- Retain original all-recorded and strict literal native subsets, but do NOT pool the native and introduced labels or make eligibility an actual global population definition. The primary botanical population remains 4,505 islands.

This is missing statistical common support, NOT a negative biological result or proof that source transfer only occurs among introduced plants.

## Biological interpretation and limits

**Supported conditional prediction:** information about original categorical floral shape and other traits is retained among different accepted species of the SAME botanical genus occurring in independent major geography, despite extensive island species turnover. The degree to which the regional local species subset improves prediction is not constant and can reverse sign (floral form in north midlatitude vs tropical). These outcomes are consistent with shared genus-associated phenotypic repertoires plus regional floristic sorting or documentation effects, not necessarily the same observed flower/visitor ecology in both regions.

**Not demonstrated:** repeated independent phylogenetic evolutionary innovations, genus-free colonization prediction, source-mainland ancestral phenotype, adaptive pollinator-syndrome replacement, and island-local population phenotype change. The model is *given* the target island's actual genus roster, source trait values originate from the same global Direct ledger, and source candidate mainland groups organize uncertainty but are not verified ancestor-specific flower donors. Actual GloBI/WOL/EuPPollNet visitor data lack source-independent continent×island pollen fitness comparisons; they are only supplementary provenance, not the mechanism behind the regional source transfer.

**Priority decision for manuscript:** retain the original global botanical functional-category repertoire and taxonomic genus-source reassembly as main results, show **8 of 13 conditionally transferable trait endpoints across 3 nonoverlapping regions** as a significant descriptive/predictive robustness finding, but do NOT equate these endpoint counts to eight independent tests or overclaim universality because direct-native and endpoint joint-colour×form support are weaker. Emphasize simultaneous **conservation of genus-linked information and contingency of local source performance** as the novel macroecological angle.
