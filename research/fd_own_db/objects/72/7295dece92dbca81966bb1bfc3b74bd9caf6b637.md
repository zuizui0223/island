## Biological question and what is ACTUALLY measured

This explicitly tests the revised *pollination syndrome* interpretation of the previously verified tropical source-relative flora results. The independently documented tropical flora has **lower zygomorphic symmetry**, but **more deepest-tube and tubular/bell morphological categories**. A key possible interpretation is *access architecture turnover* rather than simply "loss of all flower specialization." It is biologically consistent with the retention of floral traits accessible to long-tongued Lepidoptera (including butterflies and hawkmoths) or flower-visiting nectarivorous birds, but **no actual visitor guild or effective pollen service is identified from these plant category states**.

Instead of multiplying separately documented marginal traits on different accepted species, retain only REAL original high/medium species-Direct labels for BOTH characters on the SAME accepted plant species. The priority structural pairs include:
- actual **actinomorphic × independently documented deep tube**;
- actual **zygomorphic × independently documented deep tube**;
- actual **tubular or campanulate × independently documented deep tube**;
- actual **actinomorphic × tubular/campanulate** and **zygomorphic × tubular/campanulate**.

Colour × tube/form secondary patterns use source-native ontology. **Critical source coding correction:** the original high/medium Direct single-species colour categories are **`red_pink`**, **`yellow_orange`**, **`white`**, **`blue_purple`**, **`green_brown_inconspicuous`**, **`multicolored_variable`**, and **`other_described`** (source species counts respectively 2,989 /6,990 /10,603 /2,445 /2,662 /651 /213). ### Marginal prevalence versus real trait PAIRING

For any true fixed carrier cohort of species with BOTH trait categories Direct-recorded, define joint prevalence `J = P(A and B)` and the exactly fixed-one-species-marginal frequency expectation `P(A)P(B)`. The within-assemblage co-occurrence excess is:

`C = P(A and B) - P(A)P(B)`.

This is the exact expected joint prevalence under random re-pairing of the two **observed category labels across the same measured species**, holding their marginal counts fixed. Compare source-relative `J_island - J_source` and separately `C_island - C_source`, on the SAME original island flora and same trait-specific documented plant carriers. The latter identifies a **departure from trait-frequency-only pairing**, NOT evolutionary or causal syndrome integration. Taxonomic genus composition and differential Direct annotation can produce apparent positive or negative `C`.

Mainland sources are two actual fixed original potential GIFT native botanical region list constructions: five nearest geographic regions (`geo5`) and ten climate-nearest drawn from fifty geographic candidate regions (`climate10`), not real propagule sources. Candidate accepted species already anywhere in the *entire focal island flora* are excluded from BOTH mainland pools. Require >=5 jointly Direct-characterized focal island species AND >=10 distinctly Direct-characterized source candidates under BOTH definitions; original whole 10° physical-block bootstrap and first candidate GIFT source-group uncertainty. Strict native and strict native-nonendemic are small **nested, nonrandomly status-documented** populations; compare transparently, not as independent biological replications. A second screen requires <=10 percentage-point island-versus-BOTH-source difference in Direct trait annotation fraction.

## Novelty, literature, and next test

Classical syndromes can predict effective pollinators imperfectly (Rosas-Guerrero et al. 2014, *Ecology Letters*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.12224). The synthesis of syndrome methods and biases is Dellinger 2020, *New Phytologist*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.16793. An oceanic island study found real visitors broader than predicted by floral syndromes (Wang et al. 2020, *Scientific Reports*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70954-7). Conversely a Caribbean island mainland comparison found less stereotypical bird-syndrome floral traits despite distinctive hummingbird visits (Vollstädt et al. 2025, *Functional Ecology*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.70068). The broad trait system therefore cannot use a one-to-one `flower_shape -> named guild` assignment.

**Defensible main finding:** Cross-regionally heterogeneous, mainland-source-relative actual floral *trait-combination* repertoires; in tropical original floras, long-tube access is preferentially represented jointly with radial symmetry, while bilateral/deep combinations are less enriched than expected from marginals. The geographical pattern occurs in whole and native-qualified taxa, largely alongside source-qualified **botanical taxonomic assembly**. This tests access architecture vs global uniform floral simplification, not a verified historical bird-versus-butterfly pollinator swap.

**Next independent test required before pollinator-mediated interpretation**: Obtain actual locally comparable plant×visitor interaction data, keep butterflies and hawkmoths separately (the existing qualified GBIF Lepidoptera category pools them), verify bird/bee/long-tongue channel support under local sampling effort, and tie the independently observed visitors to the SAME floral trait species or sister taxa. Then compare the competing 'different floral architecture from lineage composition' and 'effective guild-dependent establishment' hypotheses with source geography, native documentation, and phylogeny. Real floral scent, nectar and landing morphology are not in the current global Direct ontology; do not impute them.
