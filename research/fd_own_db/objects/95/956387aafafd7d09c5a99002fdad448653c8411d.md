## Core ecological question

**When are island floral functional repertoires contracted, and when are they directionally reorganized relative to accessible mainland source floras?** Compare documented (a) mainland-source phenotype repertoire and (b) accepted island flora composition; distinguish botanical lineage composition from distinct-species phenotype variation within shared genera. Ask whether an independently observed pollinator channel shortage contributes additional explanation **only when supported**. Regions reflect source trait/geographic/pollinator supply composition; they are not four independent causal 'treatments'.

Proposed working title: **Regional source pools and lineage turnover structure island floral morphology without a universal simplification pattern**. "Structure" refers to observational floral assemblages, not proven adaptive change.

## Core Results narrative and interpretation ceiling

Opening result: a single scalar 'functional diversity shrinks on islands' loses the opposing structural category shifts and region-specific Q. The stronger global macroecology is **source-relative floral repertoire reassembly that is directionally heterogeneous by morphological sector and region**. The exact observed source-relative contrasts co-occur with major genus identity/relative-weight differences; within-shared-genus signed contributions tend to be small and source/trait-documentation sensitive, leaving *some* limited distinct-species state differences but no identified floral adaptation.

Interpretation should explicitly distinguish: (i) **source opportunity/checklist representation**, (ii) documented floristic composition among accepted species, (iii) taxonomic/phylogenetic assembly, (iv) observed actual visitor identities/effectiveness, and (v) genetically or phenotypically documented changes after island colonization. Only (i)-(iii) are directly addressed by fd current data; even those depend on GIFT source and annotation effort. Unrecorded candidate genera were not experimentally exposed to an island establishment filter.

Thus the trait-pairing observation is real and taxonomically structured, but apparent genus-conditional similarity is partly MECHANICAL. Neither the exact four-part genus identity decomposition nor this analytic coupling null independently proves that historical pollinator availability caused genus entry or extinction.
