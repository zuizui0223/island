### Inferential correction

A GIFT potential mainland region plant checklist records **which accepted species could be available**, but not a completely separate mainland observer's measurement of their flower colour and form. Reusing the original species-wide Direct trait labels on distinct source-listed names can support a name-disjoint **conditional known-genus prediction**. It **cannot** identify plant-arrival success, true mainland progenitors, morphological divergence within an island species, island-native adaptation, realized local pollination service, or an ecological bee-to-bird/moth guild replacement.

The source-local and global-genus predictions are scored against the SAME original plants, but source geography is not compared to two fully donor-size/coverage-matched external pseudo-sources in this new study. Its proper log-loss improvement must be treated as exploratory and evidence-limited, not source-specific causal proof. Stronger GIFT donor controls from different, less-restrictive source cohorts in earlier FD PRs cannot be silently transferred to these particular original all-4505, original native or 456-pair test populations.

## Research decision

**Protect the global 4,505-island floral composition/taxonomic-turnover story.** Its original flower-colour×form total-variation overlap remains 0.3869 across 2,699,326 pairs and 0.2472 even on 842,058 species-disjoint pairs, but that does NOT become an evolutionary positive effect simply by conditioning on sparse GIFT category donors. The previous small northern-midlatitude 12-block-disjoint/456-pair signal remains a **posthoc hypothesis generator**. There is currently insufficient truly mainland-independent Direct joint phenotype training data to attribute it to mainland-source lineage opportunities, selection, or a transition in effective pollination guild.

**Next highest-value study:** freeze the actual 456-pair eligible endpoint island UNION before donor construction, report its complete GIFT heldout congeneric source coverage separately from the larger n10/coverage10 cohort; then seek externally and directly observed mainland species flower phenotypes, truly source-independent native lineage histories and actual population-level pollination data.
