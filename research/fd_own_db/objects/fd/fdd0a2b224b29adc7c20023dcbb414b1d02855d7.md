## Novelty boundary against pertinent literature

- **Hetherington-Rauth & Johnson (2020), Am. Nat. doi:10.1086/709018**: 556 species and 136 phylogenetically independent island-mainland sister comparisons; no universal floral-size island syndrome and ecological filtering explicitly proposed as an alternative. Therefore *“island floral traits are context dependent”* alone is NOT original here.
- **Zell et al. (2025; published online 2024), New Phytol. doi:10.1111/nph.20234**: 3,222 flowering-plant species; island colonization, SC, floral symmetry, life history, arrival opportunity and taxonomy jointly analyzed. Therefore *“trait-related colonization / lineage filtering”* alone is NOT novel.
- **Barajas Barbosa et al. (2023), Nature doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06305-z**: Tenerife native-flora functional space already partitions colonization and in-situ diversification. Do not claim the first island trait-space / assembly analysis.
- **Ollerton et al. (2009), Ann. Bot. doi:10.1093/aob/mcp031; Rosas-Guerrero et al. (2014), Ecol. Lett. doi:10.1111/ele.12224**: pollination syndromes can be tested with independently observed animal visitors and pollination effectiveness; phenotype alone is not visitor identification. Island bird-pollination systems can be generalist and mismatched with textbook syndromes (see Functional Ecology doi:10.1111/1365-2435.70068 and doi:10.1111/1365-2435.14493).

**Defensible potential contribution here:** an unusually broad global island-flora frame, true same-species direct flower-category pairs without triple-completion, quantitative taxonomic-versus-functional reassembly, *signed exact mainland candidate genus accounting*, and transparent testing of the limits of ecological guild attribution. This remains botanical macroecology rather than verified pollinator-driven adaptation.
