# Tropical far-tail dispersal sensitivity — 2026-10-05

Status: **post-hoc diagnostic; outside frozen Chapter 1 inference.**

This follows PR #311 and tests the alternative explanation that the tropical
`yellow/orange × deep-tube` far-tail signal is generated by dispersal filtering of
coastal / ocean-dispersed lineages rather than pollination filtering.

## Exact replay

The direct native baseline is exactly reproduced from the frozen inputs after applying
the corrected spherical coastline distances:

- all-native: 120 islands; 67 above 783.017 km; 26 far-tail blocks; post-hinge slope
  **+0.8074**;
- native-nonendemic: 119 islands; 66 above 783.017 km; 26 far-tail blocks;
  post-hinge slope **+0.9844**.

## Strict ocean-dispersal exclusion

The strict species list is frozen independently from the response coefficient and is
limited to species with direct literature support for coastal/ocean-current dispersal.
The main contributing species include `Cordia subcordata`, `Thespesia populnea`,
`Decalobanthus peltatus`, `Ipomoea violacea`, `Ipomoea imperati`,
`Operculina turpethum`, `Ipomoea alba`, and `Hibiscus tiliaceus`.

Among direct native-nonendemic target occurrences:

- before the hinge, strict ocean-dispersed species are **6.1% of eligible trials**
  and **25.3% of deep successes**;
- above the hinge, they are **16.0% of eligible trials**
  and **51.9% of deep successes**.

Thus the strict dispersal set is strongly enriched in the remote tail.

After removing those species:

- native-nonendemic slope: **+0.9844 → -0.2330**;
- normal-approximation P = 0.462;
- tail-block t sensitivity P = 0.469;
- leave-one-tail-block slopes = **-0.500 to -0.009**;
- jackknife P = 0.534.

The same result appears in all-native flora:

- **+0.8074 → -0.4064**;
- tail-block t sensitivity P = 0.263;
- jackknife P = 0.349.

Therefore the positive tropical far-tail response is not robust to removal of the
predefined ocean-dispersed coastal species.

A deliberately broader genus-level exclusion is an upper-bound sensitivity, not the
primary test. It also reverses the slope (native-nonendemic **-0.6625**), but this
over-removes non-coastal congeners and should not be interpreted as a separate
confirmatory result.

## Tube-depth coding audit

The locked ontology defines `tube_depth_class` as corolla-tube depth and classifies
measurements >15 mm as `deep`.

Three high-leverage species are flagged because the ledger's deep state is not adequately
supported by the source morphology:

- `Thespesia populnea`: the linked Flora of China treatment describes a campanulate
  corolla ca. 5 cm across/long but does not provide corolla-tube depth;
- `Hibiscus tiliaceus`: the linked treatment gives a campanulate corolla diameter and
  petal length, not corolla-tube depth;
- `Physalis angulata`: independent descriptions give total corolla length below the
  >15 mm deep threshold.

`Crescentia cujete` is retained in the coding audit because independent descriptions
support a true multi-centimetre corolla tube.

Removing only the three coding-flagged species does **not** explain the tropical signal:

- native-nonendemic slope remains **+0.8539**;
- tail-block t P = 9.0e-7;
- jackknife P = 5.4e-6.

Therefore tube-depth miscoding is a real data-quality issue but is not the main cause of
the far-tail pattern. Once the strict ocean-dispersed set is removed, adding the coding
flags changes little (slope **-0.3480**, jackknife P = 0.520).

## Floristic-status audit

One `Crescentia cujete` occurrence is labelled native/nonendemic by the frozen
GIFT-origin status at approximately 2.26 S, 121.05 E. This is outside the modern POWO
native range (Mexico to Venezuela and the Caribbean), so the old status layer contains at
least one clear false-native record for this species. Seven other far-tail records are in
the Caribbean and are compatible with the listed native range.

Within the 120-island tropical target-support cohort, the overall floristic-status
resolution fraction does **not** increase with corrected isolation; it decreases
(Spearman rho approximately **-0.342**). Thus the support imbalance around 783 km is
real, but it is not explained by a simple increase in status-resolution rate with
distance in this cohort.

## Interpretation

The post-hoc tropical far-tail signal should no longer be described as evidence for
butterfly, bird, or other pollination-channel maintenance.

The strongest current explanation is:

> remote tropical island floras are enriched for coastal/ocean-dispersed lineages, and
> those lineages disproportionately occupy the yellow/orange × deep-tube response cell.

This is a **dispersal / assemblage-filtering result**, not a pollination result. The
hinge remains outside Chapter 1's frozen inferential claims.
