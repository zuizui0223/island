# Cover letter draft — Ecology Letters first shot

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Letter, **“Island isolation is associated with recurrent reproductive assurance but regionally contingent floral change,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

A long-standing expectation in island biology is that isolation should favour reproductive independence when compatible mates or effective pollen delivery become unreliable. Yet that expectation is often translated into a single floral island syndrome, even though the same reproductive problem need not produce the same flower phenotype in every region. We ask a more general question: **which reproductive functions recur with island isolation, which floral traits remain geographically contingent, and does the same isolation gradient carry an independently measured reproductive constraint?**

We integrate two distinct global evidence layers. The trait database indexes 106,295 angiosperm species within an 8,264-island geographic universe; the broad H1 analyses span 4,379 sampled islands. Rather than average traits into a composite syndrome score, we estimate seven separate reproductive, colour and structural responses in four geographic regions. Self-compatibility increases with isolation in all four regions in both the broad contemporary flora and the WCVP regional-native-compatible sensitivity. Other reproductive and floral responses are less uniform: selfing, generalized form, actinomorphy, tube depth and colour differ among regions. Conditional analyses further show that generalized floral accessibility is not statistically exhausted by measured reproductive assurance, with FDR-supported selfing-adjusted responses in northern high latitudes and the tropical all-analysis.

The second evidence layer reanalyses 2,969 experiments in the previously published GloPL pollen-supplementation database. Experimental pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation (β = 0.0919, finite-publication P = 0.01594), and the positive offshore-only gradient remains after continental zero-distance sites are excluded. In exact-species post-hoc comparisons, the reproductive-assurance and generalized-accessibility states enriched along the island gradient are each associated with lower current pollen limitation (P = 0.00417 and 0.02334).

Earlier work established island self-compatibility enrichment, mutualism filtering, and trait–pollen-limitation associations (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Razanajatovo et al. 2019; Delavaux et al. 2024; Burns et al. 2019). The conceptual advance is therefore not a novel single relationship or experiment, but **a cross-domain test of functional recurrence without phenotypic uniformity**. Island isolation is associated most consistently with reproductive-assurance traits, while the visible floral phenotype through which insularity is expressed remains region dependent. The same geographic axis is independently associated with stronger pollen limitation, and the island-associated response states are functionally aligned with lower contemporary limitation. This constraint–response triangle links island macroecology to experimental plant reproduction without requiring a universal flower type or a named global pollinator mechanism.

We explicitly limit the inference. The study does not establish historical mediation from pollen limitation to contemporary trait composition, a global decline in pollinator abundance or visitation, or within-lineage evolution rather than assemblage filtering. H2 is conditional decomposition and H4 is post-hoc functional triangulation. The corrected geographic exposure is a post-hoc measurement repair, not a new prospective confirmation, and the H1 individual tests are pointwise rather than a family-wise declaration of a universal syndrome.

We believe this fits *Ecology Letters* because it reframes a classic island-syndrome question around a general ecological distinction: repeated environmental constraints can generate recurrent functions without uniform phenotypes. The study brings geographically extensive island-assemblage comparisons into the same continental-isolation framework as independent *previously published* pollen-addition measurements, without claiming that the same islands or plants were followed experimentally.
