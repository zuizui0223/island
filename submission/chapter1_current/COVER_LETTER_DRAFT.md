# Cover letter draft — Ecology Letters first shot

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Letter, **“A recurrent global floral island syndrome extends beyond the selfing syndrome,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

A central expectation of island biology is that reproductive assurance should become increasingly valuable as colonization and pollen delivery become less reliable. Recent global work has shown that breeding system and floral symmetry help predict whether species colonize islands. What remains unresolved is what happens **among established island floras as source isolation increases**: whether floral change is simply downstream of selfing, whether pollinator-facing architecture forms an additional response, and whether the same isolation gradient is independently associated with experimental pollen limitation.

We address this question by combining two independent global evidence layers. The island dataset contains 106,295 angiosperm species across a corrected universe of 8,264 island units. A seven-trait floral–reproductive response to isolation recurs across four geographic regions, but individual traits are not uniformly concordant. Crucially, floral accessibility remains positively associated with isolation after reproductive assurance is conditioned on, with FDR support in northern high latitudes and the tropics. Independently, 2,969 pollen-supplementation experiments show increasing pollen limitation with isolation (β = 0.0919, P = 0.0157). In exact-species post-hoc comparisons, the reproductive-assurance and accessibility states enriched with isolation are each associated with lower current pollen limitation.

The conceptual advance is that the floral island syndrome is **functionally recurrent but not serial**. Island reproductive change cannot be reduced to an obligatory sequence of isolation → selfing → floral simplification. Instead, reproductive assurance and pollinator-facing accessibility form partially separable response components along a geographic gradient that is independently associated with stronger pollen limitation. Detailed colour and architecture trajectories remain region dependent.

We believe this fits *Ecology Letters* because the study moves beyond predicting island occurrence from colonization traits. It tests a general ecological hypothesis across broad geography and taxonomy, combines island-scale macroecology with independent experimental evidence, and identifies an isolation-associated constraint–response structure that is distinct from both Baker's law alone and prior GloPL analyses of species range edges.

We explicitly limit the inference. We do not claim historical mediation from pollen limitation to trait evolution, a global decline in pollinator abundance or visitation, a universal named pollination syndrome, or uniform change in every floral trait. The H4 functional bridge is post-hoc triangulation. A geography audit repaired a known exposure mismatch and defines the primary measurement baseline; it is not presented as a new prospective confirmation.

The manuscript is within the *Ecology Letters* Letter limits of 5,000 main-text words and six display items. The abstract has been reduced to fewer than 150 words.
