# Cover letter draft — journal-neutral

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, **“A recurrent global floral island syndrome separates reproductive assurance from pollinator-facing floral reorganization under increasing pollen limitation,”** for publication in [JOURNAL].

Island plants are often expected to become increasingly self-reliant as geographic isolation increases, but it remains unclear whether reproductive assurance and floral simplification form one recurrent global syndrome, whether floral change is simply a consequence of selfing, and whether the same trait states are functionally associated with reduced pollen limitation.

We assembled a global island plant database containing **106,295 angiosperm species and 222,688 resolved species-by-trait cells**. Following a geography audit, the primary submission baseline uses **8,264 island units** and source-matched GSHHG 2.3.7 continental coastlines. This repair removes one continental split component and corrects 1,113 spurious zero-distance island exposures while preserving the frozen trait evidence and analysis design.

The study makes four linked contributions. First, a seven-response floral/reproductive isolation vector remains jointly supported across four predeclared geographic regions, while individual trait responses retain meaningful regional heterogeneity. Second, generalized floral accessibility remains positively associated with isolation after conditioning on reproductive assurance in the primary analysis, showing that the floral response is not reducible to selfing alone. Third, experimental pollen limitation independently increases with geographic isolation (standardized beta = 0.0919, p = 0.0157). Fourth, the literal species-level H2 reproductive-assurance and floral-accessibility scores are each associated with lower current pollen limitation in exact-species GloPL overlap.

The main conceptual advance is therefore not simply that island plants self more. Rather, **a recurrent functional island response contains partially separable reproductive-assurance and pollinator-facing floral components, while detailed colour–architecture reorganization remains context dependent**. This distinction connects classic reproductive-assurance theory, floral specialization and global island biogeography.

We are careful not to infer historical mediation, global pollinator decline, one universal named pollination syndrome or uniform change in every floral trait from these cross-sectional data. The corrected geography is a post-hoc measurement repair selected as the primary exposure baseline, not a new prospective confirmation. H4 remains post-hoc functional triangulation rather than causal mediation.
