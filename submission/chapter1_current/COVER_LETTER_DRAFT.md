# Cover letter draft — Ecology Letters first shot

**Draft only. Do not send before all authors approve the final manuscript and the data-policy gate is resolved.**

Dear Editors,

Please consider our Letter, **“Do pollen-supply constraints underlie floral island syndromes?,”** for publication in *Ecology Letters*.

A classic explanation for floral island syndromes is a chain from isolation through reduced effective pollination to increased reproductive assurance and reduced floral attraction. The key reproductive constraint is seldom evaluated alongside global plant floral patterns. We therefore ask whether **pollen-supplementation evidence reveals an isolation-associated reproductive constraint**, what reproductive and floral trait states are observed along the same broad geographic axis, and whether their contemporaneous functional associations support—but do not prove—that proposed history.

We integrate two distinct global evidence layers. The trait database indexes 106,295 angiosperm species within an 8,264-island geographic universe; the broad H1 analyses span 4,379 sampled islands. Rather than average traits into a composite syndrome score, we estimate seven separate reproductive, colour and structural responses in four geographic regions. Self-compatibility increases with isolation in all four regions in both the broad contemporary flora and the WCVP regional-native-compatible sensitivity. Other reproductive and floral responses are less uniform: selfing, generalized form, actinomorphy, tube depth and colour differ among regions. Conditional analyses further show that generalized floral accessibility is not statistically exhausted by measured reproductive assurance, with FDR-supported selfing-adjusted responses in northern high latitudes and the tropical all-analysis.

The second evidence layer reanalyses 2,969 experiments in the previously published GloPL pollen-supplementation database. Experimental pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation (β = 0.0919, finite-publication P = 0.01594), and the positive offshore-only gradient remains after continental zero-distance sites are excluded. In exact-species post-hoc comparisons, the reproductive-assurance and generalized-accessibility states enriched along the island gradient are each associated with lower current pollen limitation (P = 0.00417 and 0.02334).

Earlier work established island self-compatibility enrichment, mutualism filtering, and trait–pollen-limitation associations (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Razanajatovo et al. 2019; Delavaux et al. 2024; Burns et al. 2019). The conceptual advance is therefore not a novel single correlation or experiment, but **a cross-domain empirical test of the pollen-supply-constraint premise alongside diverse reproductive and floral assemblage patterns**. Island isolation is associated most consistently with reproductive-assurance traits, while the visible floral phenotype through which insularity is expressed remains region dependent. The same geographic axis is independently associated with stronger pollen limitation, and the island-associated response states are functionally aligned with lower contemporary limitation. This constraint–response triangle links island macroecology to experimental plant reproduction without requiring a universal flower type or a named global pollinator mechanism.

We explicitly limit the inference. The study does not establish historical mediation from pollen limitation to contemporary trait composition, a global decline in pollinator abundance or visitation, or within-lineage evolution rather than assemblage filtering. H2 is conditional decomposition and H4 is post-hoc functional triangulation. The corrected geographic exposure is a post-hoc measurement repair, not a new prospective confirmation, and the H1 individual tests are pointwise rather than a family-wise declaration of a universal syndrome.

We believe this fits *Ecology Letters* because it reframes a classic island-syndrome question around a general ecological distinction: repeated environmental constraints can generate recurrent functions without uniform phenotypes. The Chapter 1 Letter couples geographically extensive island-assemblage comparisons to an independent *previously published* pollen-addition resource without claiming matched experimental follow-up or historical mediation. The mechanistic simulations are reserved for a separate `izu-core` paper and are not components of this submission.

The manuscript contains six main display items and remains within the Letter text and abstract limits. Submission remains conditional on closing the journal's data-policy gate for third-party trait records, as documented in the accompanying package.
