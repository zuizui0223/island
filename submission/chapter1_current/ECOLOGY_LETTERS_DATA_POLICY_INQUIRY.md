Rechecked against the Ecology Letters author guidelines on 8 October 2026. **Draft only; not a sent inquiry or evidence of editorial permission.** The Data and Code Availability section directs repository-acceptability questions to `ecolets2@cefe.cnrs.fr`; the journal's Editorial Office also lists `ecolets@cefe.cnrs.fr`.

**Subject:** Pre-submission question on third-party trait-data archiving

Dear Ecology Letters Editorial Office,

We are preparing a Letter that combines a global island plant-trait database with independent pollen-supplementation data to test which reproductive functions recur with island isolation, which floral phenotypes remain region dependent, and whether the same geographic gradient is associated with pollen limitation.

Before submission, we would like to confirm whether our data-archiving plan can meet the journal's Data and Code Availability requirements.

The analyses use a frozen species × trait ledger assembled from multiple published and public third-party sources. We can publicly archive the complete analysis code, corrected geography, derived model-ready outputs, metadata, provenance/rights manifests, and all trait cells for which redistribution is authorized. The frozen scientific ledger contains **222,688 resolved species × axis cells**, but only **46,274** are currently authorized for redistribution; **176,414** are restricted or require rights review. We cannot lawfully redistribute the complete frozen ledger as a new public dataset without additional permissions.

We can provide source-level provenance for every analysis cell, an immutable SHA-256 identity (`a6eef8d731b2730a99e388ddd0683d7ac9b2af30d43f1f9569c857f89f664b2a`) for the full ledger, reconstruction code where source licences permit automated retrieval, and private reviewer access to any material we are legally permitted to share. A 46,274-cell rights-filtered derivative is already public (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22704973), but is not the paper's complete analysis input. We will not describe the rights-filtered public subset as the complete analysis database.

The published author guidelines state that exceptions are discretionary and give sensitive-data examples, but they do not explicitly state whether third-party legal/licensing restrictions qualify. Would Ecology Letters consider our situation under an exception or alternative reviewer-access arrangement? If so, what minimum archive, reviewer-access or reconstruction package would the editors/data editor require at initial submission?

We are asking before submission so that we do not enter review with an archive that falls short of the journal's reproducibility requirements.

Thank you for your guidance.
