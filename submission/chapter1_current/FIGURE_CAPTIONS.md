# Main figure captions

## Figure 1 | Global scope and corrected geographic exposure
(A) Global distribution of the **8,264 corrected island units** used in the plant analysis, grouped by the four predeclared geographic strata. (B) Global distribution of the 1,248 unique sites in the independent GloPL pollen-supplementation dataset. (C) Summary of data coverage across the island trait database and GloPL. (D) Geography-audit summary: 1,113 spurious island zero distances were repaired using source-matched GSHHG 2.3.7 coastlines, one Eurasian continental split component was excluded, and 996 true continental GloPL zero distances were retained.

## Figure 2 | Database construction and analytical workflow
GSHHG geography, GBIF island floras and source-audited trait evidence were integrated into the global island trait database spanning reproductive assurance, flower colour and floral structure. H1 and H2 form the island-trait workflow. H3 uses independent GloPL pollen-supplementation experiments, and H4 links the H2 species scores to GloPL through exact species matching.

## Figure 3 | Inferential structure
Geographic isolation is tested directly against the floral/reproductive island response (H1) and experimental pollen limitation (H3). H2 decomposes the floral response into reproductive assurance and residual floral responses after reproductive-assurance adjustment. Dashed arrows denote hypothesized historical causal links; these are not directly identified. H4 tests whether island-associated H2 trait states are associated with lower current pollen limitation.

## Figure 4 | H1: recurrent multivariate response with region-dependent expression
(A) Corrected isolation coefficients for the seven atomic H1 responses in the primary all-analysis scope. The seven-response vector is jointly supported in all four regions, but individual responses are not uniformly positive; the southern shallow/open-tube coefficient is negative. (B) Descriptive means for the reproductive-assurance, colour and accessibility/generalization domains. (C) Joint multivariate q-values in the all-analysis and Direct-only scopes.

## Figure 5 | H2: conditional pathway decomposition
(A) Corrected isolation coefficients for reproductive assurance and for generalized accessibility and plain colour after conditioning on reproductive assurance. Primary selfing-adjusted accessibility is FDR-supported in northern high latitudes and the tropics; the tropical Direct-only estimate is nominally positive but not FDR-supported (q = 0.1196). (B) Confirmatory selfing-adjusted raw colour-state responses. (C) Representative FDR-supported colour-conditioned architecture responses from the corrected analysis: a northern-midlatitude yellow/orange × large-bee-form decline, a northern-high-latitude blue/purple × deep-large-bee-tube decline, a tropical Direct-only yellow/orange × butterfly-deep-tube increase, and a southern-extratropical yellow/orange × bird-form decline. These descriptive trait combinations do not identify realized pollinators. H2 is a conditional decomposition, not a causal mediation test.

## Figure 6 | H3 and H4: ecological pressure and functional bridge
(A) In the corrected GloPL analysis, pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation (beta = 0.09191, SE = 0.03806, two-sided p = 0.01575); the no-zero and supplemental-only sensitivities are shown alongside it. (B) Exact-species H4 associations for the literal Direct-only H2 reproductive-assurance score (beta = -0.29830, p = 0.00396) and generalized-accessibility score (beta = -0.29566, p = 0.02187). (C) Primary atomic-trait sensitivities. Negative H4 coefficients indicate association with lower current pollen limitation; they do not establish historical mediation.
