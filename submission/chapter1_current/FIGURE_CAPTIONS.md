## Figure 1 | Distinct plant-assemblage and pollen-supplementation data streams
(A) Corrected global geographic distribution of the 8,264 island units, in the four analysis strata. (B) Separate geography of the 1,248 published GloPL pollen-supplementation sites, including continental observations. (C) Dataset accounting: 4,379 broad-H1 observed islands; 106,295 accepted species in the trait universe; 222,688 of 318,885 raw species-axis cells resolved (69.83%); 2,969 GloPL experimental rows and 919 contributing publications. (D) Study design: solid links describe associations estimated from non-identical data streams, not a fitted joint structural-equation model. The dashed historical causal path from pollen-service restriction through sorting or adaptation to extant traits is explicitly untested. Exact species matching is available only for the post-hoc H4 comparisons.

## Figure 2 | Pollen limitation versus continental isolation (source H3)
Publication-weighted standardized isolation coefficients for the primary GloPL model and two prespecified measurement sensitivities, with pointwise finite-publication 95% t confidence intervals. Primary slope beta = +0.09191, SE = 0.03806, two-sided finite-publication p = 0.01594; the supplemental-only sensitivity is positive but unsupported (beta = +0.04410, p = 0.31082). The post-hoc offshore-only slope and mainland/offshore indicator contrast are reported in Supplement S5 rather than conflated with the primary estimate. Current experimental pollen limitation measures reproductive improvement after additional pollen, not historical pollinator disappearance.

## Figure 3 | Seven traits under final finite-cluster inference: reproductive and floral responses (source H1)
The seven-atomic-outcome H1 coefficients are replotted at two-column print width from the unchanged 112 fit rows of `results/h1_final_traitwise_t_20261004/traitwise_results.csv`. The historical H1 graphic remains preserved byte-for-byte in its original results directory. It displays trait-specific isolation coefficient estimates and pointwise 95% finite-spatial-cluster t intervals for four regions and reported evidence/origin scopes. Three reproductive outcomes are self-compatibility, selfing mating system and autonomous selfing; the remaining four describe plain colour, generalized form, actinomorphy and shallow/open tube. Nominal two-sided unadjusted significance is not a family-wise test of a pooled global floral syndrome; 83.65% of observed island×species records have unresolved floristic-origin status.

## Figure 4 | Floral accessibility after conditioning on reproductive assurance (source H2)
Three separately scaled model outcome columns show regional standardized-isolation coefficients and pointwise finite-cluster t intervals: (A) `selfing_core` (OLS), (B) `generalized_accessible` (OLS conditional on `selfing_core`) and (C) `plain_colour` (beta-binomial logit conditional on `selfing_core`). Coefficients across response/link scales must not be compared numerically as equal effects. Panel B prints the BH q values from the frozen eight-test H2b family; q = 0.00243 for northern high latitudes and q = 0.01913 for tropical All, while tropical Direct-only does not pass FDR. This is conditional decomposition, not causal mediation or direct pollinator selection.

## Figure 5 | Post-hoc association of plant traits with current pollen limitation (source H4)
Separate exact-species GloPL matched datasets show (A) reproductive-assurance `selfing_core` (455 primary matched species, 409 publications; beta = −0.29830, finite-publication p = 0.00417) and (B) `generalized_accessible` (143 primary matched species, 143 publications; beta = −0.29566, p = 0.02334). Primary, no-zero-constant and supplemental-only measurement subsets are shown, each with pointwise finite-publication t intervals and its own sample size. These are distinct, non-paired, post-hoc regression samples; neither their similar coefficient magnitude nor negative direction proves buffering, historical mediation or evolutionary causality.
