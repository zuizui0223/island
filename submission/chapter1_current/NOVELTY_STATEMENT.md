# Novelty statement — Ecology Letters

## One-sentence novelty

**Island isolation is associated with a recurrent reproductive function—most consistently self-compatibility—while floral phenotype remains region dependent, and the same geographic gradient independently carries stronger experimental pollen limitation.**

## What was known

- Baker's law predicts enrichment of uniparental reproductive capacity during colonization.
- Self-compatible species are over-represented on islands.
- Breeding system, lifespan and floral symmetry/generalization can help predict island colonization.
- Selfing can be accompanied by floral reduction, while floral architecture also mediates access by animal pollen vectors.
- Pollen limitation varies globally with ecological context, but prior global GloPL work found little general increase towards species' range edges.

## What this study changes

1. **It separates recurrent function from visible phenotype.**  
   Seven traits are analysed separately rather than collapsed into a syndrome score. Self-compatibility increases with isolation in all four regions in both broad and WCVP-compatible analyses, whereas other reproductive, colour and structural traits vary geographically.

2. **It tests a serial selfing-only explanation.**  
   Generalized accessibility remains positively associated with isolation after conditioning on measured reproductive assurance, with strongest FDR support in northern high latitudes and tropical all-analysis. Floral change is therefore not statistically exhausted by the measured selfing core.

3. **It measures the ecological constraint independently.**  
   In 2,969 pollen-supplementation experiments, pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation.

4. **It closes the functional triangle without overclaiming causality.**  
   In exact-species post-hoc overlap, reproductive-assurance and accessibility scores are each associated with lower current pollen limitation.

## Conceptual synthesis

The paper replaces the idea of a universal floral checklist with **functional recurrence plus phenotypic contingency**. A repeated reproductive problem can generate a recurrent functional response while regional source pools, interaction environments and assembly histories determine the detailed floral phenotype.

## Claim boundary

The study does not identify historical pollen limitation as a causal mediator of to present-day island assemblages, realized pollinator identity from floral traits, or within-lineage evolution rather than species sorting and colonization filtering.
