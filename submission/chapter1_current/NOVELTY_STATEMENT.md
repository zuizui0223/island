# Novelty statement — Ecology Letters

## One-sentence novelty

**Island isolation is associated with a recurrent reproductive function—most consistently self-compatibility—while floral phenotype remains region dependent, and the same geographic gradient independently carries stronger experimental pollen limitation.**

## Scale positioning

The database covers **106,295 accepted angiosperms** and an **8,264-island geographic universe**, but the H1 broad fitted union is **4,379 islands**, with different species denominators for each trait. This is much wider island coverage than Delavaux et al. (2024; **345 checklist islands, 212 oceanic**) and a much larger species-level data resource than Grossenbacher et al. (2017; **1,560 species**), Razanajatovo et al. (2019; **1,752**), or Zell et al. (2025; **2,931** analysed for species–island arrival distance). Those comparisons **do not equate GBIF occurrence-derived island units with curated native checklists** or prove superior phylogenetic/colonization inference.

The H3 **2,969 experiments** come from the **previously published GloPL database**, also used by Burns et al. (2019; **1,249 species**) and Bennett et al. (2020; **1,247 species**). The H4 joined samples are **455 species** for selfing and **143 species** for accessibility. Thus the paper's sampling advantage lies in *geographically extensive assemblage integration*, not newly assembled experimental evidence or 106,295 completely measured species.

## What was known

- Baker's law predicts enrichment of uniparental reproductive capacity during colonization.
- Self-compatible species are over-represented on islands (Grossenbacher et al. 2017; Razanajatovo et al. 2019).
- Breeding system, lifespan and floral symmetry/generalization can help predict island colonization (Zell et al. 2025), while pollinator/microbial mutualism filtering of island richness was already shown across GIFT islands (Delavaux et al. 2024).
- Selfing can be accompanied by floral reduction, while floral architecture also mediates access by animal pollen vectors.
- GloPL analyses already connected autofertility, floral generalization and pollinator dependence to pollen limitation (Burns et al. 2019; Bennett et al. 2020); separate range-edge analysis found little general pollen-limitation increase towards species' range limits.

## What this study changes

1. **It separates recurrent function from visible phenotype.**  
   Seven traits are analysed separately rather than collapsed into a syndrome score. Self-compatibility increases with isolation in all four regions in both broad and WCVP-compatible analyses, whereas other reproductive, colour and structural traits vary geographically.

2. **It tests a serial selfing-only explanation.**  
   Generalized accessibility remains positively associated with isolation after conditioning on measured reproductive assurance, with strongest FDR support in northern high latitudes and tropical all-analysis. Floral change is therefore not statistically exhausted by the measured selfing core.

3. **It tests a previously published experimental dataset against a different geographic exposure.**  
   Using 2,969 GloPL experimental rows, pollen limitation increases with corrected site-to-continental-coast distance. This is independent of the island-flora trait dataset but reuses the GloPL source.

4. **It completes a post-hoc functional triangle without overclaiming causality or independent replication.**  
   In exact-species post-hoc overlap, reproductive-assurance and accessibility scores are each associated with lower current pollen limitation.

## Conceptual synthesis

The paper replaces the idea of a universal floral checklist with **functional recurrence plus phenotypic contingency**. A repeated reproductive problem can generate a recurrent functional response while regional source pools, interaction environments and assembly histories determine the detailed floral phenotype.

## Claim boundary

The study does not identify historical pollen limitation as a causal mediator of to present-day island assemblages, realized pollinator identity from floral traits, or within-lineage evolution rather than species sorting and colonization filtering.
