# Main figure captions

## Figure 1 | Global scope of the study
(A) Global distribution of the **8,264 corrected island units** used in the plant analysis, grouped by the four predeclared geographic strata. (B) Global distribution of the 1,248 unique sites in the independent GloPL pollen-supplementation dataset. (C) Summary of data coverage across the island trait database and GloPL. (D) Corrected distance to continental coastlines measured against source-matched GSHHG 2.3.7 geometry. One Eurasian split component was excluded from the original 8,265-unit universe.

## Figure 2 | Database construction and analytical workflow
Island geography, GBIF island floras and source-audited trait evidence were integrated into a frozen global island trait database spanning reproductive assurance, flower colour and floral structure. H1 tests recurrence of the multivariate island response, H2 conditionally separates reproductive assurance from additional floral change, H3 independently tests pollen limitation against corrected geographic isolation, and H4 exact-matches species-level H2 scores to GloPL.

## Figure 3 | Inferential structure
Geographic isolation is tested directly against the floral/reproductive island response (H1) and experimental pollen limitation (H3). H2 decomposes the floral response into reproductive assurance and residual floral responses after reproductive-assurance adjustment. Dashed arrows denote hypothesized historical causal links; these are not directly identified. H4 tests whether island-associated H2 trait states are associated with lower current pollen limitation.

## Figure 4 | H1: recurrent multivariate response with region-dependent expression
(A) Corrected isolation coefficients for the seven atomic H1 responses in the primary all-analysis scope. The seven-response vector is jointly supported in all four regions, but individual responses are not uniformly positive; the southern shallow/open-tube coefficient is negative. (B) Descriptive summaries of reproductive assurance, colour and accessibility/generalization. (C) Joint multivariate q-values in the all-analysis and Direct-only scopes.

## Figure 5 | H2: conditional pathway decomposition
(A) Corrected isolation coefficients for reproductive assurance and for generalized accessibility and plain colour after conditioning on reproductive assurance. Primary selfing-adjusted accessibility is FDR-supported in northern high latitudes and the tropics; the tropical Direct-only estimate is nominally positive but not FDR-supported (q = 0.1196). (B) Selfing-adjusted raw colour-state responses. (C) Selected FDR-supported raw colour-conditioned architecture responses. H2 is a conditional decomposition, not a causal mediation test.

## Figure 6 | H3 and H4: ecological pressure and functional bridge
(A) In the corrected GloPL analysis, pollen limitation increases with geographic isolation (beta = 0.09191, SE = 0.03806, two-sided p = 0.01575). (B) Exact-species H4 associations for the literal Direct-only H2 reproductive-assurance score (beta = -0.29830, p = 0.00396) and generalized-accessibility score (beta = -0.29566, p = 0.02187). (C) Primary atomic-trait sensitivities. Negative H4 coefficients indicate association with lower current pollen limitation; they do not establish historical mediation.
